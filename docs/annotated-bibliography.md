## Blake scholarship

The following are research anchors, with access status stated rather than implied. Full-book reading is not claimed where only summaries, previews, or selected sections were inspected.

1\. **Edward Larrissy, 'Blake and Platonism', in Anna Baldwin and Sarah Hutton, eds., *Platonism and the English Imagination* (Cambridge UP, 1994), 186–198. DOI <https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511553806.018> .** Publisher summary read, chapter not accessed. It establishes a serious Blake/Platonism question: eternal realities and Neoplatonic terminology including generation/emanation, with important differences from simply adopting Plato. It identifies Harper/Raine as major proponents. Use as a map of the debate, not as evidence that every claim of those books has been independently verified.

2\. **Leopold Damrosch Jr., *Symbol and Truth in Blake's Myth* (Princeton UP, 1980), especially chapter 1, printed pp. 18–20.** Publisher/distributor preview, selected relevant pages read: <https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781400853731_A23704199/preview-9781400853731_A23704199.pdf> . Damrosch interprets Blake's universals through particularity and Berkeley, not detachable archetypes existing independently of particulars. His Jesus-centered account keeps universality while resisting abstraction. This is a precise corrective to 'Platonic essences', not a denial of all Platonist affinities. Corrected in 0.2.0: one-based PDF page 33 corresponds to printed p18; bibliography E-page numbers reflect an earlier edition.

3\. **David Fallon, 'Homelands: Blake, Albion, and the French Revolution', in A. D. Cousins and Geoffrey Payne, eds., *Home and Nation in British Literature from the English to the French Revolutions* (Cambridge UP, 2015), 202–218. DOI <https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781107587571.014> .** Open author manuscript, selected sections across introduction and conclusion read: <https://sure.sunderland.ac.uk/id/eprint/6061/1/Homelands.pdf> . Fallon balances a homeland open to other nations against both exclusionary nationalism and abstract cosmopolitanism. He surveys conflicting readings of national/imperial language and contrasts forms of destructive and regenerative growth. His account is directly useful for retaining situated belonging without equating it with possessive sovereignty. Manuscript's 12 PDF pages are not the published 202–218 pagination; do not invent a conversion.

4\. **Julia M. Wright, *Blake, Nationalism, and the Politics of Alienation* (Ohio UP, 2004).** Publisher description read, book not accessed: <https://www.ohioswallow.com/9780821415191/blake-nationalism-and-the-politics-of-alienation/> . Treats community, national narratives and alienation across the works rather than Blake as simply an isolated individualist. Fallon's chapter reports Wright's concern that late Jerusalem risks assimilating differences; that detailed claim is secondhand here (Fallon cites Wright p137), not independently read in Wright.

5\. **Steve Clark, 'Jerusalem as Imperial Prophecy', in Steve Clark and David Worrall, eds., *Blake, Nation and Empire* (Palgrave Macmillan, 2006), 167–185. DOI <https://doi.org/10.1057/9780230597068_10> .** Abstract/metadata located; full chapter not read. Book publisher <https://link.springer.com/book/10.1057/9780230597068> ; abstract <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304718074_Jerusalem_as_Imperial_Prophecy> . Important dissent from an automatically anti-imperial late Blake. Fallon quotes the critique of Protestant-nationalist imperial community at Clark p171; quote not reproduced here and original page not independently inspected.

6\. **Michael Ferber, 'Blake's Idea of Brotherhood', *PMLA* 93.3 (May 1978): 438–447.** Author bibliography verifies citation: <https://www.michaelkferber.com/language-literature/publications> ; publisher abstract/search excerpt inspected: <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/pmla/article/abs/blakes-idea-of-brotherhood/7472AB3B4E3400C5220EFD8F33E9B0E6> . Full article not accessed; repeated publisher retrieval returned 429. The abstract locates fraternity in New Testament possibilities, particularly brotherhood in Christ. Essential context for not translating brotherhood immediately into secular associational liberalism. No DOI supplied because not independently verified.

7\. **Saree Makdisi, *William Blake and the Impossible History of the 1790s* (University of Chicago Press, 2003; online platform records December 2002). DOI <https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226502618.001.0001> .** Conclusion abstract read, full book not read: <https://academic.oup.com/chicago-scholarship-online/book/16617/chapter-abstract/173700253> . Abstract frames Blake as sustaining alternatives to dominant radical tendencies through antinomian tradition. Use alongside, not instead of, the late-nationalist counterreadings. Publication-date differences should be recorded rather than silently harmonized.

8\. **Denise Gigante, 'Blake's Living Form', *Nineteenth-Century Literature* 63.4 (March 2009): 461–485. DOI <https://doi.org/10.1525/ncl.2009.63.4.461> .** Publisher abstract read, paywalled article not read: <https://online.ucpress.edu/ncl/article-abstract/63/4/461/67076/Blake-s-Living-Form> . Interprets Jerusalem through Romantic vitalism and epigenesis; organic form need not entail closure or mere reproduction. Relevant to generation/regeneration and resistance to treating living collectivity as static taxonomy. Do not treat this interpretation as uncontested.

9\. **Tara Lee, *William Blake and Romantic Biology* (Cambridge UP, 2026). DOI <https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009626422> .** Publisher metadata/description and introduction excerpt read in selected passages, not full book: <https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/william-blake-and-romantic-biology/C1E67BF63140420BCF1A0FE54B79FBD7> ; <https://assets.cambridge.org/97810096/26453/excerpt/9781009626453_excerpt.pdf> . Counters the predominance of self-organization readings with preformation, persistent individual identity and regeneration. This is a timely challenge to interpreting Blake as an unqualified theorist of novelty/emergence. The book is published March/April 2026, not 2025, according to current publisher metadata. Author's earlier Oxford thesis is openly available, but only selected search-exposed passages were inspected: <https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A353080ab-ceac-4970-bae4-1d83b4e32037> .

10\. **Shirley Dent and Jason Whittaker, 'Blake and Nationalism', in *Radical Blake: Influence and Afterlife from 1827* (Palgrave Macmillan, 2002), 67–95. DOI <https://doi.org/10.1057/9780230287402_4> .** Publisher book metadata and chapter abstract inspected; chapter not read. <https://link.springer.com/book/10.1057/9780230287402> . Useful reception-history check: later national uses of Blake are not identical to his original meanings.

11\. **S. Foster Damon, *A Blake Dictionary: The Ideas and Symbols of William Blake* (1965; revised editions thereafter), entry 'States'.** Located scan/search extract, entry inspected only; not full book. The entry's stages-of-error interpretation stresses mercy and non-identification of people with states. It is a useful traditional reading, not exhaustive of all contexts (e.g. free combinations and four states of repose). Do not redistribute the modern dictionary PDF; seek a legitimate library/publisher edition for extended use. No page number invented. Official publisher/edition route: <https://brandeisuniversitypress.com/title/a-blake-dictionary-the-ideas-and-symbols-of-william-blake/> (updated 2013 Dartmouth College Press edition, with Morris Eaves foreword and annotated bibliography; that full edition was not read here).
