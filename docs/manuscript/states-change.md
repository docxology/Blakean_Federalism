# States Change
## Blakean Federalism and composition without capture

Research article draft · version 0.1.0 · 23 October 2026 · private development

### Abstract

This project develops a political interpretation of a distinction that ordinary constitutional language obscures. A federation may contain STATES in a proposed Blakean sense of enduring imaginative possibilities while sustaining NATIONS as temporal generations, combinations and productions. The nations need not map one-to-one onto those states, and their federation need not become a single nation. The argument is a contemporary construction in dialogue with Blake. It does not recover a federal constitution hidden in his poems. Its positive proposal is composition without capture: collective histories and creations matter, while no national formation, interpretive vocabulary or jurisdiction exhausts the persons participating in it. The institutional test asks whether a classification can be translated into accountable authority without converting poetic permanence into an eternal mandate. Entomological Law motivates separating the entity, assigned role, asserting institution, warrant and consequence; issuer, competence, reach and enforcement remain distinct. Rahman's cultural-precision proposal supplies a counter-model in which broader identification increases conformity rather than freedom. Five hypothetical Xeno-California cases expose difficulties involving membership, unequal resources, affected nonmembers, treaty relationships and refusal. An executable descriptive prototype checks openly stipulated safeguards. It supplies neither an empirical freedom measure nor a proof of political legitimacy.

### 1 The inversion and what is at stake

The project begins with Daniel Ari Friedman's proposed inversion: the federation contains STATES in Blake's eternal sense, while NATIONS belong to historical production and generation. This retains a metaphysical ambition that would disappear if state were immediately translated into territorial government. A nation may articulate several imaginative possibilities; a possibility may recur in several nations. Their relation is many-to-many. A federation could sustain these different productions without demanding that one national narrative become the final image of the whole.

The political question arises when a collective formation claims the persons associated with it. A cultural body may offer language, memory, solidarity and self-government. Those goods do not automatically entitle its officers to control every member's employment, conscience or civic standing. A territorial institution may properly tax residents for shared infrastructure without claiming to embody their deepest identity. Non-capture therefore cannot mean the absence of binding decisions. It concerns the warrants, limits, consequences and contestability of those decisions.

Temporal nations are not disposable approximations of a superior eternal order. The proverb about eternity's love for temporal productions motivates attention to particular creations: languages, relationships, public practices and achievements whose value need not depend on lasting forever. Neither does the proverb sanctify every historical production. A nation can make something worth preserving and sustain relations that require transformation. Its creativity and its authority remain separately assessable.

This is a research hypothesis with three distinct burdens. Exegesis must establish what the selected Blake texts say. Reconstruction must defend the bridge from those texts to contemporary collective life. Institutional argument must justify actual powers to people who reject Blake's metaphysics. Success at the first burden does not discharge the other two.

### 2 States and persons in the primary passages

Milton 32:10–38, E131–132, is the central textual anchor. The plate is also cited as 32[35], added plate e; an edition-neutral paper must retain that ambiguity until copy collation resolves the relation. In this passage combinations of individuals can express free brotherhood or tyrannical aggregation. The distinction directs attention to how people are combined, not simply to whether combination occurs. A nation or federation does not become emancipatory merely by aggregating more participants.

The same passage separates states from the individual identities associated with them and distinguishes Imagination from a state. Both enduring identities and enduring states matter to the argument. It would invert the evidence to make individuals expendable temporal instances while assigning all permanence to collective forms. Nor can every use of state be treated as one immutable abstract essence: the passage names several different kinds of state and describes transformation. [Milton](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/milton.html).

Jerusalem 52, E200, and 73:44–45, E229, develop the distinction between persons and states through which they pass. The political extension proposed here is a guarantee that people may revise affiliations without losing basic standing. That extension is not a quotation or constitutional rule authored by Blake. The poems' theological, dramatic and visual organization must remain part of interpretation. A passage ledger is a reading instrument, not a replacement for reading the work. [Jerusalem](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html).

The eternity of Rahab or the availability of a state named Satan supplies a decisive limit on the reconstruction. Availability or intelligibility does not make a state desirable. A federation that contains states might preserve the ability to recognize, encounter and pass through a harmful condition without authorizing its domination. Giving every state a sovereign seat would confuse a vocabulary for understanding experience with a rule for allocating coercion.

The Platonic hypothesis remains open. An essence model makes historical nations instances of stable forms; a possibility model treats states as recurrent relational possibilities; a dramatic model retains their place in a visionary narrative; a regulative model uses them to orient inquiry without constitutionalizing metaphysics. The present draft favors investigating possibility and drama while requiring publicly contestable institutional reasons. It does not claim that the corpus has settled this interpretive choice. Larrissy, Damrosch, Fallon, Wright, Clark, Ferber, Makdisi, Gigante, Lee and reception history remain distinct critical interlocutors; their access and reading limits are preserved in the annotated bibliography.

### 3 Generation without biological nationalism

Nation as the born or generations is the proposed conceptual extension, not an invented Blake quotation. Biological ancestry, historical founding, artistic production, migration, initiation into a practice and the emergence of a new public are different senses of generation. The theory cannot slide between them to make descent a citizenship test. A person not born locally must retain an arguable route into political membership; a person born within a recognized nation must retain the ability to dissent from its institutions.

Emanation also resists a simple temporal production function. Jerusalem 54:1–5, E203, places emanation in Great Eternity. Its gendered, relational and theological dimensions cannot simply be replaced by a nation-making operator. The formal prototype therefore uses explicit affiliation and classification fields rather than claiming that nations computationally emanate from eternal states.

Particularity is another constraint. Jerusalem 38, 55 and 91, alongside the critique of consuming abstraction in plate 10, require the program to ask whether its categories obscure the lives they describe. Generalized national essences would undermine the intended distinction between persons and states. Conversely, refusing all categories would not eliminate the actual administrative powers through which services, sanctions and benefits are distributed. The task is to make classifications partial, warranted and challengeable.

Jerusalem 52 also contains polemic against Deists; its distinction between persons and states cannot simply be presented as an already secular guarantee of religious pluralism. Jerusalem 88 juxtaposes mutual interchange with a speaker\'s language of dominion. These contexts require a critical inheritance rather than an unqualified constitutional endorsement.\n\nThe countertexts are substantive. Jerusalem 43's election and gift-of-nations language, plate 92's amalgamation of historical peoples, and the closing vision complicate a straightforward pluralist reading. They invite criticism of Blake as well as criticism through Blake. National reciprocity in plate 27 must be read with these tensions; an unresolved E173/E174 edition discrepancy is retained rather than hidden. The program would fail exegetically if it selected only passages that already sound like contemporary liberal pluralism.

### 4 Two architectures and a constitutional remainder

Architecture A is a federation of enduring relational possibilities. Historical nations articulate, combine and transform those possibilities through particular practices. The federal whole is enacted in translation, encounter, common work, hospitality, disagreement and passage. It is not exclusively embodied by a dominant nation. This preserves the distinctive states/nations inversion.

At this level federality names a philosophical grammar. It does not yet specify shared rule and self-rule, public revenue, adjudication or representation. Containment means that possibilities remain intelligible and encounterable, not that every possible condition receives a political entitlement. Whether this grammar adds explanatory value beyond existing pluralist thought is a live question.

Architecture B distinguishes that imaginative constitution from a legal constitution. The latter must allocate real powers among territorial units, task-specific authorities, national-cultural bodies and shared institutions. A watershed and a language community may need different boundaries. A migrant may be subject to a jurisdiction without belonging to a recognized national association. A nation may cross jurisdictions without acquiring an unlimited police power over its members.

Common personhood, bodily security, conscience, expression, due process, material access and contestability are independently introduced normative premises. They do not follow automatically from a poem. Differentiated arrangements may protect freedom where uniform administration reproduces dispossession; unequal institutional forms are not automatically unequal standing. But every asymmetry needs a reason, an affected population and a means of challenge.

Three proposed mechanisms translate the grammar into institutional questions. A translation obligation requires accessible reasons across national narratives and an account of whom the deciding institution does not represent. A passage guarantee protects portable basic standing and practical affiliation change without eliminating legitimate continuing debts or duties. A non-exhaustion rule denies an organization's claim to speak every member's whole identity. None is presumed already endorsed as a final constitutional design.

### 5 Classification decisions and the institutional test

Entomological Law follows organisms through different evidentiary, regulatory, property, protective and moral roles. Its §§2 and 11–13 motivate inspection of the institutional translations that make the same entity visible in different ways. The human political extension borrows this discipline of description. It neither equates human persons with regulated insects nor makes dignity depend on a classifier's recognition. [Friedman, Entomological Law](https://danielarifriedman.com/papers/2026_EntomologicalLaw/full_text.md).

The classification-decision record in this project is explicitly new. It separates the person or collective, any claimed poetic-state interpretation, national affiliations, assigned legal role, temporal scope, classification warrant, asserting institution, competence warrant and consequence. An assertion of authority is not its validation. A correct classification does not automatically authorize the body asserting it to impose the proposed consequence.

The Figure 13 caption, printed p.28/PDF p.30, associated with §12.2, supplies a particularly concrete warning: issuing jurisdiction does not establish reach or enforcement. The prototype consequently records issuer and level, competence source, affected reach, enforcement actor and limits separately. Counting institutions, members or citations does not weight legal importance or assign constitutional authority.

The record also requires a contestation forum, voice, materially supported exit, revision, public reasons and representation limits. In resource decisions it asks who pays and who bears residual risk. Affected nonmembers must have standing. Treaty and refusal questions must receive their own treatment. Completing those fields does not prove that the described arrangements actually work; it makes missing warrants and exclusions inspectable.

The software can flag a particular prohibited move, such as a vision ranking determining rights or a poetic interpretation serving as the sole basis for coercion. Its output means consistency with stipulated project safeguards. It does not mean lawful in California, politically legitimate, empirically emancipatory or metaphysically true. A body could fill in every field dishonestly; a person could face an inaccessible appeal despite a formally complete record. Evidence about practice would still be needed.

### 6 Political interlocutors and the limit of novelty

Nicolaïdis's demoicracy is the closest concise comparator: plural peoples acting jointly without fusion into one demos. Her normative benchmark is not a description of every European practice, and an extension to overlapping intra-state nations requires new argument. Equal standing among peoples and equal influence among persons can conflict. The project must defend voting rules rather than promise that all forms of equality coincide. [Nicolaïdis 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcms.12006).

Kymlicka differentiates minority-rights claims; Shachar examines authority sharing and vulnerable insiders. Together they prevent recognition of a nation from becoming an undifferentiated entitlement to govern everyone classified within it. Exit cannot substitute for voice when departure costs housing, livelihood, language access or family relations. Their full institutional arguments require further reading beyond the bounded materials inspected here. [Kymlicka 1995](https://doi.org/10.1093/0198290918.001.0001); [Shachar 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511490330).

Bauböck supplies a direct challenge to national-cultural autonomy based on politically consequential registers. A nominal choice among permitted identities may still impose exclusive identification, exclude mixed or new affiliations, and entangle franchise or taxation. Open cultural association and public jurisdiction must remain different relations. This objection cannot be answered by renaming an exclusive register fluid. [Bauböck, December 2001](https://eif.univie.ac.at/downloads/workingpapers/IWE-Papers/WP22.pdf).

Ostrom's polycentric governance asks about rules and performance; Hooghe and Marks distinguish general-purpose and task-specific architectures. Neither turns a cultural nation into a functional jurisdiction. Overlap can enable adaptation or diffuse responsibility. Water, labor, housing and revenue may resist tidy decomposition. Their historical California illustrations are not current evaluations of California institutions. [Ostrom 2010](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.100.3.641); [Hooghe and Marks 2003](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055403000649).

Indigenous treaty federalism and refusal pose a different question: a people may reject incorporation on the federation's terms. Henderson, Simpson and the scope's UN declaration references require specific historical and constitutional inquiry. They must not be absorbed into a menu of optional affinity associations. This draft makes no updated legal determination from those sources and does not treat a synthetic consent field as evidence of actual consent.

The new contribution is therefore not the discovery of multinational federalism, plural sovereignty or overlapping governance. The candidate contribution is a critical grammar connecting nonidentity, national generation and imaginative passage to inspectable institutional transitions. If established approaches already supply every useful institutional mechanism, the project must acknowledge that result and narrow its novelty claim.

### 7 Xeno California as a discriminating case program

Xeno-California is a hypothetical stress test, not a blank description of California or a proposal imposed on actual peoples. Its cases must keep land, labor, infrastructure, power and affected nonmembers visible. Each case has two machine-readable alternatives; their labels identify stipulated design differences, not observed political outcomes.

Case A places several nations and unaffiliated residents in one territory. The bounded alternative grounds common services and ordinary political standing in personhood and residence while permitting language and cultural institutions. The capture alternative requires one exclusive national registration to obtain those services. The relevant evidence in a future study would include effective access, internal dissent and representation, not the number of recognized organizations.

Case B places one national community across jurisdictions. A limited cultural agreement can coordinate institutions without moving borders or licensing universal control over co-nationals. The capture alternative treats the association's national claim as authority over every affiliate everywhere. The issuer's level, the agreement's reach and the body able to enforce it must be separately specified. Territorial institutions retain duties to residents who reject the cultural organization.

Case C asks how a new generation becomes a public. Recognition need not require common descent or imitation of a constitutional list of old nations. But a new public's cultural claims do not automatically generate taxing, policing or secession powers. The rejected alternative infers coercive competence from an asserted eternal-state interpretation. A future mechanism must distinguish opportunities to organize, public recognition, representation and authorized powers.

Case D refuses a civic hierarchy of vision. Officials who classify a community as fourfold cannot infer that it deserves leadership over a supposedly single-vision community. Changing from three to four or six interpretive lenses in the prototype leaves admissibility unchanged. That software control establishes only the absence of a programmed lens-count entitlement; it does not validate any interpretation of fourfold vision.

Case E involves a wealthy national association seeking fiscal autonomy, an Indigenous community with land and water claims, migrant labor without association membership, and downstream affected residents. A meaningful alternative identifies duties, standing, public finance, ability to pay, essential access, residual risks and review. The capture alternative excludes nonmembers, leaves costs unspecified and absorbs treaty/refusal issues into ordinary association membership. The checks reject those omissions under proposed norms.

The resource case also needs comparison beyond the two diagnostic examples: ordinary territorial administration, asymmetric multinational arrangements and a hybrid task-specific authority may each have advantages and failures. Future empirical work must hold material conditions explicit and examine the actual procedures, rather than define the preferred hybrid as freer. A complete record is a prerequisite for criticism, not a substitute for it.

### 8 Broader cultural identification can intensify capture

Shagor Rahman's Myth of Objectivity profile in the 2025 Institute report, pp.124–130, proposes that broader cultural models can influence subordinate inference. Its developmental and AI claims remain speculative here. They belong to Rahman's contribution to a collective report, not to a theory newly attributed to Daniel Friedman. [Institute v3, Rahman profile](https://danielarifriedman.com/papers/2025_AII_v3/full_text.md).

The counter-model matters because a federation could widen contextual understanding while strengthening conformity to a dominant prior. Greater social scale is consequently not an automatic measure of freedom or fourfold vision. Coordination and dissent protection need different outcomes. A system can achieve agreement by raising the cost of challenge rather than by making collective reasons more revisable.

A future model should vary control of communication, precision-setting power, information asymmetry and the material consequences of dissent while holding the coordination problem explicit. Rights constraints must remain independently justified. The current prototype deliberately supplies no scalar freedom score and infers nothing from the number of affiliations. Its controls prevent a built-in monotonic conclusion; they do not empirically establish Rahman's proposed mechanism.

### 9 Coherence Rupture Regeneration and institutional revision

Alexander Sabine's canonical expansion is Coherence–Rupture–Regeneration. The April 2026 letter and later phase-gating paper are interlocutors for examining accumulated practice, interruption and reconstruction. Repair can describe an application, but it does not replace Regeneration in the name. Golden Compass §19.4 already makes a portfolio connection; this project does not claim to introduce it there. [April letter](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19388291); [phase-gating](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-33195-3_17).

The letter's reported square-root-of-two optimum is conditional on its objective and allocation constraints. It provides no universal constitutional ratio. The inspected open phase-gating manuscript limits its setting, describes a toy simulation, retains an untested regeneration kernel and qualifies an earlier equation-identity claim. Manuscript pagination, the published chapter and code execution remain different evidence objects. No simulations were independently replicated for this project.

The political research question is how institutions can correct accumulated exclusions while preserving persons' rights, relationships and material security. Coherence may stabilize oppression; rupture may install another monopoly; regeneration may restore the arrangement that needed transformation. Amendment, negotiation, redistribution and abolition must be assessed by independent criteria. Nothing in the framework makes violence necessary or legitimate.

### 10 Portfolio continuity and targeted correction

Fourfold Vision supplies a methodological bridge by separating historical, formal, interpretive, software and rights warrants. Mapping supplies corpus custody and provenance infrastructure. Doors supplies useful-boundary and person/state questions; Agent and Niche provides relational ecology; Golden Compass examines non-totalizing value and the recreation of domination. Architecture of False Gods already discusses plural transnational cooperation. The Meta-Pragmatic Framework helps keep executable evidence distinct from justified ends.

The version crosswalk preserves the actual extraction read and its printed DOI where those differ from current metadata. The Mapping mirror in the live portfolio remains the earlier extraction; this project's corpus software is pinned to Blake v0.2.0 and the new working-paper DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23601681. A later software publication does not silently make an older mirror its new text.

Two proposed errata require full version comparison before any predecessor is changed. Before Pragmatism's supplemental America material is an excerpt with local numbering, not the complete poem. Angels and Governors must remain distinct. Architecture's attempted correction attributing Boston's Angel's refusal to Orc must itself be checked against the primary poem. This repository records the correction program; it does not publish unreviewed errata to other projects.

The MathArt code lives in an Active_InferAnts directory rather than a separate repository. A public 404 for the documented Fourfold project URL does not establish whether it is private, renamed or removed. Dedicated project repositories, portfolio mirrors, concept DOIs and version DOIs remain distinct.

### 11 What the corpus analysis establishes

The imported object is a hash-bound private text-search projection of the recovered Blake capture. Its 2,067 source units comprise 540 reading documents, 731 XML units and 796 retained Erdman pages. Source roles remain 199 primary, 1,068 unverified and 800 editorial/mixed units. Copy repetition, page segmentation and raw XML prevent interpreting those counts as unique Blake works.

The concordance searches explicit whole-word variant sets covering states, persons, eternity, nations, generation, emanation, Albion, liberty, exchange, law, brotherhood, tyranny, contraries, negation, particulars, imagination, vision, Rahab and Satan. A nonsense query supplies a zero control. Every private hit retains original character offsets, local context, source role, plane, edition and text hash. The committed aggregate summary contains counts rather than provider bodies.

Counts locate passages to read. Co-occurrence does not show that Blake endorsed a political relation; absence in the pinned projection does not establish whole-oeuvre absence. The 28 unresolved discovered image identifiers remain unresolved, image contents have not all been read, and the search does not collate every illuminated copy. The new project uses the recovered corpus without rewriting its originals, indexes or backup.

### 12 Defeat conditions and the connected program

The textual hypothesis loses force if its strongest passages cannot sustain the state/person distinction or if omitted countertexts substantially reverse the interpretation. The philosophical reconstruction loses its novelty if it adds only terminology to an existing account. The institutional proposal fails if it protects a minority by silencing a minority within it, makes exit feasible only for the wealthy, or lets functional overlap evade responsibility for harms.

The positive sequence remains substantive article, then a typed compositional model once a concrete institutional operation is defined, then a critical text-image atlas with rival readings and rights-aware passage provenance. Category-theoretic or active-inference elaborations earn their place only by clarifying an actual problem. No Markov blanket supplies a border, and no optimization objective supplies justice.

For the article to become submission-ready, critical scholarship and political comparators require fuller authorized reading; central plates require copy-specific collation; portfolio errata require version comparison; and specialists in Blake and multinational or Indigenous constitutional politics need to criticize the argument. A result that the metaphysical grammar remains useful while the proposed institutions add little must remain acceptable. That willingness to lose a claim is part of composition without capture: the program must not claim possession of its own possible conclusions.
