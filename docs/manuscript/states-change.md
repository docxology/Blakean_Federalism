# States Change
## Blakean Federalism and composition without capture

Expanded research article draft · version 0.3.0 · 23 October 2026 · private development

### Abstract

This project develops a political interpretation of a distinction that ordinary constitutional language obscures. A federation may contain STATES in a proposed Blakean sense of enduring imaginative possibilities while sustaining NATIONS as temporal generations, combinations and productions. The nations need not map one-to-one onto those states, and their federation need not become a single nation. The argument is a contemporary construction in dialogue with Blake. It does not recover a federal constitution hidden in his poems. Its positive proposal is composition without capture: collective histories and creations matter, while no national formation, interpretive vocabulary or jurisdiction exhausts the persons participating in it. The institutional test asks whether a classification can be translated into accountable authority without converting poetic permanence into an eternal mandate. Entomological Law motivates separating the entity, assigned role, asserting institution, warrant and consequence; issuer, competence, reach and enforcement remain distinct. Rahman's cultural-precision proposal supplies a counter-model in which broader identification increases conformity rather than freedom. Five hypothetical Xeno-California cases expose difficulties involving membership, unequal resources, affected nonmembers, treaty relationships and refusal. An executable descriptive prototype checks openly stipulated safeguards. It supplies neither an empirical freedom measure nor a proof of political legitimacy.

### 1 The inversion and what is at stake

The project begins with Daniel Ari Friedman's proposed inversion: the federation contains STATES in Blake's eternal sense, while NATIONS belong to historical production and generation. This retains a metaphysical ambition that would disappear if state were immediately translated into territorial government. A nation may articulate several imaginative possibilities; a possibility may recur in several nations. Their relation is many-to-many. A federation could sustain these different productions without demanding that one national narrative become the final image of the whole.

The political question arises when a collective formation claims the persons associated with it. A cultural body may offer language, memory, solidarity and self-government. Those goods do not automatically entitle its officers to control every member's employment, conscience or civic standing. A territorial institution may properly tax residents for shared infrastructure without claiming to embody their deepest identity. Non-capture therefore cannot mean the absence of binding decisions. It concerns the warrants, limits, consequences and contestability of those decisions.

Temporal nations are not disposable approximations of a superior eternal order. The proverb about eternity's love for temporal productions motivates attention to particular creations: languages, relationships, public practices and achievements whose value need not depend on lasting forever. Neither does the proverb sanctify every historical production. A nation can make something worth preserving and sustain relations that require transformation. Its creativity and its authority remain separately assessable.

This is a research hypothesis with three distinct burdens. Exegesis must establish what the selected Blake texts say. Reconstruction must defend the bridge from those texts to contemporary collective life. Institutional argument must justify actual powers to people who reject Blake's metaphysics. Success at the first burden does not discharge the other two.

The formulation can be made more precise without identifying people with national essences. Let P denote persons, S poetic-state interpretations, N temporal national formations and J coercive jurisdictions. An affiliation relation connects P and N; an interpretive relation connects N and S; an authorization relation connects a specified institution in J to a specified act. These relations have different domains and warrants. Affiliation does not entail authorization, interpretation does not entail jurisdiction, and a person's standing does not depend on assignment to an S. This is a new descriptive construction, not an extracted Blakean algebra.

> Eternity is in love with the productions of time.

Q13. William Blake, The Marriage of Heaven and Hell, plate 7, E36. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/marriage_of_heaven_and_hell.html).

The proverb makes the temporal term affirmative: a friendship, language or public achievement need not become eternal to matter. Its placement among the Proverbs of Hell also resists turning it into a freestanding moral axiom. Production remains a question for judgment. A punitive register and a shared school are both historical acts; the adjective temporal does not decide between them. Composition without capture must preserve the capacity to discriminate among productions.

### 2 States and persons in the primary passages

Milton 32:10–38, E131–132, is the central textual anchor. The plate is also cited as 32[35], added plate e; an edition-neutral paper must retain that ambiguity until copy collation resolves the relation. In this passage combinations of individuals can express free brotherhood or tyrannical aggregation. The distinction directs attention to how people are combined, not simply to whether combination occurs. A nation or federation does not become emancipatory merely by aggregating more participants.

> We are not Individuals but States: Combinations of Individuals

Q01. William Blake, Milton 32:10, E131. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/milton.html).

> But the Divine Humanity & Mercy gave us a Human Form as multitudes
> Because we were combind in Freedom & holy Brotherhood Vox Populi
> While those combind by Satans Tyranny first in the blood of War
> And Sacrifice &, next, in Chains of imprisonment: are Shapeless Rocks
> Retaining only Satans Mathematic Holiness, Length: Bredth & Highth

Q02. William Blake, Milton 32:14-18, E131-132. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/milton.html).

The dramatic setting matters. Milton converses on the Couch of Death with the Seven Angels; Hillel, identified as Lucifer, replies immediately before this instruction (32:8-9). The plural speaker is not straightforwardly a present-day constitutional author. Yet the instruction's contrast is concrete: combination under freedom and combination through war, sacrifice and imprisonment produce different relations. Multitude is compatible with human form, whereas coerced aggregation can reduce people to measurable extension. This is stronger evidence for criticizing a mode of combination than for opposing all common institutions or all calculation.

The dimensions in line 18 do not themselves prove that measurement is tyrannical. The passage attacks their promotion into an exclusive holiness while imagination is called madness and blasphemy. A public body still needs budgets, population counts and water measurements. The reconstruction limits their claim to be sufficient descriptions of human existence. An institution that mistakes its register for a person repeats a pattern of reduction; the poem does not supply the correct administrative remedy.

> Distinguish therefore States from Individuals in those States.
> States Change: but Individual Identities never change nor cease:
> You cannot go to Eternal Death in that which can never Die.

Q03. William Blake, Milton 32:22-24, E132. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/milton.html).

The contrast is not eternal collective states against replaceable persons: individual identities also endure. An account that preserves every state while sacrificing inconvenient individuals would fail at its textual center. Identity's endurance does not mean that an official can certify a person's unchanging real nature. The distinction should restrain the classifier's authority, including that of a benevolent Blake interpreter.

> The Imagination is not a State: it is the Human Existence itself
> Affection or Love becomes a State, when divided from Imagination
> The Memory is a State always, & the Reason is a State
> Created to be Annihilated & a new Ratio Created
> Whatever can be Created can be Annihilated Forms cannot
> The Oak is cut down by the Ax, the Lamb falls by the Knife
> But their Forms Eternal Exist, For-ever. Amen Halle[l]ujah

Q04. William Blake, Milton 32:32-38, E132. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/milton.html).

The sequence distinguishes several kinds of persistence and change. Memory and reason are states; a ratio can be created and annihilated; forms cannot. Imagination is expressly excluded from the inventory of states. A federation containing S therefore cannot claim to contain or exhaust Imagination. Even its inventory of enduring possibilities must remain revisable as an interpretation. This supports retaining rival models of statehood rather than encoding a definitive ontology and calling its persistence Blakean truth.

The same passage separates states from the individual identities associated with them and distinguishes Imagination from a state. Both enduring identities and enduring states matter to the argument. It would invert the evidence to make individuals expendable temporal instances while assigning all permanence to collective forms. Nor can every use of state be treated as one immutable abstract essence: the passage names several different kinds of state and describes transformation. [Milton](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/milton.html).

Jerusalem 52, E200, and 73:44–45, E229, develop the distinction between persons and states through which they pass. The political extension proposed here is a guarantee that people may revise affiliations without losing basic standing. That extension is not a quotation or constitutional rule authored by Blake. The poems' theological, dramatic and visual organization must remain part of interpretation. A passage ledger is a reading instrument, not a replacement for reading the work. [Jerusalem](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html).

> The Spiritual States of
> the Soul are all Eternal
> Distinguish between the
> Man, & his present State

Q05. William Blake, Jerusalem 52, right epigraph, E200. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html).

> As the Pilgrim passes while the Country permanent remains
> So Men pass on: but States remain permanent for ever

Q08. William Blake, Jerusalem 73:44-45, E229. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html).

Q05 follows the right epigraph of plate 52, separately from its left epigraph and central heading; it is not numbered verse 1. Q08 is a simile of passage. A country remaining while a pilgrim moves through it does not establish that poetic states have territorial borders or governments. Passage can nevertheless organize a political question: what remains available to someone who leaves a collective classification? The proposed answer concerns portable standing, voice and practical means, rather than a metaphysical guarantee of effortless departure.

> Albion hath enterd the State Satan! Be permanent O State!
> And be thou for ever accursed! that Albion may arise again:
> And be thou created into a State! I go forth to Create
> States: to deliver Individuals evermore! Amen.

Q06. William Blake, Jerusalem 31:13-16, E177-178. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html).

A permanent accursed state and the deliverance of individuals appear together. This makes the inference from eternity to legitimate rule untenable. Permanence may mean that a recognizable possibility remains, so that persons can be delivered from its hold without denying its intelligibility. This does not make Satan good or grant it a constitutional office. Recognition, evaluation and authorization remain distinct even when their object shares one name.

The eternity of Rahab or the availability of a state named Satan supplies a decisive limit on the reconstruction. Availability or intelligibility does not make a state desirable. A federation that contains states might preserve the ability to recognize, encounter and pass through a harmful condition without authorizing its domination. Giving every state a sovereign seat would confuse a vocabulary for understanding experience with a rule for allocating coercion.

The Platonic hypothesis remains open. An essence model makes historical nations instances of stable forms; a possibility model treats states as recurrent relational possibilities; a dramatic model retains their place in a visionary narrative; a regulative model uses them to orient inquiry without constitutionalizing metaphysics. The present draft favors investigating possibility and drama while requiring publicly contestable institutional reasons. It does not claim that the corpus has settled this interpretive choice. Larrissy, Damrosch, Fallon, Wright, Clark, Ferber, Makdisi, Gigante, Lee and reception history remain distinct critical interlocutors; their access and reading limits are preserved in the annotated bibliography.

The scholarly dispute cannot be reduced to pure Platonism against pure nominalism. Larrissy's publisher summary identifies a question about the extent and character of Blake's Neoplatonic inheritance, not an inventory of national archetypes; the chapter remains unread. Damrosch's selected printed pp.15-21 locate universality in particular manifestations and in Jesus, resisting detachable forms. His Cusanus comparison is explicitly distinguished from demonstrated influence (p.20 n.25). In the acquired preview, printed p.18 is PDF p.33, correcting the earlier conversion. [Larrissy 1994](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511553806.018); [Damrosch 1980, selected preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781400853731_A23704199/preview-9781400853731_A23704199.pdf).

Lee's available introduction excerpt supplies another resistance to equating plasticity with emancipation: pre-existent form can protect identity against demands for indefinitely remaking life. The complete ten-page excerpt was read in the present research pass; the book and later chapters were not. A continually changing collective can still force people to adapt to an authorized future. A persistent claim about personhood may protect them against it. The institutional question concerns who invokes permanence or becoming, with what consequences. This is our political extension, not Lee's theory of federalism. [Lee 2026, excerpt pp.2-10](https://assets.cambridge.org/97810096/26453/excerpt/9781009626453_excerpt.pdf).

### 3 Generation without biological nationalism

Nation as the born or generations is the proposed conceptual extension, not an invented Blake quotation. Biological ancestry, historical founding, artistic production, migration, initiation into a practice and the emergence of a new public are different senses of generation. The theory cannot slide between them to make descent a citizenship test. A person not born locally must retain an arguable route into political membership; a person born within a recognized nation must retain the ability to dissent from its institutions.

> In Great Eternity, every particular Form gives forth or Emanates
> Its own peculiar Light, & the Form is the Divine Vision
> And the Light is his Garment This is Jerusalem in every Man
> A Tent & Tabernacle of Mutual Forgiveness Male & Female Clothings.
> And Jerusalem is called Liberty among the Children of Albion

Q07. William Blake, Jerusalem 54:1-5, E203. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html).

Emanation also resists a simple temporal production function. Jerusalem 54:1–5, E203, places emanation in Great Eternity. Its gendered, relational and theological dimensions cannot simply be replaced by a nation-making operator. The formal prototype therefore uses explicit affiliation and classification fields rather than claiming that nations computationally emanate from eternal states.

Particularity is another constraint. Jerusalem 38, 55 and 91, alongside the critique of consuming abstraction in plate 10, require the program to ask whether its categories obscure the lives they describe. Generalized national essences would undermine the intended distinction between persons and states. Conversely, refusing all categories would not eliminate the actual administrative powers through which services, sanctions and benefits are distributed. The task is to make classifications partial, warranted and challengeable.

Jerusalem 52 also contains polemic against Deists; its distinction between persons and states cannot simply be presented as an already secular guarantee of religious pluralism. Jerusalem 88 juxtaposes mutual interchange with a speaker's language of dominion. These contexts require a critical inheritance rather than an unqualified constitutional endorsement.

> I elected Albion for my glory; I gave to him the Nations,
> Of the whole Earth. he was the Angel of my Presence: and all
> The Sons of God were Albions Sons: and Jerusalem was my joy.

Q09. William Blake, Jerusalem 43:6-8, E191. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html).

> What do I see? The Briton Saxon Roman Norman amalgamating
> In my Furnaces into One Nation the English: & taking refuge
> In the Loins of Albion. The Canaanite united with the fugitive
> Hebrew, whom she divided into Twelve, & sold into Egypt
> Then scatterd the Egyptian & Hebrew to the four Winds!
> This sinful Nation Created in our Furnaces & Looms is Albion
> So Los spoke. Enitharmon answerd in great terror in Lambeths Vale

Q10. William Blake, Jerusalem 92:1-7, E252. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html).

The countertexts are substantive. Jerusalem 43's election and gift-of-nations language, plate 92's amalgamation of historical peoples, and the closing vision complicate a straightforward pluralist reading. They invite criticism of Blake as well as criticism through Blake. National reciprocity in plate 27 must be read with these tensions; an unresolved E173/E174 edition discrepancy is retained rather than hidden. The program would fail exegetically if it selected only passages that already sound like contemporary liberal pluralism.

> When in Eternity Man converses with Man they enter
> Into each others Bosom (which are Universes of delight)
> In mutual interchange. and first their Emanations meet
> How then can I ever again be united as Man with Man
> While thou my Emanation refusest my Fibres of dominion.

Q11. William Blake, Jerusalem 88:3-5, 12-13, E246. Two excerpts; lines 6-11 omitted. [ASU-hosted Erdman transcription](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html).

Mutual interchange is followed, in the same speech, by a complaint about refusal of dominion. The omitted lines 6-11 describe embracing, separation, fear and emanations at the gates of humanity. A political appropriation must ask whether its ideal of union makes another person's refusal appear as a defect to overcome. The difficult context cannot be resolved by drawing equal nodes in a diagram.

Fallon's complete submitted Homelands manuscript is the strongest newly read interlocutor on national mediation. PDF pp.1-4 examine situated vision and possessive Albion; pp.5-8 national manners and revolutionary disappointment; pp.9-12 destructive rooting and regenerative relations. The conclusion resists both possessive rootedness and abstract cosmopolitanism. It remains oriented toward Divine Humanity, not a defense of permanently plural constitutional peoples. Wright's detailed assimilation objection and Clark p.171 remain reported through Fallon. The current pass directly read only Clark's continuous opening pp.167-168: he challenges an assumed radical identity and situates the poem in a nationalist artistic economy, then develops provocative anti-Catholic and Tory-Ultra comparisons with a constitutional qualification. These are Clark's interpretations, not an uncontested historical verdict; the full chapter and its cited historians remain unread. [Fallon 2015, submitted manuscript](https://sure.sunderland.ac.uk/id/eprint/6061/1/Homelands.pdf); [Clark 2006, preview](https://page-one.springer.com/pdf/preview/10.1057/9780230597068_10).

A homeland can support memory and obligation without possessing its members. A mobile network can distribute extraction across borders. The character of relations matters more than a preference for roots or circulation. The proposal must also allow a people to challenge the language in which the federation recognizes it. Plurality under one compulsory final metaphysics would remain assimilationist.

Matthews's publisher excerpt, pp.1-10, challenges accounts of late Blake as necessarily rejecting erotic life and historicizes the vocabulary of sex and sexuality. It reports competing feminist readings rather than eliminating difficulty by contextualization. Only that available excerpt was read, not the whole book or the critics it cites. A woman, religious dissenter or unaffiliated resident must be able to contest collective authority without accepting the designers' account of divine imagination. Historicizing Blake and justifying present powers are different tasks. [Matthews 2011, excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805215/13579/excerpt/9780521513579_excerpt.pdf).

Plural membership also leaves a second route to assimilation. A federation might recognize many communities while requiring every one to accept its final metaphysical description of humanity. The non-exhaustion rule must therefore constrain the federation's interpretive authority: people need standing to challenge the terms of recognition and the classifier's competence without first endorsing the poetic-state vocabulary. Historical creativity cannot warrant treating a people's refusal as imaginative immaturity. This is our contemporary objection and proposed burden of justification; it is not a newly verified claim from Wright's unavailable chapter.

### 4 Two architectures and a constitutional remainder

Architecture A is a federation of enduring relational possibilities. Historical nations articulate, combine and transform those possibilities through particular practices. The federal whole is enacted in translation, encounter, common work, hospitality, disagreement and passage. It is not exclusively embodied by a dominant nation. This preserves the distinctive states/nations inversion.

At this level federality names a philosophical grammar. It does not yet specify shared rule and self-rule, public revenue, adjudication or representation. Containment means that possibilities remain intelligible and encounterable, not that every possible condition receives a political entitlement. Whether this grammar adds explanatory value beyond existing pluralist thought is a live question.

Architecture B distinguishes that imaginative constitution from a legal constitution. The latter must allocate real powers among territorial units, task-specific authorities, national-cultural bodies and shared institutions. A watershed and a language community may need different boundaries. A migrant may be subject to a jurisdiction without belonging to a recognized national association. A nation may cross jurisdictions without acquiring an unlimited police power over its members.

Common personhood, bodily security, conscience, expression, due process, material access and contestability are independently introduced normative premises. They do not follow automatically from a poem. Differentiated arrangements may protect freedom where uniform administration reproduces dispossession; unequal institutional forms are not automatically unequal standing. But every asymmetry needs a reason, an affected population and a means of challenge.

Three proposed mechanisms translate the grammar into institutional questions. A translation obligation requires accessible reasons across national narratives and an account of whom the deciding institution does not represent. A passage guarantee protects portable basic standing and practical affiliation change without eliminating legitimate continuing debts or duties. A non-exhaustion rule denies an organization's claim to speak every member's whole identity. None is presumed already endorsed as a final constitutional design.

### 5 Classification decisions and the institutional test

Entomological Law follows organisms through different evidentiary, regulatory, property, protective and moral roles. Its §§2 and 11–13 motivate inspection of the institutional translations that make the same entity visible in different ways. The human political extension borrows this discipline of description. It neither equates human persons with regulated insects nor makes dignity depend on a classifier's recognition. [Friedman, Entomological Law](https://danielarifriedman.com/papers/2026_EntomologicalLaw/full_text.md).

The classification-decision record in this project is explicitly new. It separates the person or collective, any claimed poetic-state interpretation, national affiliations, assigned legal role, temporal scope, classification warrant, asserting institution, competence warrant and consequence. An assertion of authority is not its validation. A correct classification does not automatically authorize the body asserting it to impose the proposed consequence.

The Figure 13 caption, printed p.28/PDF p.30, associated with §12.2, supplies a particularly concrete warning: issuing jurisdiction does not establish reach or enforcement. The prototype consequently records issuer and level, competence source, affected reach, enforcement actor and limits separately. Counting institutions, members or citations does not weight legal importance or assign constitutional authority.

The record also requires a contestation forum, voice, materially supported exit, revision, public reasons and representation limits. In resource decisions it asks who pays and who bears residual risk. Affected nonmembers must have standing. Treaty and refusal questions must receive their own treatment. Completing those fields does not prove that the described arrangements actually work; it makes missing warrants and exclusions inspectable.

The software can flag a particular prohibited move, such as a vision ranking determining rights or a poetic interpretation serving as the sole basis for coercion. Its output means consistency with stipulated project safeguards. It does not mean lawful in California, politically legitimate, empirically emancipatory or metaphysically true. A body could fill in every field dishonestly; a person could face an inaccessible appeal despite a formally complete record. Evidence about practice would still be needed.

Consider a recognition decision concerning a bilingual household. The entities are individual members, who retain standing; the role might be eligible users of a language service. The asserting body could be a municipal service board. Its competence requires a warrant to supply the service; its reach might be residents using specified facilities. An enforcement actor might recover a defined fee with reviewable limits. None of these statements authorizes the board to assign an eternal identity, govern the household abroad or suspend its ordinary franchise. This invented example exposes a transition the record must justify rather than hide within a national label.

An Ostrom-informed extension asks who occupies a position, who can enter or leave it, what information is available, which actions are permitted, how choices are aggregated, and how costs and benefits are assigned. Authority to revise the rules must also be located. The present model records selected descriptive safeguards; it is not a complete Institutional Analysis and Development implementation. Field completion supplies no behavioral evidence.

### 6 Political interlocutors and the limit of novelty

Nicolaïdis's demoicracy is the closest concise comparator: plural peoples acting jointly without fusion into one demos. Her normative benchmark is not a description of every European practice, and an extension to overlapping intra-state nations requires new argument. Equal standing among peoples and equal influence among persons can conflict. The project must defend voting rules rather than promise that all forms of equality coincide. [Nicolaïdis 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcms.12006).

The newly read Nicolaïdis author manuscript differentiates its normative benchmark from observed EU practices. Author-PDF pp.7-8 bound the method geographically and resist universal export; pp.8-10 make non-domination and recognition jointly demanding; pp.12-15 address voting, rights, mediation and accountability. Its online-first pagination differs from final pp.351-369. Joint rule is a problem to solve, not a consequence of keeping several names for peoples. Mutual recognition must not mean endorsing other authorities' control of dissenters. This project extends the question to overlapping nations; it cannot inherit a solution through European vocabulary. [Author manuscript](https://kalypsonicolaidis.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Demoicracy-jcms-March-2013-1.pdf).

Kymlicka differentiates minority-rights claims; Shachar examines authority sharing and vulnerable insiders. Together they prevent recognition of a nation from becoming an undifferentiated entitlement to govern everyone classified within it. Exit cannot substitute for voice when departure costs housing, livelihood, language access or family relations. Their full institutional arguments require further reading beyond the bounded materials inspected here. [Kymlicka 1995](https://doi.org/10.1093/0198290918.001.0001); [Shachar 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511490330).

Bauböck supplies a direct challenge to national-cultural autonomy based on politically consequential registers. A nominal choice among permitted identities may still impose exclusive identification, exclude mixed or new affiliations, and entangle franchise or taxation. Open cultural association and public jurisdiction must remain different relations. This objection cannot be answered by renaming an exclusive register fluid. [Bauböck, December 2001](https://eif.univie.ac.at/downloads/workingpapers/IWE-Papers/WP22.pdf).

The full December 2001 Bauböck working paper strengthens the objection beyond inconvenience. PDF pp.3-7 distinguish cultural liberty, recognition and political autonomy; pp.21-23 examine consequential affiliation, registers and exclusion of multiple identification; pp.24-26 resist separating cultural from general governmental interests. The conclusion generally favors territorial autonomy while retaining contextual exceptions. A many-to-many cultural map does not settle franchise. Giving a full public vote for every affiliation may duplicate influence; requiring one final affiliation restores exclusivity at the moment of decision. A response might distinguish equal general representation from several limited memberships, but must defend its scope, fiscal effects and provision for unrecognized people. Task-specific overlap does not by itself refute objections to nationality corporations exercising general public powers.

Ostrom's polycentric governance asks about rules and performance; Hooghe and Marks distinguish general-purpose and task-specific architectures. Neither turns a cultural nation into a functional jurisdiction. Overlap can enable adaptation or diffuse responsibility. Water, labor, housing and revenue may resist tidy decomposition. Their historical California illustrations are not current evaluations of California institutions. [Ostrom 2010](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.100.3.641); [Hooghe and Marks 2003](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055403000649).

Ostrom's complete main text, printed pp.641-665, separates resource properties, institutions and a framework of inquiry. Property is a bundle of rights (p.651); design principles are contextual regularities, not universal successful rules (pp.652-653). Her field and experimental evidence was read as reported evidence, not replicated. A Blakean interpretation cannot inherit empirical successes for a different institutional object. Performance requires observations of actual rules, people and material conditions. [Institution-hosted text](https://web.pdx.edu/~nwallace/EHP/OstromPolyGov.pdf).

Hooghe and Marks's complete main text, pp.233-241, separates general-purpose Type I from task-specific Type II arrangements. Nonintersecting Type I memberships do not imply nonoverlapping competence: p.237 n.6 expressly permits overlap of competencies. Type II bodies often sit within Type I settings and have variable franchises (p.238); their democratic effects remain questions (p.241). Stable citizenship can coexist with shared water competence, and a functional body can exclude affected residents from its electorate. These are hypothetical distinctions, not results about California today. [Author-hosted article](https://garymarks.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13018/2016/09/hooghe.marks_.unravelingcentralstate.apsr_.2003.pdf).

Indigenous treaty federalism and refusal pose a different question: a people may reject incorporation on the federation's terms. Henderson, Simpson and the scope's UN declaration references require specific historical and constitutional inquiry. They must not be absorbed into a menu of optional affinity associations. This draft makes no updated legal determination from those sources and does not treat a synthetic consent field as evidence of actual consent.

Henderson's 2019 article argues that treaty federalism is foundational rather than a concession in minority policy. This pass read printed pp.17-21 and 35-41, including each Treaty Nation's legitimate consent and proposed structural and fiscal changes. The middle sections and cited cases were not fully read or updated; his constitutional arguments are not adopted as present legal determinations. The 1994 article remains metadata and contents only. Simpson's publisher synopsis introduces refusal in a specific Kahnawà:ke setting; the book remains unread. Those limits prevent treating refusal as a generic portable technique while allowing it to challenge a federation that assumes it originates every relationship. [Henderson 2019](https://www.constitutionalstudies.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/02_Henderson-4.pdf); [Simpson 2014, publisher](https://www.dukeupress.edu/mohawk-interruptus).

The complete UN Declaration was read in its official March 2008 printing of the 2007 resolution. Articles 3-5 address self-determination and institutions; 18-19 participation and consultation toward consent; 25-28 land and redress; 32-35 projects and institutions; 36-37 cross-border relations and treaties. Articles 44 and 46 qualify selective reading through sex equality, human rights and territorial-integrity language. No universal veto or domestic enforceability finding is inferred. An ordinary association and a people asserting treaty relations must remain institutionally distinct. [UN General Assembly, A/RES/61/295](https://www.un.org/esa/socdev/unpfii/documents/DRIPS_en.pdf).

Pettit's directly read 2012 introduction excerpt, pp.1-10, makes non-capture more demanding than the absence of punishment. Asymmetric dependency can persist through control of housing, work or access; goodwill does not remove another's capacity to intervene (p.7). His distinction between protection from private power and protection from the protective state itself (pp.3-6) prevents treating any additional appeals authority as automatically emancipatory. Public interference need not dominate when appropriately controlled (p.10). This supports a quiet-domination counterexample without establishing any actual association's conduct. The 1997 book remains unread beyond its abstracts. [Pettit 2012, authorized excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/05112/excerpt/9781107005112_excerpt.pdf).

The complete 2005 working paper distinguishes collective electoral authorization from control retained by persons in their plurality (printed pp.1-4). Its account of reactive, representative and anticipated contestation (pp.8-14) requires publicly available reasons and effective correction, while warning that representatives can become lobbies and appeals depend on impartiality. Voice, practical exit and revision are different burdens: a forum must hear a challenge; a person needs means to leave; rules and powers must be revisable. His expensive-exit example concerns international arrangements (p.15); its application to personal affiliation is an analogy proposed here, not his empirical finding. The institutional historical examples and aggregation argument were read as reported arguments, not independently replicated or updated. [Pettit 2005, working paper pp.1-21](https://www.iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Petit-Two-Dimensional-Democracy-National-and-International-2005.pdf).

This changes what a future inquiry must observe. Low complaint or sanction counts could reflect trustworthy restraint, officials anticipating correction, residents fearing retaliation, or material dependence. A completed record cannot distinguish those possibilities. Ask who can obtain information, survive delay, secure an impartial hearing and make a remedy alter the original decision. Translation services and protectors themselves require scrutiny. Conversely, revising affiliation cannot automatically erase legitimate continuing duties or externalize common costs. These are proposed research questions, not effects demonstrated by the prototype.

The new contribution is therefore not the discovery of multinational federalism, plural sovereignty or overlapping governance. The candidate contribution is a critical grammar connecting nonidentity, national generation and imaginative passage to inspectable institutional transitions. If established approaches already supply every useful institutional mechanism, the project must acknowledge that result and narrow its novelty claim.

### 7 Xeno California as a discriminating case program

Xeno-California is a hypothetical stress test, not a blank description of California or a proposal imposed on actual peoples. Its cases must keep land, labor, infrastructure, power and affected nonmembers visible. Each case has two machine-readable alternatives; their labels identify stipulated design differences, not observed political outcomes.

Case A places several nations and unaffiliated residents in one territory. The bounded alternative grounds common services and ordinary political standing in personhood and residence while permitting language and cultural institutions. The capture alternative requires one exclusive national registration to obtain those services. The relevant evidence in a future study would include effective access, internal dissent and representation, not the number of recognized organizations.

Case B places one national community across jurisdictions. A limited cultural agreement can coordinate institutions without moving borders or licensing universal control over co-nationals. The capture alternative treats the association's national claim as authority over every affiliate everywhere. The issuer's level, the agreement's reach and the body able to enforce it must be separately specified. Territorial institutions retain duties to residents who reject the cultural organization.

Case C asks how a new generation becomes a public. Recognition need not require common descent or imitation of a constitutional list of old nations. But a new public's cultural claims do not automatically generate taxing, policing or secession powers. The rejected alternative infers coercive competence from an asserted eternal-state interpretation. A future mechanism must distinguish opportunities to organize, public recognition, representation and authorized powers.

Case D refuses a civic hierarchy of vision. Officials who classify a community as fourfold cannot infer that it deserves leadership over a supposedly single-vision community. Changing from three to four or six interpretive lenses in the prototype leaves admissibility unchanged. That software control establishes only the absence of a programmed lens-count entitlement; it does not validate any interpretation of fourfold vision.

Case E involves a wealthy national association seeking fiscal autonomy, an Indigenous community with land and water claims, migrant labor without association membership, and downstream affected residents. A meaningful alternative identifies duties, standing, public finance, ability to pay, essential access, residual risks and review. The capture alternative excludes nonmembers, leaves costs unspecified and absorbs treaty/refusal issues into ordinary association membership. The checks reject those omissions under proposed norms.

The resource case also needs comparison beyond the two diagnostic examples: ordinary territorial administration, asymmetric multinational arrangements and a hybrid task-specific authority may each have advantages and failures. Future empirical work must hold material conditions explicit and examine the actual procedures, rather than define the preferred hybrid as freer. A complete record is a prerequisite for criticism, not a substitute for it.

#### 7.1 A worked material conflict

Suppose, solely for comparison, a drought requires reduced withdrawals. A prosperous cultural association owns upstream storage; a Treaty Nation contests the source of the authority's jurisdiction; migrant workers need safe drinking water and wages; downstream residents bear ecological and health risks. These are stipulated parties, not claims about named California communities. Collective identities neither determine hydrology nor distribute costs. A proposal must identify existing obligations, evidence of scarcity, infrastructure control and the bodies authorized to negotiate or decide.

Alternative E1 gives recognized associations exclusive representation. It appears plural because several peoples have seats. It nevertheless excludes people outside the rolls, may misidentify a Treaty Nation as another voluntary club and can let wealth buy agenda control. Adding a ceremonial fourth seat or calling the board fourfold changes none of these defects. The failure concerns distribution of power, information and exposure, not symbolic diversity.

Alternative E2 creates one territorial rule with equal per-person voting. It protects a general franchise and avoids duplicating votes through affiliations. It can still subordinate a treaty relationship to an ordinary majority and conceal unequal starting entitlements to storage. Uniform procedure may govern an unequal baseline. The question concerns which equality is specified and whether the body imposing it has established its jurisdiction.

Alternative E3 gives a watershed body limited competence while requiring separately identified treaty negotiations, general resident standing, worker representation, public hydrological evidence and review. It distinguishes emergency drinking-water protection from longer-term allocation and compensation, identifies who can revise rules and assigns compliance costs. This is a candidate for investigation, not a declared correct constitution. Multiple forums may slow action, fragment accountability or let powerful actors move disputes to favorable venues. Emergency powers could become permanent and appeals remain unaffordable.

Comparison must keep physical scarcity and the resource baseline explicit while changing institutional rules. It should record access to water, costs, ability to secure a hearing, ability to survive delay and power to revise the arrangement. Essential provision cannot depend on buying national membership. Translation needs funding, reciprocal language access and limits protecting knowledge a people has reason not to disclose. Affected-person standing must confront rather than silently override self-determination. Counting affected individuals does not settle a treaty-specific jurisdictional dispute.

The record separates storage owner as property holder, supplier as service provider, watershed body as claimant of competence, and each enforcement actor. A valid scarcity measurement does not establish power to impose a levy; a valid levy does not establish that the board speaks every person's national identity. A successful cultural interpretation supplies neither warrant. The institutional test is useful when it locates these transitions clearly enough for opponents to dispute them.

The executable E-bounded and E-capture records are narrower than this comparison. They test stipulated fields and prohibited transitions. They do not simulate E1-E3, calculate equitable allocations, establish consent or forecast ecological outcomes. Treating the green alternative as optimal policy would reproduce the movement from classification to authority that the article criticizes.

### 8 Broader cultural identification can intensify capture

Shagor Rahman's Myth of Objectivity profile in the 2025 Institute report, pp.124–130, proposes that broader cultural models can influence subordinate inference. Its developmental and AI claims remain speculative here. They belong to Rahman's contribution to a collective report, not to a theory newly attributed to Daniel Friedman. [Institute v3, Rahman profile](https://danielarifriedman.com/papers/2025_AII_v3/full_text.md).

The counter-model matters because a federation could widen contextual understanding while strengthening conformity to a dominant prior. Greater social scale is consequently not an automatic measure of freedom or fourfold vision. Coordination and dissent protection need different outcomes. A system can achieve agreement by raising the cost of challenge rather than by making collective reasons more revisable.

A future model should vary control of communication, precision-setting power, information asymmetry and the material consequences of dissent while holding the coordination problem explicit. Rights constraints must remain independently justified. The current prototype deliberately supplies no scalar freedom score and infers nothing from the number of affiliations. Its controls prevent a built-in monotonic conclusion; they do not empirically establish Rahman's proposed mechanism.

### 9 Coherence Rupture Regeneration and institutional revision

Alexander Sabine's canonical expansion is Coherence–Rupture–Regeneration. The April 2026 letter and later phase-gating paper are interlocutors for examining accumulated practice, interruption and reconstruction. Repair can describe an application, but it does not replace Regeneration in the name. Golden Compass §19.4 already makes a portfolio connection; this project does not claim to introduce it there. [April letter](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19388291); [phase-gating](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-33195-3_17).

The letter's reported square-root-of-two optimum is conditional on its objective and allocation constraints. It provides no universal constitutional ratio. The inspected open phase-gating manuscript limits its setting, describes a toy simulation, retains an untested regeneration kernel and qualifies an earlier equation-identity claim. Manuscript pagination, the published chapter and code execution remain different evidence objects. No simulations were independently replicated for this project.

The political research question is how institutions can correct accumulated exclusions while preserving persons' rights, relationships and material security. Coherence may stabilize oppression; rupture may install another monopoly; regeneration may restore the arrangement that needed transformation. Amendment, negotiation, redistribution and abolition must be assessed by independent criteria. Nothing in the framework makes violence necessary or legitimate.

Institutional revision makes the question concrete: what survives interruption? Care relationships, entitlements, language services and unresolved claims may need continuity even when an abusive office is abolished. Preserving them need not restore its monopoly. Conversely, a ritual of rupture can replace one sovereign interpreter with another. A future comparison should identify the revision trigger, who declares it, protections during transition, reconstruction costs and how dissenters contest the regenerated arrangement. These are political research questions, not deductions from a phase-gating equation.

### 11 What the corpus analysis establishes

The imported object is a hash-bound private text-search projection of the recovered Blake capture. Its 2,067 source units comprise 540 reading documents, 731 XML units and 796 retained Erdman pages. Source roles remain 199 primary, 1,068 unverified and 800 editorial/mixed units. Copy repetition, page segmentation and raw XML prevent interpreting those counts as unique Blake works.

The concordance searches explicit whole-word variant sets covering states, persons, eternity, nations, generation, emanation, Albion, liberty, exchange, law, brotherhood, tyranny, contraries, negation, particulars, imagination, vision, Rahab and Satan. A nonsense query supplies a zero control. Every private hit retains original character offsets, local context, source role, plane, edition and text hash. The committed aggregate summary contains counts rather than provider bodies.

Counts locate passages to read. Co-occurrence does not show that Blake endorsed a political relation; absence in the pinned projection does not establish whole-oeuvre absence. The 28 unresolved discovered image identifiers remain unresolved, image contents have not all been read, and the search does not collate every illuminated copy. The new project uses the recovered corpus without rewriting its originals, indexes or backup.

The 0.3.0 workbench reconciles every stored occurrence and all 180 aggregate cells against the copied source index. For each query and plane/role category, coverage is matched source units divided by all units in that category, and is undefined when the category has no source units. For example, states has 264 occurrences in 60 of the 199 primary reading units: 30.2% unit coverage. This is neither length-normalized frequency nor a probability over independently sampled works. Matched-unit counts cannot be summed across query groups to obtain total corpus coverage. Structural empty categories display N/A; an absent term in a populated category displays zero.

The workbench additionally exposes all fifteen checks for each of ten alternatives in five synthetic case families, including rules whose conditions do not apply. Removing poetic-state and national-affiliation labels leaves these programmed assessments unchanged. That demonstrates the implementation's separation of interpretive labels and stipulated safeguards for these records; it does not test whether imaginative descriptions matter in political practice.

#### 11.2 When would computation add something?

A typed relational graph can separate persons, affiliations, interpretations and competencies. Multiplex relations can coexist without transferring authority across their layers. A model can expose a missing warrant or prohibited identification, but cannot create a political right by drawing an edge. A richer implementation should begin with a decision, rival account and independently justified norm before a compositional or active-inference formalism.

An ablation is especially important. Remove poetic-state descriptors while holding safeguards and material circumstances fixed. If the useful distinctions remain, institutional novelty belongs to political comparators, while Blake supplies critical or interpretive value. If imaginative description makes a difference, identify how: whose reasons become audible, what assumption changes and whether this is possible without compelled metaphysical assent. Current invariance under lens count is one small software control, not that entire empirical comparison.

### 12 Defeat conditions and the connected program

The textual hypothesis loses force if its strongest passages cannot sustain the state/person distinction or if omitted countertexts substantially reverse the interpretation. The philosophical reconstruction loses its novelty if it adds only terminology to an existing account. The institutional proposal fails if it protects a minority by silencing a minority within it, makes exit feasible only for the wealthy, or lets functional overlap evade responsibility for harms.

The positive sequence remains substantive article, then a typed compositional model once a concrete institutional operation is defined, then a critical text-image atlas with rival readings and rights-aware passage provenance. Category-theoretic or active-inference elaborations earn their place only by clarifying an actual problem. No Markov blanket supplies a border, and no optimization objective supplies justice.

For the article to become submission-ready, the expanded reading still needs wider authorized scholarship and specialist challenge; central plates require copy-specific collation; portfolio errata require version comparison; and specialists in Blake and multinational or Indigenous constitutional politics need to criticize the argument. A result that the metaphysical grammar remains useful while the proposed institutions add little must remain acceptable. That willingness to lose a claim is part of composition without capture: the program must not claim possession of its own possible conclusions.
