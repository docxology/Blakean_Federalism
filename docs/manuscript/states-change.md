# Abstract {.unnumbered}

A federation can sustain several nations without becoming one nation. This article develops that claim through Blake's distinction between states and persons: STATES are enduring imaginative possibilities, while NATIONS are historical formations that articulate and recombine them. Its governing principle is composition without capture. Collective histories and creations matter, but no nation, interpretive vocabulary or jurisdiction exhausts its participants. Close readings of Milton and Jerusalem establish the state/person distinction and confront its nationalist, theological and gendered tensions. Comparison with demoicracy, non-domination, cultural autonomy and treaty federalism identifies what institutions must decide: who has standing, which body may act, who bears the cost and how a decision can be challenged. A classification-decision record separates the entity, assigned role and consequence from the issuer, competence, reach and enforcement of authority. Five hypothetical Xeno-California cases test these distinctions. An executable prototype evaluates fifteen safeguards across ten case alternatives and searches finite intervention families for minimum repairs. Corpus statistics compare token-normalized rates, source-family weighting, duplicate sensitivity and lexical overlap across the primary reading extracts.

# Composition without capture: poetic states, temporal nations and public power {#sec:s1}

The argument begins with an inversion: the federation contains STATES in Blake's eternal sense, while NATIONS belong to historical production and generation. This retains a metaphysical ambition that would disappear if state were immediately translated into territorial government. A nation may articulate several imaginative possibilities; a possibility may recur in several nations. Their relation is many-to-many. A federation could sustain these different productions without demanding that one national narrative become the final image of the whole.

The political question arises when a collective formation claims the persons associated with it. A cultural body may offer language, memory, solidarity and self-government. Those goods do not automatically entitle its officers to control every member's employment, conscience or civic standing. A territorial institution may properly tax residents for shared infrastructure without claiming to embody their deepest identity. Non-capture therefore cannot mean the absence of binding decisions. It concerns the warrants, limits, consequences and contestability of those decisions.

Temporal nations are not disposable approximations of a superior eternal order. The proverb about eternity's love for temporal productions motivates attention to particular creations: languages, relationships, public practices and achievements whose value need not depend on lasting forever. Neither does the proverb sanctify every historical production. A nation can make something worth preserving and sustain relations that require transformation. Its creativity and its authority remain separately assessable.

The argument proceeds through three tasks. Close reading establishes what the selected passages say in their dramatic and material contexts. Reconstruction develops a political account of persons, states and national generation. Institutional comparison asks how shared powers can be justified to people who hold different metaphysical commitments. Each task supplies a different kind of reason.

The formulation can be made more precise without identifying people with national essences. Let P denote persons, S poetic-state interpretations, N temporal national formations and J institutions claiming jurisdiction. Issuing jurisdictions remain separately identified attributes of those institutions. An affiliation relation connects P and N; an interpretive relation connects N and S; an authorization relation connects a specified institution in J to a specified act. These relations have different domains and warrants. Affiliation does not entail authorization, interpretation does not entail jurisdiction, and a person's standing does not depend on assignment to an S. The typed model records these distinctions explicitly.

> Eternity is in love with the productions of time.

The proverb makes the temporal term affirmative: a friendship, language or public achievement need not become eternal to matter. Its placement among the Proverbs of Hell also resists turning it into a freestanding moral axiom. Production remains a question for judgment. A punitive register and a shared school are both historical acts; the adjective temporal does not decide between them. Composition without capture must preserve the capacity to discriminate among productions.

# Changing states and enduring persons: reading Milton and Jerusalem together {#sec:s2}

## Free combination and tyrannical aggregation

Milton 32:10–38, E131–132, is the central textual anchor. The transcription calls it plate 32; the selected copy-D leaf is numbered 35, also cited as 32[35], added plate e. In this passage combinations of individuals can express free brotherhood or tyrannical aggregation. The distinction directs attention to how people are combined, not simply to whether combination occurs. A nation or federation does not become emancipatory merely by aggregating more participants.

\Needspace{4\baselineskip}

> We are not Individuals but States: Combinations of Individuals

Q01. William Blake, Milton 32:10, E131. [@blakeMilton].

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}

> But the Divine Humanity & Mercy gave us a Human Form as multitudes\
> Because we were combind in Freedom & holy Brotherhood Vox Populi\
> While those combind by Satans Tyranny first in the blood of War\
> And Sacrifice &, next, in Chains of imprisonment: are Shapeless Rocks\
> Retaining only Satans Mathematic Holiness, Length: Bredth & Highth

Q02. William Blake, Milton 32:14-18, E131-132. [@blakeMilton].

The dramatic setting matters. Milton converses on the Couch of Death with the Seven Angels; Hillel, identified as Lucifer, replies immediately before this instruction (32:8-9). The plural speaker is not straightforwardly a present-day constitutional author. Yet the instruction's contrast is concrete: combination under freedom and combination through war, sacrifice and imprisonment produce different relations. Multitude is compatible with human form, whereas coerced aggregation can reduce people to measurable extension. This is stronger evidence for criticizing a mode of combination than for opposing all common institutions or all calculation.

The dimensions in line 18 do not themselves prove that measurement is tyrannical. The passage attacks their promotion into an exclusive holiness while imagination is called madness and blasphemy. A public body still needs budgets, population counts and water measurements. The reconstruction limits their claim to be sufficient descriptions of human existence. An institution that mistakes its register for a person repeats a pattern of reduction; the poem does not supply the correct administrative remedy.

## Identities endure while states change

> Distinguish therefore States from Individuals in those States.\
> States Change: but Individual Identities never change nor cease:\
> You cannot go to Eternal Death in that which can never Die.

Q03. William Blake, Milton 32:22-24, E132. [@blakeMilton].

The contrast is not eternal collective states against replaceable persons: individual identities also endure. An account that preserves every state while sacrificing inconvenient individuals would fail at its textual center. Identity's endurance does not mean that an official can certify a person's unchanging real nature. The distinction should restrain the classifier's authority, including that of a benevolent Blake interpreter.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}

> The Imagination is not a State: it is the Human Existence itself\
> Affection or Love becomes a State, when divided from Imagination\
> The Memory is a State always, & the Reason is a State\
> Created to be Annihilated & a new Ratio Created\
> Whatever can be Created can be Annihilated Forms cannot\
> The Oak is cut down by the Ax, the Lamb falls by the Knife\
> But their Forms Eternal Exist, For-ever. Amen Halle[l]ujah

Q04. William Blake, Milton 32:32-38, E132. [@blakeMilton].

Blake assigns endurance to individual identities and Forms while describing states that change, are created, or are annihilated. The proposed inversion needs a distinction within its own vocabulary. Here, an enduring state names a recurrent imaginative possibility; a nation names a historical formation through which such possibilities become articulate. Recurrence does not preserve the authority of a particular enactment. A national institution can lose its mandate while its history remains intelligible and its members retain their standing. Imagination exceeds this repertoire of interpretations, just as persons exceed an institution's account of them [@blakeMilton, 32:22–38, E132; @blakeJerusalem, plate 52, E200].


## Passage through permanent states

Jerusalem 52, E200, and 73:44–45, E229, develop the distinction between persons and states through which they pass. The political extension proposed here is a guarantee that people may revise affiliations without losing basic standing. The reconstruction connects this poetic distinction to portable standing, voice and practical support for changing affiliations. [@blakeJerusalem].

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}

> The Spiritual States of\
> the Soul are all Eternal\
> Distinguish between the\
> Man, & his present State

Q05. William Blake, Jerusalem 52, right epigraph, E200. [@blakeJerusalem].

> As the Pilgrim passes while the Country permanent remains\
> So Men pass on: but States remain permanent for ever

Q08. William Blake, Jerusalem 73:44-45, E229. [@blakeJerusalem].

Q05 follows the right epigraph of plate 52, separately from its left epigraph and central heading; it is not numbered verse 1. Q08 is a simile of passage. A country remaining while a pilgrim moves through it does not establish that poetic states have territorial borders or governments. Passage can nevertheless organize a political question: what remains available to someone who leaves a collective classification? The proposed answer concerns portable standing, voice and practical means, rather than a metaphysical guarantee of effortless departure.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}

> Albion hath enterd the State Satan! Be permanent O State!\
> And be thou for ever accursed! that Albion may arise again:\
> And be thou created into a State! I go forth to Create\
> States: to deliver Individuals evermore! Amen.

Q06. William Blake, Jerusalem 31:13-16, E177-178. [@blakeJerusalem].

A permanent accursed state and the deliverance of individuals appear together. This makes the inference from eternity to legitimate rule untenable. Permanence may mean that a recognizable possibility remains, so that persons can be delivered from its hold without denying its intelligibility. This does not make Satan good or grant it a constitutional office. Recognition, evaluation and authorization remain distinct even when their object shares one name.

## Provision sustains passage before the costs of departure fall due


> The Sons of Los clothe them & feed, & provide houses & gardens

Q23. William Blake, Jerusalem 73:49, E229. [@blakeJerusalem].

The pilgrim’s passage does not end with permanence. Spectres gather at Los’s porches amid the feuds of Albion’s cities, and his sons supply their essentials. The recipients are troubling spectres, rather than an idealized company already reconciled to its hosts. Clothing, food, houses and gardens belong beside a labor of creation and destruction that includes weapons, kings and the hammer [@blakeJerusalem, 73:1–54, E228–229]. Passage requires practical support within conflict.

> The stones are pity, and the bricks, well wrought affections:\
> Enameld with love & kindness, & the tiles engraven gold\
> Labour of merciful hands: the beams & rafters are forgiveness:\
> The mortar & cement of the work, tears of honesty: the nails,\
> And the screws & iron braces, are well wrought blandishments,\
> And well contrived words, firm fixing, never forgotten,\
> Always comforting the remembrance: the floors, humility,

Q19. William Blake, Jerusalem 12:30–36, E155. Selected excerpt. [@blakeJerusalem].

The building description names the materials of a durable relation: pity, affection, merciful labor, forgiveness and comforting remembrance. Jerusalem nevertheless wanders outside Los's gate at 12:43–44. An elaborate structure of assistance can coexist with practical exclusion. The institutional question is when and how the person can use what has been built. A hearing after eviction leaves the lost interval of security unresolved; a translation service after a missed deadline leaves the exclusion operative. Supported passage therefore includes interim provision, timely notice and assistance before a foreseeable loss.

## Distance changes how a person, state and nation appear

A Vision of the Last Judgment unsettles a strict lexical division between eternal states and temporal nations. Blake first distinguishes Moses and Abraham as persons from the states signified by their names. He then describes those states appearing as one man at a distance and as multitudes of nations when approached. Nations participate here in visionary representation, while the apparent individual changes with the observer’s distance. The political reconstruction therefore distinguishes historical national formations from persons without treating those categories as Blake’s exhaustive taxonomy [@blakeLastJudgment, notebook p. 76, second passage, E556–557].

Mitchell asks whether Blake’s pictures communicate the exemption of individuals from damnation that an interpreter derives from the writings. His seminar paper ends with an interpretive hypothesis, while its diagrams keep the geometrical reading open. The disagreement supplies a discipline for this article’s visual models: their separate nodes display proposed distinctions; their arrangement does not establish a reconciled social whole. A diagram of affiliations must remain answerable to the people represented and to competing accounts of the relation [@mitchell1975Judgment, secs. II–IV, PDF pp. 8–11].

## Endurance grants no mandate to rule

The eternity of Rahab or the availability of a state named Satan supplies a decisive limit on the reconstruction. Availability or intelligibility does not make a state desirable. A federation that contains states might preserve the ability to recognize, encounter and pass through a harmful condition without authorizing its domination. Giving every state a sovereign seat would confuse a vocabulary for understanding experience with a rule for allocating coercion.

The Platonic hypothesis remains open. An essence model makes historical nations instances of stable forms; a possibility model treats states as recurrent relational possibilities; a dramatic model retains their place in a visionary narrative; a regulative model uses them to orient inquiry without constitutionalizing metaphysics. The present draft favors investigating possibility and drama while requiring publicly contestable institutional reasons. The readings below compare what each account explains about permanence and particularity.

The dispute turns on the kind of permanence at stake. The publisher summary of Larrissy’s chapter frames Blake's Neoplatonic inheritance as a question of extent and character [@larrissy1994]. Damrosch locates universality in particular manifestations and in Jesus, resisting detachable forms; his Cusanus comparison is distinguished from a claim of demonstrated influence [@damrosch1980, pp. 15–21 and p. 20 n. 25]. The reconstruction accordingly treats recurrence as a relation among particular enactments rather than a national essence available for official certification.

Lee's introduction complicates the equation of plasticity with emancipation: pre-existent form can protect identity against demands for indefinitely remaking life [@lee2026, pp. 1–10]. A continually changing collective can force people to adapt to an authorized future, while a persistent claim about personhood may protect them against it. The political question concerns who invokes permanence or becoming, and with what consequences.

# National generation, reciprocal exchange and the struggle over agency {#sec:s3}

## National generation is embodied work, inheritance and the making of new publics

Nation as the born or generations is the proposed conceptual extension, not an invented Blake quotation. Biological ancestry, historical founding, artistic production, migration, initiation into a practice and the emergence of a new public are different senses of generation. The theory cannot slide between them to make descent a citizenship test. A person not born locally must retain an arguable route into political membership; a person born within a recognized nation must retain the ability to dissent from its institutions.



> Inclosing all the Children of Los time after time.\
> Their Giant forms condensing into Nations & Peoples & Tongues

Q24. William Blake, Jerusalem 53:7–8, E202. [@blakeJerusalem].

Los repeatedly encloses his children before their forms condense into nations, peoples and tongues. The account moves through organs, furnaces and a spiritual London continually built and decaying [@blakeJerusalem, 53:1–29, E202–203]. National generation is organized work with pain and confinement as well as creative possibility. A language persists through speakers, teachers, carers, publishers and places to meet. Recognition can support that work while concentrating every grant in one certified body. Supporting a practice and certifying its exclusive representative are different decisions. Younger participants can challenge a school or cultural budget without first proving that they constitute another nation; newcomers can change a tradition through their contribution to its continuation.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}

> In Great Eternity, every particular Form gives forth or Emanates\
> Its own peculiar Light, & the Form is the Divine Vision\
> And the Light is his Garment This is Jerusalem in every Man\
> A Tent & Tabernacle of Mutual Forgiveness Male & Female Clothings.\
> And Jerusalem is called Liberty among the Children of Albion

Q07. William Blake, Jerusalem 54:1-5, E203. [@blakeJerusalem].

Emanation also resists a simple temporal production function. Jerusalem 54:1–5, E203, places emanation in Great Eternity. Its gendered, relational and theological dimensions cannot simply be replaced by a nation-making operator. The formal prototype therefore uses explicit affiliation and classification fields rather than claiming that nations computationally emanate from eternal states.

Particularity is another constraint. Jerusalem 38, 55 and 91, alongside the critique of consuming abstraction in plate 10, require the program to ask whether its categories obscure the lives they describe. Generalized national essences would undermine the intended distinction between persons and states. Conversely, refusing all categories would not eliminate the actual administrative powers through which services, sanctions and benefits are distributed. The task is to make classifications partial, warranted and challengeable.

## Particularity means relevant discrimination, not exhaustive collection

Heppner complicates an opposition between particularity and visual simplification. His reading of *The Woman Taken in Adultery* finds sparse setting and restrained expression while locating significance in posture, narrative and words beyond the image. In the dispute with Reynolds, the issue is discrimination of character rather than accumulation of incidental detail [@heppner1983Adultery, pp. 55–56, 59]. This changes the standard for a classification record. A longer identity file can overlook who controls a claimant's wages; a limited record can protect a claim whose grounds require no complete personal narrative. The record needs the difference relevant to the decision and a way for the affected person to name what it misses.

## National mediation and the danger of incorporation

Jerusalem 52 also contains polemic against Deists; its distinction between persons and states cannot simply be presented as an already secular guarantee of religious pluralism. Jerusalem 88 juxtaposes mutual interchange with a speaker's language of dominion. These contexts require a critical inheritance rather than an unqualified constitutional endorsement.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}

> I elected Albion for my glory; I gave to him the Nations,\
> Of the whole Earth. he was the Angel of my Presence: and all\
> The Sons of God were Albions Sons: and Jerusalem was my joy.

Q09. William Blake, Jerusalem 43:6-8, E191. [@blakeJerusalem].

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}

> What do I see? The Briton Saxon Roman Norman amalgamating\
> In my Furnaces into One Nation the English: & taking refuge\
> In the Loins of Albion. The Canaanite united with the fugitive\
> Hebrew, whom she divided into Twelve, & sold into Egypt\
> Then scatterd the Egyptian & Hebrew to the four Winds!\
> This sinful Nation Created in our Furnaces & Looms is Albion\
> So Los spoke. Enitharmon answerd in great terror in Lambeths Vale

Q10. William Blake, Jerusalem 92:1-7, E252. [@blakeJerusalem].

The countertexts are substantive. Jerusalem 43's election and gift-of-nations language, plate 92's amalgamation of historical peoples, and the closing vision complicate a straightforward pluralist reading. They invite criticism of Blake as well as criticism through Blake. National reciprocity in plate 27 must be read with these tensions; for Jerusalem 27:85–88 this article follows the ASU transcription's E173 locator, while Fallon's submitted manuscript gives E174 [@blakeJerusalem; @fallon2015, submitted ms. p. 4]. The program would fail exegetically if it selected only passages that already sound like contemporary liberal pluralism.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}

> When in Eternity Man converses with Man they enter\
> Into each others Bosom (which are Universes of delight)\
> In mutual interchange. and first their Emanations meet\
> How then can I ever again be united as Man with Man\
> While thou my Emanation refusest my Fibres of dominion.

Q11. William Blake, Jerusalem 88:3-5, 12-13, E246. Two excerpts; lines 6-11 omitted. [@blakeJerusalem].

Mutual interchange is followed, in the same speech, by a complaint about refusal of dominion. The omitted lines 6-11 describe embracing, separation, fear and emanations at the gates of humanity. A political appropriation must ask whether its ideal of union makes another person's refusal appear as a defect to overcome. The difficult context cannot be resolved by drawing equal nodes in a diagram.

National mediation and national possession are different relations. Fallon interprets the homeland as a setting for imaginative cultivation that opens toward other nations, while distinguishing regenerative growth from the Polypus's coercive aggregation. His explicit disagreement with Wright concerns whether this distinction holds: loving union may transform the character of relations or reproduce incorporation in a redemptive idiom [@fallon2015, submitted ms. pp. 10–12 and p. 12 n. 40]. Clark raises a different historical challenge by situating Jerusalem in a nationalist artistic economy [@clark2006, pp. 167–168]. Recognition of more national names leaves open the question of who can challenge the common account of what recognition means.

The temporal character of nations sharpens this requirement. Makdisi contrasts histories that absorb other peoples into one sequence of progress with Blake's heterogeneous prophetic present [@makdisi1998, pp. 1–3]. Nations can be historical productions without being ranked as earlier and later versions of the same authorized future. A community's refusal of the federation's self-description need not mark a failure to mature. The constitutional question is whether it can dispute that description while retaining voice, material security and an arguable claim to powers of its own.

Matthews historicizes the vocabulary of sex and sexuality and challenges the assumption that late Blake simply rejects erotic life [@matthews2011, pp. 1–10]. Historical context sharpens, rather than settles, the political question. A woman, dissenter or unaffiliated resident must be able to contest collective authority without accepting its account of divine imagination.

Plural membership also leaves a second route to assimilation. A federation might recognize many communities while requiring every one to accept its final metaphysical description of humanity. The non-exhaustion rule must therefore constrain the federation's interpretive authority: people need standing to challenge the terms of recognition and the classifier's competence without first endorsing the poetic-state vocabulary. Historical creativity cannot warrant treating a people's refusal as imaginative immaturity. This places the burden of justification on the federation as well as its constituent bodies.


## Exchange makes national particularity relational

The closing stanza of *To the Jews* names a mutual action rather than the merger of lands into one national subject:


\Needspace{9\baselineskip}

> In my Exchanges every Land\
> Shall walk, & mine in every Land,\
> Mutual shall build Jerusalem:\
> Both heart in heart & hand in hand.

Q14. William Blake, Jerusalem 27:85–88, E173. [@blakeJerusalem].

The grammar joins distinct agents through exchange: each land walks in the other's exchanges, and both build. The reciprocal terms support a relation among national productions rather than a single owner of the universal. Yet the prose address framing this stanza makes religious claims about Jews and the Bible. Reciprocity in the verse cannot erase the conversionary and supersessionist pressure a reader may find in that address. A federation that adopts the exchange image has to defend participation without demanding a common redemptive history. Fallon’s account of a situated national opening and the competing incorporation reading therefore confront the same material page [@fallon2015, submitted ms. pp. 4, 10–12; @blakeJerusalem, plate 27, E171–174].


![Jerusalem 27, To the Jews, copy I. The exchange stanza sits in the right verse column below the prose address. Its reciprocity operates within an account of Christian origins, so the whole sheet lets mutual enrichment and religious incorporation be read together. LOC, Rosenwald Collection, item 48038103; posthumous impression, approximately 1832?.](../figures/plate-J27.jpg){#fig:plate-J27 height=3.8in}

The exchange image changes the institutional question from how many cultures are recognized to who controls the exchange. A language service can widen participation while a compulsory identity register narrows it. The proposed decision record therefore distinguishes the provider, recipient, assigned role and public consequence. The parties need power to negotiate terms, refuse an offered classification and contest the institution that certifies their common good.

Kaplan connects this address to the poem's criticism of patriarchal possession: a family elevated over the rest of the world can become an instrument of destruction [@kaplan1996Patriarchy, pp. 68–69]. This gives the national argument an internal rather than merely external test. A nation can participate in exchange while retaining an exclusive right to decide which members embody its authentic history. Conversely, a federation can condemn national self-enclosure while absorbing every people's account of its origins into its own story. The proposed reconstruction must refuse both privileges. A people should be able to dispute a common narrative without thereby surrendering its voice in shared affairs; its officers should face challenges from those whom its own narrative misrecognizes.

Heppner's reading of the early drawing catalogued as Butlin 92 makes the woman-city an agent rather than simply a destination. He proposes reading a supposed number as Gog's name and understands the descending Jerusalem as driving him away. His comparison with biblical narratives and earlier designs makes compositional transformation consequential: the image can generate a prophetic relation rather than merely reproduce an antecedent text [@heppner1986Jerusalem, pp. 4–5, 9–10]. This reading prevents us from treating every female personification as passive by definition. It also opens a harder question for the political reconstruction: whose agency is represented when a nation appears as a woman? The force of a collective personification cannot stand in for the powers of the women, workers or dissenters associated with it.

## The public image of a nation distributes credibility

Matthews reads the 1809 exhibition as an intervention in national visual culture. Her argument connects public art, Barry and Wollstonecraft, placing Jane Shore alongside the national heroes. She also challenges an inference from evangelical echoes to agreement with evangelical regulation [@matthews2010Gallery, paras. beginning “Blake’s 1809 exhibition can be seen” and “Blake’s decision to include”; notes 18–28, 42–49, 67]. The national question concerns whose embodiment and sexuality can appear publicly, and under what judgment.

A school history, civic monument or association's celebration can distribute credibility before its organizers exercise formal jurisdiction. Some participants appear as makers of the common world; others appear as its moral problem. Non-exhaustion therefore concerns the means of representation as well as an officer's disclaimer that a body speaks only for a purpose. Participants need ways to contest the exemplary figures, omissions and standards through which that purpose becomes public. Jerusalem 3's arts statement makes this question internal to national flourishing: cultural provision can widen production while cultural policing reserves to officials the power to certify imaginative health [@blakeJerusalem, plate 3, E146].

Blake also supplies a countertext to the preferred non-fusion thesis. His letter to Flaxman of 19 October 1801 hopes that France and England become one country and their arts one, amid enthusiasm for peace and artistic work [@blakeFlaxman1801, para. 3, E718]. Shared creation can therefore appear under a stronger image of unification than this reconstruction adopts. The distinction to defend is between making a common artistic world and authorizing a common government: neither undertaking fixes the rightful scale or powers of the other.

## A shared national voice can carry incompatible political projects

Ferber traces the hymn commonly called Jerusalem through wartime mobilization and women’s suffrage, and follows how performance and editorial changes alter its address. First-person vows become a collective undertaking through a chorus [@ferber2000Hymn, pp. 83–86, 87–89]. A national work is made again through its use: common words and melody can sustain different political projects. The reception history makes national production concrete without assigning the later users’ purposes to Blake.

The distinction has an institutional consequence. A representative body may commission a shared performance and invite several communities. Attending, contributing, endorsing a statement, authorizing a representative and consenting to a decision remain separate acts. Singing need not surrender a treaty claim; declining to sing need not reject common provision. A record that collapses these acts can manufacture authorization from evidence of contact. Participants can care deeply about the common work while disputing who invokes it in their name.

Unofficial choirs, workers’ publications and women’s assemblies also need access to venues, funding and circulation. Protecting only expression already certified as representative lets recognized bodies monopolize the means by which alternatives become audible. Composition without capture consequently protects both common cultural production and the capacity to contest claims made about participation.

## Gender and the supposedly universal subject

Mellor distinguishes imaginative reciprocity from sexual equality. Brotherhood and androgyny can leave creative labor and authority unequally distributed; mutual dependence can coexist with subordination [@mellor1982, pp. 148–153]. Her reading of Jerusalem plate 99 challenges the apparent symmetry of the final transformation. The question for this reconstruction is therefore concrete: who can initiate a claim, interpret the common vocabulary, refuse a function and redistribute authority?

Kaplan's central distinction sharpens the manuscript's gender objection. An attack on possessive patriarchs need not produce equal authority between genders. A brotherhood can redistribute power among men while leaving women responsible for the relations through which that fraternity becomes possible [@kaplan1996Patriarchy, pp. 70–71, 77–78]. The relevant rival interpretation therefore accepts much of the emancipatory reading before challenging its conclusion. Deliverance from jealous ownership, criticism of punitive morality and affirmation of sexual life may coexist with a hierarchy of creative subjects and emanated participants. Listing those emancipatory commitments does not answer the objection. The reconstruction has to explain which relations it can inherit and which it must transform.

The difficulty reaches the subject of our reconstruction. A grammar may insist that persons exceed classifications while reserving interpretation and decision for a privileged participant and assigning mediation, care or embodiment to others. Its universal person can therefore be partial before any classification begins. The test asks who may initiate a claim, refuse a function, interpret the shared vocabulary and redistribute authority. Neutral names for the sorts in [@def:typed] do not themselves remove a hierarchy built into their relations.

Ostriker identifies four partly inconsistent orientations: liberation, complementarity, erotic confinement and female subordination. Oothoon's agency does not secure deliverance, and the late poems leave the distribution of mediation and authority contested [@ostriker1982, pp. 156–164]. These orientations cut across any simple story of an early emancipatory Blake followed by a uniformly patriarchal one. They direct attention to relationships within the proposed union, including the position of the person who refuses its promised harmony.

Freedom to refuse must include the possibility of remaining. Mellor's criticism of gendered creative priority asks who determines the purposes of a shared world, even when everyone is said to contribute to it [@mellor1982, pp. 149–153]. Simpson's account of a person who affirms citizenship while contesting exclusion from membership puts a related problem in a different political setting [@simpson2007, pp. 76–78]. A person may reject an assigned role without wishing to abandon a people, place or relationship. Supported passage therefore needs a companion: standing to challenge the terms of participation from within. The federation must protect that challenge without requiring an exhaustive account of the claimant's identity or assuming that it originates the people's authority.

Q11 places interchange and dominion in the same speech. Its juxtaposition tests the reconstruction more severely than a promise of union taken alone: the other participant's refusal must remain an action to answer, rather than a deficiency the whole is entitled to overcome.


\Needspace{9\baselineskip}

> All Human Forms identified even Tree Metal Earth & Stone. all\
> Human Forms identified, living going forth & returning wearied\
> Into the Planetary lives of Years Months Days & Hours reposing\
> And then Awaking into his Bosom in the Life of Immortality.\
> And I heard the Name of their Emanations they are named Jerusalem

Q15. William Blake, Jerusalem 99:1–5, E258–259. [@blakeJerusalem].

The ending moves through years, months, days and hours into renewed life; it neither abandons temporal rhythms nor supplies an institutional voting rule. Plate 99 places the naming of emanations above a design of intertwined forms. Read alongside the dominion language of Q11, this juxtaposition turns the distribution of agency into a question for the theory: who names the shared life, and who can refuse an assigned part in it? Mellor’s account of gendered creative priority and Ostriker’s analysis of female bodies make the attractive image of union answerable to these questions [@mellor1982, pp. 149–153; @ostriker1982, pp. 162–165]. A shared world needs participants who can interpret and revise its terms, including from within a continuing relationship.


The plate 99 dispute makes that test unusually precise. Kaplan disagrees with Mellor's interpretation of Jerusalem's musculature, attributing it instead to problems of drawing and the influence of Michelangelo. He nevertheless locates absorption in the embrace and its narrative setting [@kaplan1996Patriarchy, p. 82 n. 27]. Agreement on the political objection thus does not establish agreement on its visual evidence. A stronger response must distinguish the contested bodily feature from the relation inferred between the figures. It cannot dismiss the larger criticism merely by offering another explanation of musculature, or certify that criticism by treating bodily appearance as an unambiguous code.

The final five lines also complicate a complete disappearance thesis: their last act names the emanations Jerusalem [@blakeJerusalem, 99:1–5, E258–259]. Naming can register persistence without establishing an equal capacity to initiate, refuse or judge. Conversely, an image of union need not by itself demonstrate the elimination of every difference. The issue is what account of agency makes these features cohere. The political construction should therefore treat reciprocal union and assimilation as rival readings whose explanatory burdens remain visible. Its own commitment is that common life must leave participants able to contest their assigned part. That commitment cannot depend on proving that the poem's conclusion has already achieved it.

Herrstrom supplies a substantive rival to a reading organized wholly around escape from incorporation. He reads Jerusalem through the reconstruction of a living human body and the reunion of furnace and wheel: transformed unity answers estrangement [@herrstrom1981Cherubim, pp. 66, 68, 75]. The reconstruction must therefore defend independent persons within relations, rather than independence from all relations. People can owe contributions to shared food, care, language and infrastructure without becoming the property of their administrators. Its normative premise is that participants retain standing to dispute the purpose and distribution of that common work, including when they reject the whole’s account of their refusal.

## Reprieve, release and the conditions attached to forgiveness

Heppner leaves a difficult visual fact in view: the accusers depart, but the woman remains bound. His account connects dependency and strength in her posture and asks the spectator to participate in the unwritten words near Jesus's finger [@heppner1983Adultery, pp. 49–54]. Reprieve and release are different. Reversing an accusation does not return a job or remove a dependency. A successful appeal needs an actor responsible for the remaining constraint and resources to remove it; relief cannot depend on gratitude to the institution that grants it.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}

> Saying, Doth Jehovah Forgive a Debt only on condition that it shall\
> Be Payed? Doth he Forgive Pollution only on conditions of Purity\
> That Debt is not Forgiven! That Pollution is not Forgiven\
> Such is the Forgiveness of the Gods, the Moral Virtues of the\
> Heathen, whose tender Mercies are Cruelty. But Jehovahs Salvation\
> Is without Money & without Price, in the Continual Forgiveness of Sins\
> In the Perpetual Mutual Sacrifice in Great Eternity! for behold!

Q20. William Blake, Jerusalem 61:17–23, E212. Selected excerpt. [@blakeJerusalem].

Joseph reports the dream-voice after Mary's challenge to his rejection and his embrace of her. The speech questions forgiveness conditional on payment or purity while retaining perpetual mutual sacrifice. Its religious relation supplies the terms of the difficulty. In the institutional reconstruction, a claimant regains basic standing without first accepting an association's judgment of their character, reconciling with its officials or ending criticism. A person harmed by an office need not forgive it to obtain protection. Accountability can still address the act, evidence, restitution and prevention of further harm.

Jerusalem makes the distinction between persons and states a condition of forgiveness, rather than simply a theory of identity:

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}

> Learn therefore O Sisters to distinguish the Eternal Human\
> That walks about among the stones of fire in bliss & woe\
> Alternate! from those States or Worlds in which the Spirit travels:\
> This is the only means to Forgiveness of Enemies[.]

Q27. William Blake, Jerusalem 49:72–75, E199. [@blakeJerusalem].

Erin's address has just described heavens feeding upon condemned souls, then refused to identify Luvah with the state named Satan. Deliverance recurs “time after time”: it cannot depend on certifying that a person has finally acquired an immaculate identity. The enemy remains someone whose present relation can change. This is more demanding than tolerating harmless difference, since the distinction operates amid accusations, sacrifice and the enjoyment of another's condemnation [@blakeJerusalem, 49:60–75, E199].

The passage also resists an easy administrative translation. Erin calls distinguishing person and state the *only* means to forgiveness; the ensuing response asks that the remembrance of sin be taken away. That religious demand cannot settle what an institution should preserve as evidence of injury. Nor can an office require a harmed person to forgive an adversary, abandon a complaint or endorse the office's account of reconciliation. A public record may need to retain what happened precisely so that the victim does not have to recount it repeatedly [@blakeJerusalem, 50:18–30, E200].

Durable evidence differs from a permanent civic sentence. It can preserve an account of a harmful act, impose a warranted protective restriction and assign restitution without treating the person as the exhaustive embodiment of the wrong. Revision needs its own conditions: which evidence could change the restriction, who hears the request, and what protections continue for those exposed to renewed harm? Passage concerns the conditions under which relations can alter; it does not require that every injured participant resume the relation. Forgiveness supplies a challenge to punitive identification while leaving the institutional duties of protection and accountability to be defended.

# From imaginative federation to shared rule and self-rule {#sec:s4}

Architecture A is a federation of enduring relational possibilities. Historical nations articulate, combine and transform those possibilities through particular practices. The federal whole is enacted in translation, encounter, common work, hospitality, disagreement and passage. It is not exclusively embodied by a dominant nation. This preserves the distinctive states/nations inversion.

At this level federality names a philosophical grammar. It does not yet specify shared rule and self-rule, public revenue, adjudication or representation. Containment means that possibilities remain intelligible and encounterable, not that every possible condition receives a political entitlement. Whether this grammar adds explanatory value beyond existing pluralist thought is a live question.

Architecture B distinguishes that imaginative constitution from a legal constitution. The latter must allocate real powers among territorial units, task-specific authorities, national-cultural bodies and shared institutions. A watershed and a language community may need different boundaries. A migrant may be subject to a jurisdiction without belonging to a recognized national association. A nation may cross jurisdictions without acquiring an unlimited police power over its members.

Common personhood, bodily security, conscience, expression, due process, material access and contestability are independently introduced normative premises. They do not follow automatically from a poem. Differentiated arrangements may protect freedom where uniform administration reproduces dispossession; unequal institutional forms are not automatically unequal standing. But every asymmetry needs a reason, an affected population and a means of challenge.

Three proposed mechanisms translate the grammar into institutional questions. A translation obligation requires accessible reasons across national narratives and an account of whom the deciding institution does not represent. A passage guarantee protects portable basic standing and practical affiliation change without eliminating legitimate continuing debts or duties. A non-exhaustion rule denies an organization's claim to speak every member's whole identity. Together, these mechanisms specify the institutional work of composition without capture.


## Making together can change the vocabulary of the whole

Viscomi's reconstruction of illuminated printing places invention within execution: graphic experiments could generate projects that did not precede the technique. The finished composite does not establish either a prior illustrated plan or an original rejection of the marketplace [@viscomi2012Invention, secs. II–IV, especially sec. IV, paras. beginning “With no page designs” and “Illuminated printing evolved”]. This bears directly on the states–nations inversion. If nations only selected from a complete repertoire already known to the theorist, temporal production would rearrange possibilities while the theorist retained authority over their meaning. Composition must also allow collective work to change what participants can perceive and articulate.

The ending of Jerusalem gives imaginative production a reciprocal, dramatic form:

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}

> And they conversed together in Visionary forms dramatic which bright\
> Redounded from their Tongues in thunderous majesty, in Visions\
> In new Expanses, creating exemplars of Memory and of Intellect\
> Creating Space, Creating Time according to the wonders Divine\
> Of Human Imagination, throughout all the Three Regions immense

Q29. William Blake, Jerusalem 98:28–32, E257–258. Selected excerpt; the sentence continues into line 33. [@blakeJerusalem].

Conversation here produces exemplars, memory and intellectual relations. Its participants do more than exchange finished objects in a common space: the space and time of encounter also change. Read with national generation, this makes inheritance productive. A shared public can make something through which its members remember differently, rather than merely distribute recognized traditions among predetermined seats. The reconstruction must leave room for such changes without requiring them to count as the birth of a new nation.

Conversation follows armed, fourfold annihilation; the creatures proceed irresistibly, and participants walk as One Man according to fitness and order. Discourse varies with organs of perception, yet the final covenant also claims an embracing unity. The passage can support a strong reading of transformed common life as well as the worry that reconciliation leaves no place from which to refuse [@blakeJerusalem, 98:1–27, 33–54, E257–258].

For this political construction, conversation has to permit a change in what the whole can say about itself. A worker's account might reorganize a cultural dispute around hours and wages; a people's objection might turn consultation into negotiation over jurisdiction. Neither change follows merely from including another voice in a diagram. The record should preserve the initial question, the proposed reframing, who can authorize a changed decision and which objections remain unresolved. Common making then has a consequence that can be examined: participants acquire power to alter the terms under which they are understood, while retaining the means to continue the dispute.

A new public can reveal a relation missing from the initial classification. A language dispute might become a dispute about working hours once participants describe how translation is provided. Revision then changes the object of deliberation, rather than moving a person between established labels. The decision record should retain that change and its reasons. Recurrence remains intelligible without making the current vocabulary complete.

Viscomi's later Turner essay also questions whether Blake's refusal to supply detached designs in 1818 establishes absolute indivisibility. Reused images, available prints and practical artistic choices complicate that account [@viscomi2022Turner, secs. II–III, paras. beginning “Questioning Blake’s verity” and “Blake really was redirecting”]. Participation in a composite can coexist with a part's further uses. Politically, shared institutions can acquire capacities that participants do not exercise separately, while participants retain purposes the institution does not authorize. Continuing water or infrastructure obligations can be specified without giving the institution possession of a contributor's future activity.

## Translation without compulsory legibility

Simpson makes refusal a question of knowledge production as well as membership. Her discussion of Kahnawake connects sovereignty, gendered recognition and local disagreement; the interview distinguishes lived citizenship from formal membership while withholding parts of a personal predicament. Stopping the recording becomes part of ethnographic responsibility [@simpson2007, pp. 67–78]. Translation, on this account, must attend to who controls the questions and the record.

A passage guarantee should not require exhaustive legibility. A person may contest a classification without furnishing a replacement total identity. The translation obligation makes an institution's coercive act and competence answerable; it does not entitle the federation to complete cultural, familial or spiritual disclosure. Refusal of disclosure, membership, a particular decision and the forum's jurisdiction are different actions. None may be automatically recorded as missing data, administrative failure or assent.

There is a hard converse: information protection cannot give officials an unlimited defense against people challenging exclusion. An affected person may need records a governing body prefers to withhold. A defensible procedure must identify the relevant decision, evidence, access rights, sensitive-information protections and the forum's warrant. Neither universal disclosure nor collective secrecy settles that conflict. The procedure must reconcile access to reasons with protection against compulsory disclosure.


## Counterpublics and the means of contestation

Fraser makes the right to remain collective as well as individual. Her counterpublics provide settings in which subordinated people develop interpretations of their needs and identities; their withdrawal and regrouping can support intervention in wider public debate. She also warns that counterpublics can reproduce exclusion [@fraser1992PublicSphere, pp. 122–124]. A federation should therefore protect the formation of opposition within and across recognized nations without equating every opposition with a new sovereign unit. A women's forum, a workers' publication or an unaffiliated residents' assembly may need resources and access to common institutions while refusing the claim to represent an entire people. Cultural plurality cannot be measured only by the organizations already awarded seats.

Her argument about participation also exceeds the removal of formal barriers: unequal social relations can continue to structure discussion after a forum admits everyone [@fraser1992PublicSphere, pp. 118–121]. In this reconstruction, reimbursement for a hearing cannot be assumed to neutralize dependence on an employer or an association that controls housing. Support must reach the conditions under which people prepare, speak and return to daily life. A study should therefore examine who can lose work, care or accommodation through contestation, alongside who appears on a participant list.

This changes what the translation obligation must secure. A claimant needs more than permission to submit reasons in an authorized language: people need places and time to formulate reasons together before presenting them to a body they oppose. Funding only official national representatives can make dissent dependent on the very institutions it challenges. A possible arrangement would support independent meeting space, interpretation, childcare and circulation of arguments, with allocation criteria that opponents can themselves question. These are proposals for the present reconstruction. They do not follow from labeling a group a counterpublic, and their effect would depend on whether participants actually control the relevant means.

Fraser distinguishes publics that form opinion from those that also make decisions, then raises the accountability of decision-making bodies to people affected outside them [@fraser1992PublicSphere, pp. 134–136]. That distinction exposes a weakness in a record that simply names a consultation forum. A resident may be fully heard while every consequential power remains elsewhere. The record must identify what can change after an intervention: the agenda, the evidence considered, an allocation, an enforcement condition or the rule of representation. Different bodies can carry different powers, but the connection between argument and decision must be stated. Independence from officials and influence over officials are separate institutional achievements.

This also prevents counterpublics from becoming another compulsory national taxonomy. People may organize around an injury that crosses languages and histories, or around an exclusion inside a recognized community. They need not prove a distinct collective essence to secure a hearing. The federation's obligation would concern access and power for the specified dispute, not the certification of an additional permanent identity. A shared world remains open when its participants can create new sites of argument without obtaining prior approval for their account of who they are.

# Following a classification from named entity to enforceable consequence {#sec:s5}

Entomological Law follows organisms through different evidentiary, regulatory, property, protective and moral roles. Its §§2 and 11–13 motivate inspection of the institutional translations that make the same entity visible in different ways. The human political extension borrows this discipline of description. It neither equates human persons with regulated insects nor makes dignity depend on a classifier's recognition. [@friedman2026law].

The classification-decision record in this project is explicitly new. It separates the person or collective, any claimed poetic-state interpretation, national affiliations, assigned legal role, temporal scope, classification warrant, asserting institution, competence warrant and consequence. An assertion of authority is not its validation. A correct classification does not automatically authorize the body asserting it to impose the proposed consequence.

Jerusalem 74 describes a further danger: a record may preserve its authority by closing the range of experience that can count against it.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}

> The Spectre is the Reasoning Power in Man; & when separated\
> From Imagination, and closing itself as in steel, in a Ratio\
> Of the Things of Memory. It thence frames Laws & Moralities\
> To destroy Imagination! the Divine Body, by Martyrdoms & Wars

Q26. William Blake, Jerusalem 74:10–13, E229. [@blakeJerusalem].

The temporal sequence matters. Reason becomes destructive through separation, enclosure and the use of remembered things to frame an exclusive rule. The target is a relation among faculties and their products. It is insufficient to identify one institution as rational and another as imaginative, then transfer power to the latter. Indeed, the same plate asks for understanding of Divine Law, implicates Los in driving people from their mountains, and describes the division of sixteen into twelve by cruel laws. A classification can help bring about the population it subsequently claims merely to describe [@blakeJerusalem, 74:14–32, E229–230].

White reads this passage through the difference between a revisable ratio of experience and a system that prevents further knowledge. His argument joins Blake's defense of practical art and science to criticism of their philosophical overextension [@white2005Science, pp. 108–109, 116–117, 122–123]. That interpretation supports retaining measurement while questioning its sufficiency. His insistence on the truth of immediate experience also leaves a political difficulty: an official's asserted certainty cannot determine which testimony others must accept [@white2005Science, pp. 108–109, 123].

The decision record therefore needs more than a correction box. It must allow a claimant to show that its existing categories miss the relevant injury. A language-service file may need to become a record of unpaid translation work; an affiliation dispute may reveal an employment dependency. The institution must identify who can revise the question, suspend an adverse consequence and preserve provision while the revision occurs. Otherwise, impeccable records of earlier decisions can supply the steel in which a present grievance is enclosed.

The Figure 13 caption, printed p. 28/PDF p. 30, associated with §12.2, supplies a particularly concrete warning: issuing jurisdiction does not establish reach or enforcement [@friedman2026law, sec. 12.2, Fig. 13]. The prototype consequently records issuer and level, competence source, affected reach, enforcement actor and limits separately. Counting institutions, members or citations does not weight legal importance or assign constitutional authority.

The record also requires a contestation forum, voice, materially supported exit, revision, public reasons and representation limits. In resource decisions it asks who pays and who bears residual risk. Affected nonmembers must have standing. Treaty and refusal questions must receive their own treatment. These fields expose missing warrants, excluded participants and unassigned costs.

The software flags specified transitions: a vision ranking determining rights, poetic interpretation serving as the sole warrant for coercion, or an affected population excluded from review. A completed record makes these claims available for inspection. Practical inquiry must then establish whether reasons are truthful, hearings accessible and remedies effective.

Consider a recognition decision concerning a bilingual household. The entities are individual members, who retain standing; the role might be eligible users of a language service. The asserting body could be a municipal service board. Its competence requires a warrant to supply the service; its reach might be residents using specified facilities. An enforcement actor might recover a defined fee with reviewable limits. None of these statements authorizes the board to assign an eternal identity, govern the household abroad or suspend its ordinary franchise. This invented example exposes a transition the record must justify rather than hide within a national label.

An Ostrom-informed extension asks who occupies a position, who can enter or leave it, what information is available, which actions are permitted, how choices are aggregated, and how costs and benefits are assigned. Authority to revise the rules must also be located. Ostrom’s action situation separates actors, positions, actions, information, outcome mappings and assigned costs and benefits [@ostrom2010, pp. 646–648, Figs. 2–3]. The implemented record makes these selected safeguards explicit and retains the decision evidence needed to investigate them.


## Who must activate review?

Shachar's joint-governance proposal differentiates submatters, prohibits monopoly and identifies reversal points [@shachar2005, pp. 71–73]. Its treatment of arbitration also locates the burden of activating protection. Independent advice and recorded choice can leave loyalty pressures intact; review before an award becomes enforceable shifts initiative away from the vulnerable party [@shachar2005, pp. 75–77]. The mechanism matters because a right whose exercise jeopardizes housing, care or belonging may remain unused.

Los’s own work makes protective authority answerable. His search through London in plate 45 attends to injuries whose hidden authors escape him; vengeance would punish the already punished. Plate 91 follows the attack on destructive aggregation with the severe remaking of the Spectre and a demand for obedience [@blakeJerusalem, 45:2–38, E194; 91:18–58, E251–252]. Replacing moral rank with intellectual rank can preserve the classifier’s privilege to decide whose judgment counts. Herrstrom’s guard-to-guide transformation supplies the corresponding question: does protection enable an adverse claim, including one against its provider, or control the threshold of participation [@herrstrom1981Cherubim, p. 65]?

An applicant needing water should not have to reconcile with the alleged source of injury or supply a total personal narrative before receiving it. Where a supplier knows that a disputed decision will interrupt essentials, notice to an identified reviewer, continuing provision and a response deadline can shift initiating work away from the dependent claimant. The reviewer’s preventive duty also needs a limit and an independent challenge route. Helpful administration cannot become the condition of survival.

Compare three arrangements while holding a person's dependency on housing, work and care constant. Exclusive association control may leave no effective remedy. A formal individual appeal may place every monetary and social cost on the dissenter. A defined review condition before enforcement may redistribute that burden, but can also create delay, professional gatekeeping or collusion. An unconditional public provision might reduce dependency more effectively than another jurisdictional forum. More institutions cannot become the result variable for freedom.

A reviewer also needs justified competence, a standard, access to evidence, a workable timetable and means to make relief effective. Its funding, appointments, assumptions and accountability remain open to challenge. Protective review may be demanded by an excluded person and resisted as incorporation by a collective authority. The paper cannot pre-decide that conflict by calling either claim neutral. A jointly constituted forum is a candidate response whose warrant still has to be argued. Religious-arbitration proposals and treaty relationships cannot be assimilated into one class of voluntary associations.

# Plural peoples, overlapping powers and the conditions of non-domination {#sec:s6}

Nicolaïdis's demoicracy is the closest concise comparator: plural peoples acting jointly without fusion into one demos. Her normative benchmark is not a description of every European practice, and an extension to overlapping intra-state nations requires new argument. Equal standing among peoples and equal influence among persons can conflict. The project must defend voting rules rather than promise that all forms of equality coincide. [@nicolaidis2013].

Nicolaïdis distinguishes a normative benchmark from observed European practice, develops recognition among demoi, and addresses voting, rights, mediation and accountability [@nicolaidis2013, author ms. pp. 7–16]. Joint rule concerns the terms on which people remain capable of governing together. Mutual recognition must therefore confront the control of dissenters within each constituent people as well as relations among its governing bodies.

Kymlicka’s author synopsis differentiates minority-rights claims, while Shachar’s joint-governance article examines authority sharing and vulnerable insiders [@kymlicka1995, author synopsis; @shachar2005, pp. 71–77]. Together they prevent recognition of a nation from becoming an undifferentiated entitlement to govern everyone classified within it. Exit cannot substitute for voice when departure costs housing, livelihood, language access or family relations. [@kymlicka1995]; [@shachar2001].

Bauböck supplies a direct challenge to national-cultural autonomy based on politically consequential registers. A nominal choice among permitted identities may still impose exclusive identification, exclude mixed or new affiliations, and entangle franchise or taxation. Open cultural association and public jurisdiction must remain different relations. This objection cannot be answered by renaming an exclusive register fluid. [@baubock2001].

Bauböck distinguishes cultural liberty, recognition and political autonomy, then examines consequential registers and the exclusion of multiple identification [@baubock2001, PDF pp. 3–7, 21–26]. A many-to-many cultural map leaves the franchise unsettled. A full public vote for every affiliation may duplicate influence; one compulsory final affiliation restores exclusivity at the point of decision. The response has to distinguish equal general representation from several limited memberships and defend its fiscal effects and provision for unrecognized people.

Ostrom's polycentric governance asks about rules and performance; Hooghe and Marks distinguish general-purpose and task-specific architectures. Neither turns a cultural nation into a functional jurisdiction. Overlap can enable adaptation or diffuse responsibility. Water, labor, housing and revenue may resist tidy decomposition. Their historical California illustrations are not current evaluations of California institutions. [@ostrom2010]; [@hoogheMarks2003].

Ostrom treats property as a bundle of rights and develops design principles as contextual regularities rather than universal rules [@ostrom2010, pp. 651–653]. This shifts the comparison from the number of governing bodies to the rules that organize entry, information, decisions, benefits and sanctions. A watershed authority must be assessed through those relations and the resource it governs.

Hooghe and Marks distinguish general-purpose Type I from task-specific Type II arrangements. Nonintersecting Type I memberships can coexist with overlapping competencies; Type II bodies often operate within Type I settings and have variable franchises [@hoogheMarks2003, pp. 233–241, especially p. 237 n. 6]. Stable territorial citizenship can therefore coexist with shared water competence, while a functional body can still exclude affected residents from its electorate. Boundary overlap and democratic inclusion are separate questions.

Treaty federalism raises a prior question: a people may contest the federation's authority to incorporate it. This concerns the source of jurisdiction, rather than simply the range of cultural choices available within an accepted state. It changes what counts as agreement to the common institutions [@henderson2019; @simpson2007].

Henderson treats treaty federalism as constitutive of Canadian authority, rather than an added minority concession. Inherent powers and continuing treaty relationships lead into a territorial account of treaty governance and proposals for structural and fiscal change [@henderson2019, pp. 21–41]. His institutional argument contains a consequential tension: direct treaty representation could lessen reliance on consent, consultation and accommodation (p. 38), while consolidation requires each Treaty Nation's legitimate consent (p. 40). Representation and agreement therefore have to be distinguished within the design itself.

A federation cannot prove its title by first classifying every people as one of its constituents. Authority to recognize and allocate powers may itself be contested. The proposed record therefore needs a *jurisdictional remainder*: a question not presumed resolved when other fields are completed. This is our term. A people may negotiate without conceding that the forum originates its standing. Affiliation, residence, property claims, public competence and treaty relations remain separately describable, but decomposition does not make them materially independent. Dividing a land relationship into a language service and a water entitlement may discard the very claim under dispute.

A delegate's presence, a mandate to negotiate, assent to a particular agreement and a continuing power to reopen it also differ. An ordinary shared decision cannot promise every person's agreement, yet a general majority cannot be assumed to dispose of every jurisdictional claim. The institutional task is to identify the form of agreement required for a decision and defend whose agreement counts. This proposed burden of justification remains contestable.

The UN Declaration connects self-determination and institutions (Articles 3–5), participation and consultation toward consent (18–19), land and redress (25–28), projects and institutions (32–35), and cross-border relations and treaties (36–37). Its sex-equality and human-rights provisions, alongside the territorial-integrity language of Article 46, require these claims to be read together [@undrip2007, Articles 3–5, 18–19, 25–28, 32–37, 44, 46]. A classification record must identify which relation and which decision are at issue.

Pettit's account makes non-capture more demanding than freedom from actual punishment. Dependency persists when someone controls access to housing, work or essential services, even if that power is exercised benevolently. Protection from private power and control of the protective state are separate requirements [@pettit2012, pp. 3–10]. A new appeals authority must therefore be assessed for the power it acquires as well as the protection it promises.

Pettit also distinguishes collective electoral authorization from the control retained by persons in their plurality. Reactive, representative and regulative contestation require public reasons and effective correction; representatives can nevertheless become lobbies, and appeals depend on impartiality [@pettit2005, pp. 1–4, 8–14]. Voice, practical exit and revision impose different demands: a forum must hear challenges, a person needs means to leave, and rules must be open to change.

The relevant capacity is the power to make a challenge change what an authority may do. Pettit's account includes procedures that constrain decisions before a person must appeal [@pettit2005, pp. 9–14]. Shachar makes this concrete by proposing review as a condition of enforceability in family-law arbitration, with an agreed standard and an identified reviewing body [@shachar2005, pp. 75–77 and p. 76 nn. 89–90]. Applied to the hypothetical watershed, the comparison specifies who initiates scrutiny, who pays, what happens to essential provision during delay and whether relief can alter the allocation. A procedure that requires workers to risk their wages before it operates may preserve the dependency it promises to remedy.

The contribution is a critical grammar joining nonidentity, national generation and imaginative passage to specific institutional transitions. Demoicracy supplies joint rule among plural peoples; autonomy and non-domination supply tests of power. Blake supplies a demanding account of the person who exceeds a present state. The proposed record asks where those accounts agree, where their demands conflict and what decision follows.

## An institutional comparator: two chambers, resources and remedies

The Swiss constitutional text examined here makes several equalities coexist through different institutions. Population-based representation in the National Council operates alongside a separate cantonal representation rule in the Council of States; federal parliamentary decisions require both chambers' agreement. Political rights cannot be exercised contemporaneously in several Cantons. Financial autonomy has its own provisions for sufficient resources and equalization [@swissConstitution2024, arts. 39(3), 47(2), 135, 149–150, 156(2)]. This historical English text, consolidated to 1 January 2024, identifies choices for the proposed federation: which units receive collective representation, how population affects influence, and how poorer participants obtain means to govern.

Collective voice differs from a person's access to protection. The text ties the federal political franchise to Swiss citizenship and eligibility while providing procedural guarantees to every person, subject to specified conditions and exceptions [@swissConstitution2024, arts. 29–29a, 136]. Participation in an allocation hearing, access to legal assistance, a vote and a collective jurisdictional claim do different work. In the drought case, the federation must say which powers reach a migrant worker and what support makes them usable. Cantonal seats supply no answer to that question, and the court's jurisdiction and applicable-law provisions delimit the available remedy [@swissConstitution2024, arts. 189–190].

The comparison separates territorial constituent units from cultural nations. It also exposes a substantive limit on treating any federation as a pluralist exemplar: Article 72(3) prohibits minarets [@swissConstitution2024, art. 72(3)]. Institutional diversity has to be examined alongside specific exclusions. Treaty relations pose the further question of the federation's title to decide, which a cantonal analogy does not settle.

## Revising the procedures of recognition

Tully places the governing procedure within the dispute. His dialogical approach allows challenges to norms of recognition and to the procedures through which those norms are negotiated; reasonable disagreement need not disappear after a settlement [@tully2006Recognition, pp. 24–27]. This supplies a more demanding interpretation of revision than an amendment field. A person can have a route to appeal while being forbidden to challenge the categories that made the decision possible. The reconstruction should distinguish correcting a mistaken classification, changing its consequences, revising the classification rule and contesting the institution's authority to impose that rule. Success at one level cannot be presumed to settle the others.

Non-finality does not require every decision to wait for unanimity. Tully distinguishes dialogue from decision-making and retains other institutions as checks where negotiation leaves minorities vulnerable. He also asks whether ostensibly inclusive negotiations are governed at a distance through their agendas and resource inequalities [@tully2006Recognition, pp. 29–31]. Our response must therefore specify how an act can bind provisionally while its basis remains contestable. A decision could have an effective date, a review trigger and continuing protective duties without declaring that participation ratifies the forum's entire jurisdiction. Equally, repeated negotiation should not allow those who control essential resources to postpone relief indefinitely. A timetable distributes power when some parties can survive delay and others cannot.

National continuity and public contestation need different guarantees. A nation may sustain language, institutions and a claim to self-government across generations; a public may organize around one controversy and dissolve when its work changes. Giving each public a constituent veto would allow organizational multiplication to reorder the federation without a defended rule. Giving only established nations agenda rights would let inherited recognition suppress emerging opposition. A possible division would protect collective self-government through specified competences while securing direct channels for people contesting their exercise. Its legitimacy would depend on the relation between the two, including who may reopen their boundaries. Neither the age of an institution nor the novelty of its challengers determines that answer.

In the hypothetical drought, this would separate three questions currently liable to collapse. Workers need drinking water and a practicable role in decisions affecting their lives. A Treaty Nation may contest the watershed body's title to allocate water. Residents may challenge an upstream association's control of storage while declining membership in it. These claims concern intersecting material relations but do not carry identical warrants. A temporary provision agreement should identify who undertakes the duty, who funds it, what participation authorizes and which jurisdictional claims remain unsettled. The preservation of a claim cannot itself supply water; the supply of water cannot silently extinguish the claim.

Remaining together consequently requires more than portable standing and toleration of disagreement. Participants need means to develop opposition, powers through which it can affect decisions and routes to contest the procedures that distribute those powers. They may continue to disagree about the federation's story, their own representative institutions and the proper source of common authority. Composition without capture names a defensible task only if these disagreements can alter the arrangement while people retain the conditions of participation. Its political value lies in exposing the points where such alteration is blocked, including by the reconstruction's own account of reconciliation.

# Five Xeno-California conflicts: standing, resources and counterfactual repair {#sec:s7}

Xeno-California is a hypothetical stress test, not a blank description of California or a proposal imposed on actual peoples. Its cases must keep land, labor, infrastructure, power and affected nonmembers visible. Each case has two machine-readable alternatives; their labels identify stipulated design differences, not observed political outcomes.

Case A places several nations and unaffiliated residents in one territory. The bounded alternative grounds common services and ordinary political standing in personhood and residence while permitting language and cultural institutions. The capture alternative requires one exclusive national registration to obtain those services. The relevant evidence in a future study would include effective access, internal dissent and representation, not the number of recognized organizations.

Case B places one national community across jurisdictions. A limited cultural agreement can coordinate institutions without moving borders or licensing universal control over co-nationals. The capture alternative treats the association's national claim as authority over every affiliate everywhere. The issuer's level, the agreement's reach and the body able to enforce it must be separately specified. Territorial institutions retain duties to residents who reject the cultural organization.

Case C asks how a new generation becomes a public. Recognition need not require common descent or imitation of a constitutional list of old nations. But a new public's cultural claims do not automatically generate taxing, policing or secession powers. The rejected alternative infers coercive competence from an asserted eternal-state interpretation. A future mechanism must distinguish opportunities to organize, public recognition, representation and authorized powers.

Case D refuses a civic hierarchy of vision. Officials who classify a community as fourfold cannot infer that it deserves leadership over a supposedly single-vision community. Changing from three to four or six interpretive lenses in the prototype leaves admissibility unchanged. That software control establishes only the absence of a programmed lens-count entitlement; it does not validate any interpretation of fourfold vision.

Case E involves a wealthy national association seeking fiscal autonomy, an Indigenous community with land and water claims, migrant labor without association membership, and downstream affected residents. A meaningful alternative identifies duties, standing, public finance, ability to pay, essential access, residual risks and review. The capture alternative excludes nonmembers, leaves costs unspecified and absorbs treaty/refusal issues into ordinary association membership. The checks reject those omissions under proposed norms.

The resource case also needs comparison beyond the two diagnostic examples: ordinary territorial administration, asymmetric multinational arrangements and a hybrid task-specific authority may each have advantages and failures. Future empirical work must hold material conditions explicit and examine the actual procedures, rather than define the preferred hybrid as freer. A complete record is a prerequisite for criticism, not a substitute for it.

## A worked material conflict

Suppose, solely for comparison, a drought requires reduced withdrawals. A prosperous cultural association owns upstream storage; a Treaty Nation contests the source of the authority's jurisdiction; migrant workers need safe drinking water and wages; downstream residents bear ecological and health risks. These are stipulated parties, not claims about named California communities. Collective identities neither determine hydrology nor distribute costs. A proposal must identify existing obligations, evidence of scarcity, infrastructure control and the bodies authorized to negotiate or decide.

Alternative E1 gives recognized associations exclusive representation. It appears plural because several peoples have seats. It nevertheless excludes people outside the rolls, may misidentify a Treaty Nation as another voluntary club and can let wealth buy agenda control. Adding a ceremonial fourth seat or calling the board fourfold changes none of these defects. The failure concerns distribution of power, information and exposure, not symbolic diversity.

Alternative E2 creates one territorial rule with equal per-person voting. It protects a general franchise and avoids duplicating votes through affiliations. It can still subordinate a treaty relationship to an ordinary majority and conceal unequal starting entitlements to storage. Uniform procedure may govern an unequal baseline. The question concerns which equality is specified and whether the body imposing it has established its jurisdiction.

Alternative E3 gives a watershed body limited competence while requiring separately identified treaty negotiations, general resident standing, worker representation, public hydrological evidence and review. It distinguishes emergency drinking-water protection from longer-term allocation and compensation, identifies who can revise rules and assigns compliance costs. This alternative places a further burden on the comparison: Multiple forums may slow action, fragment accountability or let powerful actors move disputes to favorable venues. Emergency powers can endure; appeal costs can exclude.

Comparison must keep physical scarcity and the resource baseline explicit while changing institutional rules. It should record access to water, costs, ability to secure a hearing, ability to survive delay and power to revise the arrangement. Essential provision cannot depend on buying national membership. Translation needs funding, reciprocal language access and limits protecting knowledge a people has reason not to disclose. Affected-person standing must confront rather than silently override self-determination. Counting affected individuals does not settle a treaty-specific jurisdictional dispute.

The record separates storage owner as property holder, supplier as service provider, watershed body as claimant of competence, and each enforcement actor. A valid scarcity measurement does not establish power to impose a levy; a valid levy does not establish that the board speaks every person's national identity. A successful cultural interpretation supplies neither warrant. The institutional test locates these transitions so opponents can dispute them.


A further test concerns interim protection while the watershed body's title is disputed. Who must keep water flowing, who can survive delay, and which emergency measure protects life without being treated as a permanent settlement of title? A protective order may be demanded by a worker while its forum is rejected by a Treaty Nation. Neither the urgency nor the objection automatically disposes of the other warrant. Compare the wealthy association's and the worker's ability to obtain interim relief under the same scarcity. Record the temporary duty, evidence, funding, duration and procedure for revisiting the act. No current California facts or legal holdings are supplied by this invented scenario.

Follow the work needed to keep E3’s challenge alive: notice, translation, emergency supply, evidence gathering and a decision about interruption. If carers and workers perform those tasks informally while recognized bodies control the budget, plural representation can preserve the same dependence. A provisional provision agreement should identify minimum service, supplier, payment and review date separately from the disputed allocation. A worker can demand continuity without speaking for a Treaty Nation; that nation can contest jurisdiction without becoming responsible for every resident’s subsistence. Participation authorizes the defined undertaking recorded, while other claims remain open.

## Counterfactual repair with the material dispute held fixed

The finite experiment applies a declared catalog of twelve safeguard interventions to each of the ten records. It preserves the subject, institutional identities, action label, affected people, affected nonmembers, coercion, resource-decision status and treaty/refusal trigger. An operator acts only when its associated rule fails. For example, nonmember standing repairs a recorded exclusion; it cannot make the excluded people disappear. Charter and competence operators insert explicitly synthetic procedural terms whose values remain visible in the result.

The baseline has 118 active checks: 107 satisfied and 11 failed. Thirty-two conditional checks have absent triggers. The revised matrix displays all three states. This accounting distinguishes an exercised safeguard from a condition that the record never encounters.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}

::: {.definition #def:repair title="A finite safeguard-repair family"}
Let $\mathcal O$ be the twelve declared interventions, $F(d)$ the failed-rule set of decision $d$, and $d_B$ the record obtained by applying $B\subseteq\mathcal O$ in the catalog's fixed order. The catalog preserves a protected context $\pi$ containing the material and jurisdictional triggers. Define

$$
\mathcal A(d)=\{B\subseteq\mathcal O:F(d_B)=\varnothing\},\qquad
m(d)=\min_{B\in\mathcal A(d)}\lvert B\rvert.
$$ {#eq:repair}

The minimum is undefined when $\mathcal A(d)$ is empty. A bundle is inclusion-minimal when none of its proper subsets belongs to $\mathcal A(d)$. Distinct subsets may produce the same record because an inactive operator makes no change. The report therefore records both subset count and distinct resulting-record count, and verifies $\pi(d_B)=\pi(d)$ throughout the family.
:::

The five capture records contribute eight, four, two, four and eight. A total of 25,600 subsets satisfy all programmed predicates. This subset total includes many selections of inactive operators; distinct records give the operational denominator.

## Complementarity appears at admission, not as an extra resolved rule

B's two changes illustrate the difference between repairing a field and obtaining an admissible conjunction. Non-capture repairs one failed predicate; contestation repairs another. Neither alone admits B, while the pair does. D has the same structure for unranked rights and equal personhood. The baseline pair interaction in the admission indicator is positive for those two pairs. Each pair resolves exactly the union of the single-operator failures: its resolved-rule excess is zero.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}

::: {.definition #def:interaction title="Admission complementarity for two interventions"}
For interventions $i$ and $j$, define

$$
\Delta_{ij}(d)=T(d_{\{i,j\}})-T(d_{\{i\}})-T(d_{\{j\}})+T(d),
$$ {#eq:interaction}

where $T$ is the conjunction in [@eq:trace]. Positive $\Delta_{ij}$ means the pair achieves admission beyond its separate baseline effects. This concerns the conjunction, so it can occur when the two interventions resolve disjoint failures.
:::

A shared-field diagnostic shows why the minimum bundle must be computed rather than inferred from the number of failures. With an interpretive warrant and blank competence source, both POETRY_TO_POWER and AUTHORITY_GAP fail. The charter operator can repair both by supplying the stipulated warrant and its source. Three structural type rules lie outside the repair catalog; a mistyped issuer remains irreparable even after every available intervention. The software retains both controls. Its search reports the minimum over the declared operations, making the scope of repair explicit.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}

::: {.proposition #prop:monotone-repair title="A sufficient condition for monotone repair"}
Suppose each intervention $f_i$ acts on a failure-set representation, satisfies $f_i(F)\subseteq F$, and is order-preserving: $F\subseteq G$ implies $f_i(F)\subseteq f_i(G)$. Apply selected operators in one fixed catalog order, substituting the identity for unselected operators. Then $B\subseteq B'$ implies $F(d_{B'})\subseteq F(d_B)$; every superset of an admissible bundle is admissible.

*Proof.* Compare the two executions at each catalog position. Initially their failure sets agree. If both select the operator, order preservation maintains inclusion; if only $B'$ selects it, contraction maintains inclusion; if neither selects it, nothing changes. Induction through the catalog proves the result. The actual field-based implementation is checked by complete finite enumeration for the supplied records. Its observed monotonicity is recorded independently of this sufficient abstract criterion.
:::

This formal structure identifies the next institutional comparison. A repair bundle specifies which commitments must be implemented together; the shared-field control identifies when one institutional change serves several safeguards. Studying an actual forum then asks whether those commitments can coexist in appointments, finance, interim provision and enforceable remedies. The computational output is the explicit bundle to investigate.

# Institutional revision through coherence, rupture and regeneration {#sec:s9}

Coherence–Rupture–Regeneration offers a vocabulary for examining accumulated practice, interruption and reconstruction [@sabine2026letter; @sabine2026phase]. In institutional terms, the question is what survives revision: care relationships, language services, entitlements and unresolved claims may need continuity even when an abusive office is abolished.

Sabine's letter derives a square-root-of-two optimum for a specified allocation problem [@sabine2026letter, sec. 6, PDF pp. 18–19]; the phase-gating author manuscript develops a toy simulation and identifies its regeneration kernel as untested [@sabine2026phase, open author manuscript, sec. 6, PDF pp. 14–15]. Here their use is comparative: stabilization can preserve an exclusion, interruption can reopen a decision, and reconstruction can either redistribute authority or restore its monopoly. The constitutional assessment concerns rights, material security and the control of the revised institution.

Amendment, negotiation, redistribution and abolition are different forms of institutional revision. Each needs a trigger, an authorized procedure, continuity arrangements and an account of who pays. A change of officeholders leaves capture intact when it preserves the same unchecked power over dependent participants.

The decision record can follow a claim across this transition: who declares the interruption, which obligations continue, who authorizes the replacement and how dissenters reopen it. Regeneration becomes a question about redistributed powers and sustained relationships rather than a label that settles the outcome.

# Research infrastructure and the contribution of the states–nations distinction {#sec:s10}

The reconstruction draws on earlier work connecting Blake, perception and methodological pluralism [@Friedman2026FourfoldVisionWilliamBlake196; @Friedman2026DoorsPerceptionAreThreshold007]. \Needspace{11\baselineskip}

::: {.definition #def:coverage title="Coverage and token-normalized retrieval"}
For primary units $U$, let $t_u$ count Unicode word tokens, $c_{uq}$ count occurrences of the declared variants of query $q$, and $h_{uq}=1$ exactly when $c_{uq}>0$. For a subset $V\subseteq U$,

$$
C_q(V)=\frac{\sum_{u\in V}h_{uq}}{\lvert V\rvert},\qquad
R_q(V)=10^4\frac{\sum_{u\in V}c_{uq}}{\sum_{u\in V}t_u}.
$$ {#eq:coverage}

Coverage is undefined for an empty subset; rate is undefined for a zero token denominator. A populated denominator with no matches gives zero. Whole-word phrase variants contribute one occurrence while their constituent words remain tokens in the denominator.
:::

## Copy repetition and family balance change different results

Equal-family weighting changes the state rate to 3.48624 per 10,000 tokens ([@fig:copy-balanced-rates]). It can increase as well as decrease a rate: imagination rises from 2.03339 pooled to 4.35097 equal-family, and exchange/interchange rises from 0.10865 to 0.20482. The two estimates answer different questions about the same collection.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}

::: {.definition #def:family-rate title="Equal-family rates and finite deletion sensitivity"}
Let $U_w$ collect the units bearing source-family identifier $w$, and let $W_+$ contain the families with positive token totals. Define

$$
\overline R_q=\frac{1}{\lvert W_+\rvert}\sum_{w\in W_+}R_q(U_w),\qquad
\mathcal D_q=\{R_q(U\setminus U_w):w\in W\}.
$$ {#eq:family-rate}

The equal-family mean weights each eligible family once. The minimum and maximum of defined values in $\mathcal D_q$ describe the full leave-one-family-out range for this collection. Each omission removes both its occurrences and its tokens.
:::

## Repetition, bodily constraint and the making of eternal law

Urizen has the highest state rate among the three prophetic source families compared above, but its 49 matches have one repeated form. Each of the seven retained primary copies contains seven occurrences of the refrain “a state of dismal woe”; every state-query match in those copies falls within that phrase. The seven copies are byte-distinct, so exact-text deduplication leaves this repetition intact. The source-bound context audit identifies all seven inputs and their text hashes. This is a different dependence from an accidentally duplicated file.

In the ASU transcription, the refrain closes successive ages in the production of Urizen’s body: spine and ribs, heart, eyes, ears, nostrils, throat and tongue, then limbs. Los’s labor constructs a bounded sensorium through chains, rivets and furnaces; he eventually shrinks from the task [@blakeUrizen, 10:25–44, 11:1–27, 13:1–23, E75–77]. The repeated condition carries the sequence’s affective force. Its seven occurrences do not perform the same argumentative work as Milton’s explicit distinction between individuals and states. A lexical comparison becomes stronger when it identifies that difference.

The law speech supplies the adjacent political problem. Urizen’s book does not announce itself through cruelty alone:

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}

> 8. Laws of peace, of love, of unity:\
> Of pity, compassion, forgiveness.\
> Let each chuse one habitation:\
> His ancient infinite mansion:\
> One command, one joy one desire,\
> One curse, one weight, one measure\
> One King, one God, one Law.

Q21. William Blake, The [First] Book of Urizen 4:34–40, E72. [@blakeUrizen].

Peace, love, pity and forgiveness culminate in one command and one measure. The objection is therefore directed at a form of imposed unity that can claim humane ends. The speech follows solitary writing in books of metal and receives the Eternals’ rage in response (4:24–50, E72). In the proposed federation, common provision needs binding rules, but its beneficial purpose cannot by itself authorize one institution to settle every participant’s desires, attachments and interpretation of the whole.

Near the poem’s end, children who mourn and build tombs make a further transformation:

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}

> And form\'d laws of prudence, and call\'d them\
> The eternal laws of God

Q22. William Blake, The [First] Book of Urizen 28:6–7, E83. [@blakeUrizen].

The verbs separate making a rule from calling it eternal. Historical experience and prudence matter, yet the divine designation is itself an act within the history being narrated. A federation must preserve the reasons for an inherited arrangement while keeping its authority open to challenge. This sharpens the states–nations distinction: recognizing an enduring imaginative possibility differs from making an enduring claim on other people. The institutional record accordingly separates a classification, its asserted warrant and the consequences officials seek to impose.

## Shared vocabulary identifies comparisons for close reading

The analysis computes pairwise overlap in three universes: retained primary extracts, exact-text-distinct extracts and source-family presence. The last counts a family once regardless of its number of copies. The complete result contains 570 association records, 190 query pairs in each universe.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}

::: {.definition #def:association title="Overlap, conditional presence and lift"}
In a declared universe of $n$ units, let $X_q$ and $X_r$ be the presence sets for two queries. Define

$$
\operatorname{Jac}(q,r)=\frac{\lvert X_q\cap X_r\rvert}{\lvert X_q\cup X_r\rvert},\quad
\Pr(r\mid q)=\frac{\lvert X_q\cap X_r\rvert}{\lvert X_q\rvert},\quad
\operatorname{Lift}(q,r)=\frac{n\lvert X_q\cap X_r\rvert}{\lvert X_q\rvert\lvert X_r\rvert}.
$$ {#eq:association}

Each expression is undefined when its denominator is zero. The universe and presence sets travel with every reported value. Presence in one full extraction records shared textual coverage, including distant or opposed passages.
:::

Nations and states share nine of seventeen source families in their union: Jaccard 0.52941 and lift 1.75740. Persons and states share three of fourteen: Jaccard 0.21429 and lift 1.90385. The latter has narrower shared coverage but larger relative concentration because the person/identity vocabulary occurs in only four families. Liberty/freedom and nation/people share four families; four of the five liberty-bearing families contain the national vocabulary, while only four of thirteen nation-bearing families contain liberty. Conditional presence makes that asymmetry visible.

## Corpus-wide retrieval follows an argument back to its source units

The fixed query groups support a stable comparison across versions. The new search engine also accepts ad hoc words and exact phrases across all 2,067 imported units. Its default selection is primary text; role, source plane and recorded work ID can be selected independently. An alternative query accepts any of its terms. A conjunctive query requires every term within the same stored source unit. The latter identifies a shared document context; a reading extract may encompass a whole poem, so conjunction does not imply a shared sentence or dramatic speaker.

Search preserves the source spelling and punctuation. Unicode casefolding supports case-insensitive matching, with each folded character mapped back to its original text offset. A match must align with complete source characters. Literal phrases retain their spaces and line breaks; the interface offers substring matching separately from whole-word boundaries. Search results include the edition, locator, source URL, role, rights statement, original-text hash and exact span. These fields let a reader reopen a passage and distinguish the text being interpreted from the layers through which it was retrieved.

::: {.definition #def:search-scope title="Search counts precede the displayed sample"}
For the eligible source-unit set $U$, let $H_q(u)$ be the nonoverlapping, character-aligned literal matches for query term $q$ in unit $u$. An alternative query admits units with any nonempty $H_q(u)$; a conjunctive query admits units for which every $H_q(u)$ is nonempty. Write the admitted units as $M$. The occurrence count and matched-unit count are

$$
O=\sum_{u\in M}\sum_{q\in Q}\lvert H_q(u)\rvert,\qquad B_{\mathrm{match}}=\lvert M\rvert.
$$ {#eq:search-counts}

The result limit selects the first $L$ records from the deterministic ordering of term-labeled matches. It does not change $O$, $B_{\mathrm{match}}$, the source-family facets or their eligible token denominators. Different overlapping query terms retain separate match records.
:::

## An aggregate keeps its vitality in the particulars

> May take the aggregate; & you call the aggregate Moral Law:\
> And you call that Swelld & bloated Form; a Minute Particular.\

Los distinguishes an organized whole from the destruction of particulars in the name of moral law. He speaks at the anvil in darkness; the following exchange exposes his own resort to coercion. The speech supplies an internal test of the interpreter and modeler: retaining individual rows is insufficient if the aggregate removes their capacity to answer. The statistical analyses retain work and copy labels, while the institutional experiments preserve affected people and obligations. ## Production, design and interpretive authority

Composition without capture must constrain the scholar who reconstructs it. An interpretation can give every person several affiliations yet reserve to its author the power to decide what those affiliations ultimately mean. Its generosity then depends on a monopoly over the common description. The question is whether a participant can contest the account of humanity through which inclusion is offered while continuing to share institutions, resources and obligations. A defensible reconstruction must allow disagreement with its poetic vocabulary to count as political argument. Otherwise, passage between states remains available only within a world whose interpretive boundaries someone else has fixed.

Viscomi supplies a methodological restraint on this authority. His account of the workshop makes production part of meaning and resists both excusing execution in the name of vision and decoding every mark as a symbol. Decoration can organize attention while also serving the practical requirements of etching and inking [@viscomi1982Workshop, author reprint web pp. 3–8, compilation PDF pp. 60–65]. The implication here is that neither an attractive political reading nor an exact transcription exhausts an illuminated page. A claim about reciprocity needs an account of dramatic speech and visual relations; a claim about a particular mark needs alternatives that include medium and execution. Interpretation becomes more answerable when it identifies what could defeat it.

Essick and Viscomi make the material disagreement specific. Their inquiry distinguishes monochrome, hand-colored and color-printed impressions and argues against a general two-pull account. They interpret the exceptional second printing of Nurses Song in copy E as repair [@essickViscomi2002Color, pp. 74–77, 98–102]. The physical history assigned to a feature changes what an interpretation can infer from it. Their technical conclusion is an attributed argument; the selected opening and ending supply this comparison, rather than an independent resolution of the controversy.

A transcription supports the selected wording; an identified impression supports an observation about that material page; an analytical diagram exposes the proposed relation. Captions make those objects explicit and connect a visible or measured feature to the section’s question. Copy sensitivity therefore exceeds duplicated words. Deduplication cannot decide which material differences matter; equal-family weighting cannot supply a comparison of pages. Each calculation can expose a choice that further reading and inspection must examine.

## When would computation add something?

A typed relational graph can separate persons, affiliations, interpretations and competencies. Multiplex relations can coexist without transferring authority across their layers. A model can expose a missing warrant or prohibited identification, but cannot create a political right by drawing an edge. A richer implementation should begin with a decision, rival account and independently justified norm before a compositional or active-inference formalism.

An ablation is especially important. Remove poetic-state descriptors while holding safeguards and material circumstances fixed. If the useful distinctions remain, institutional novelty belongs to political comparators, while Blake supplies critical or interpretive value. If imaginative description makes a difference, identify how: whose reasons become audible, what assumption changes and whether this is possible without compelled metaphysical assent. Current invariance under lens count is one small software control, not that entire empirical comparison.
