## 1. Equal standing requires attention to unequal conditions

**Insert:** subsection “Particularity means relevant discrimination, not exhaustive collection,” after its existing paragraph, before national mediation.

> One Law for the Lion & Ox is Oppression

The Marriage of Heaven and Hell, plate 24, E44. [Primary text](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/marriage_of_heaven_and_hell.html#MHH24;E44). Repeated prose anchor occurrence 6.

**Proposed discussion:** The maxim directs attention to what a rule does to differently situated beings. A formally identical requirement can demand unequal sacrifices: a hearing scheduled during paid working hours, a fee charged without regard to means, or a water levy imposed without examining access. Yet the lion and ox cannot simply become names for naturally entitled social ranks. Who identifies the relevant difference, and who can challenge that identification? The institutional construction joins equal standing to differentiated provision, requiring reasons for the distinction and scrutiny of its consequences. This keeps the maxim from becoming a warrant for inherited privilege. Its immediate setting sharpens the problem: the narrator has announced an interpretation of scripture that the world will receive whether it wishes to or not. A denunciation of uniform law can itself accompany interpretive command. The proposed safeguard therefore governs the classifier as well as the classified.

**Context and objection:** Read plates 22–24, especially the Angel’s conversion and the narrator’s final announcement. The argument is not that Blake endorses a modern equality test; it is that his maxim makes the relation between common standing and differentiated treatment a problem requiring an answer. Frosch’s criticism of defending Urizen’s One Law by its peaceful intention (printed p. 44) is a useful adjoining citation.

## 2. Protective power must examine where its remedy lands

**Insert:** “Who must activate review?”, replacing the first two sentences of the paragraph beginning “Los’s own work makes protective authority answerable” and retaining its later plate-91 comparison.

> If I should dare to lay my finger on a grain of sand\
> In way of vengeance; I punish the already punishd: O whom\
> Should I pity if I pity not the sinner who is gone astray!

Jerusalem 45:33–35, E194. [Primary text](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html#J45.33;E194).

**Proposed discussion:** Los’s restraint follows a failure of attribution: he sees the injured particulars but cannot find those who have possessed and degraded them. Knowledge of suffering does not yet identify a justified target. His response asks where the force of a remedy would fall. In the institutional construction, suspending an association’s water supply to punish its officers might expose residents to the very deprivation under dispute; removing a provider without continuity arrangements could burden those who depend on it. Effective review therefore records both the act to be corrected and the people who bear the correction. Immediate protection, restitution and prevention need different measures. Los’s pity for the person gone astray also preserves a distinction between stopping a harmful act and making a person permanently identical with it. The question is how to interrupt the injury without distributing another penalty through its victims.

**Context and objection:** Lines 29–32 make a stronger theological claim about Providence and vengeance; do not reduce the passage to an administrative calculation of side effects. The proposed institutional distinctions remain this article’s response. Frosch, printed pp. 45–46, helps keep release of persons distinct from approval of their beliefs or acts.

## 3. Release includes a changed relation to danger

**Insert:** “Delight in common work can coexist with exhausting dependence,” after the paragraph explaining Moyer’s comparison and before the Jerusalem 59 block.

> Rise and look out, his chains are loose, his dungeon doors are open.\
> And let his wife and children return from the opressors scourge;\
> They look behind at every step & believe it is a dream.

America a Prophecy 6:10–12, E53. [Primary text](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/america_a_prophecy.html#Am6.10;E53).

**Proposed discussion:** The opened door does not end the scene. The family returns, but the backward glance carries the former relation of terror into the first steps of release. The passage gives emancipation a bodily and temporal texture: freedom has to become credible in action. Within the federal construction, an announced right of exit is incomplete if an employer can retain wages, an office can withhold documents, or an association can threaten the departing person’s dependants. Passage requires usable means and protection against retaliation; a decision record should name who supplies them and when. The poem’s visionary dawn also exceeds the political achievement of a national declaration. Its claim that empire has ended cannot be used as a historical report that enslavement ended with American independence. National self-description and the conditions of those subject to national power must be examined separately.

**Context and objection:** This is the proclamation from the temple at 5:7–6:15; Albion’s Angel answers the voice as Orc at 7:1–7, and Orc identifies himself at 8:1. Do not attribute it to Washington or confuse Albion’s Angel with the Boston Angel. Preserve ASU’s spelling “opressors.” The excerpt is three lines from a longer emancipation sequence, not the whole plate.

## 4. Opposition can sustain a relation without making it symmetrical

**Insert:** “Translation without compulsory legibility,” after the first paragraph. The compact maxim can be an italic block or a verified inline quotation if the source ledger and validator are adapted deliberately.

> Opposition is true Friendship.

The Marriage of Heaven and Hell, plate 20, E42. [Primary text](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/marriage_of_heaven_and_hell.html#MHH20;E42). Repeated anchor occurrence 15.

**Proposed discussion:** The maxim closes a contest over who can show whom a world. The narrator has driven the Angel into a contrary vision, the Angel charges him with imposition, and the reply acknowledges that they impose on one another. Friendship here contains antagonism and correction; it does not certify an equal exchange. The political reconstruction should therefore ask whether opposition can change the terms of a continuing relation without forcing conversion to the prevailing account. An interpreter may convey a claimant’s words accurately while controlling which questions the hearing permits. Translation needs a route for challenging that framing, including through a rival interpreter or an alternative description of the dispute. The right to answer is valuable when its exercise can alter an act or its justification. Mere exposure to another’s account leaves the distribution of interpretive power unsettled.

**Context and objection:** The Angel’s later conversion into a Devil belongs to a separate Memorable Fancy, plates 22–24. Keep these episodes distinct. Do not present the plate-20 journey as an example of already voluntary, reciprocal deliberation.

## 5. New scholarship sharpens the method of reconciliation and display

Thomas Frosch’s complete scholarly review of Mitchell’s Composite Art, printed pp. 40–48, supplies a pointed disagreement. On pp. 45–46 he resists equating criticism of moralistic judgment with relativism, and distinguishes forgiveness of people from reconciliation with their ideas. This can strengthen the existing forgiveness subsection immediately after its discussion of retaining evidence while revising civic restrictions. Suggested sentence pair:

Frosch’s objection to Mitchell is that forgiveness becomes misleading when it absorbs continued intellectual opposition: redemption of an adversary does not settle the truth of the adversary’s account [@frosch1979Composite, pp. 45–46]. The institutional distinction is correspondingly precise: a person retains standing while an assertion, practice or decision remains open to adverse judgment.

The review’s psychoanalytic claims about gender are Frosch’s disputed interpretation, not premises that this article must adopt. Reading this review gives no reading credit for Mitchell’s book.

Caroline Anjali Ritchie’s 2025 article follows the reworking of the Blake–Stedman engravings by contemporary artists. Her final section asks what authority a reproduction carries forward even when its declared purpose is critical. The useful connection is to this manuscript’s placement of historical art beside modern diagrams. Suggested insertion in “Production, design and interpretive authority”:

Ritchie’s study of the Blake–Stedman engravings makes reproduction an interpretive act: a critical caption can still carry forward an image’s accumulated authority [@ritchie2025Embodied, paras. 26–30]. Here each sheet should name its material identity and the claim for which it is shown, while the adjoining diagram identifies the article’s construction. A historical image can complicate an argument without becoming its certificate.

The article discusses different objects from the LOC sheets reproduced here. It does not establish that every Blake reproduction has the same history or effect. Its thirty paragraphs, textual notes, captions and bibliography were read; its photographs and embedded animation were not opened.
