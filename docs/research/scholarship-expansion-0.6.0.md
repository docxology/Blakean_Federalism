## 1. Making can change the possibilities being composed

Suggested location: §11, “Production, design and interpretive authority.”

Viscomi’s account of illuminated printing contests an origin story that treats Blake’s finished books as evidence of the intention that produced their technique. He reconstructs a movement from graphic experiments to integrated writing and design, rather than from already illustrated poems to a means of reproduction. His argument places invention within execution: working with a medium can generate projects that did not precede it. This also complicates an interpretation of technical independence as an original rejection of the marketplace. The eventual form of the illuminated book cannot by itself establish that political motive. [Viscomi 2012, §§II–IV, especially §IV paragraphs beginning “We know that,” “With no page designs,” and “Illuminated printing evolved”; cached text lines 41–81, 110–125.](https://branchcollective.org/?ps_articles=joseph-viscomi-blakes-invention-of-illuminated-printing-1788)

For this reconstruction, the consequence concerns the relation between enduring possibilities and historical invention. If nations merely selected from a fixed catalogue of poetic states, temporal production would contribute arrangement while the interpreter retained authority over every available meaning. Composition should instead include the possibility that making together changes what participants can perceive and articulate. A new public can reveal a relation that the initial classification did not represent. Its arrival need not be certified as the activation of an already named state before it can alter a shared decision. Endurance can concern the continued intelligibility of a possibility without making the theorist’s current vocabulary complete.

Institutional procedures should therefore admit more than correction of an incorrectly assigned label. A claimant may challenge the available labels, the division of the decision into separate questions, or the assumption that an existing body represents the relevant relation. A language dispute might become a dispute about working hours once participants can describe how interpretation is actually provided. Revision would then change the object of deliberation, rather than merely move a person between established categories. The proposed decision record should preserve that change and the reasons for it. A federation that welcomes new affiliations but cannot revise what counts as an affiliation still controls the terms of imaginative production.

## 2. A composite need not prohibit the independence of its parts

Suggested location: §3, after “Exchange makes national particularity relational.”

In his later Turner essay, Viscomi questions whether Blake’s refusal to supply detached designs in 1818 establishes an absolute commitment to the indivisibility of illuminated books. He compares that rationale with Blake’s reuse of images and with prints still available in the studio, considering artistic medium and practical choice alongside integrity. The selected discussion permits a distinction between participation in a composite work and the independent aesthetic force of an image. It does not license treating every extracted image as equivalent to its original setting. [Viscomi 2022, §§II–III, especially paragraphs beginning “Questioning Blake’s verity,” “As noted, Thomson,” and “Blake really was redirecting”; cached lines 203–249.](https://branchcollective.org/?ps_articles=joseph-viscomi-william-blakes-1818-letter-to-dawson-turner-and-later-career-as-graphic-artist)

This distinction gives composition without capture a more demanding form. A shared institution can acquire capacities that its participants do not exercise separately while those participants retain purposes that the institution does not authorize. A person’s capacity to initiate another association need not amount to a repudiation of every existing relation. Nor must a nation become politically meaningless when one of its practices travels beyond the people who first sustained it. Translation, reuse and separation can produce further relations without making the original relation dispensable. What requires justification is an asserted exclusive entitlement to determine those movements.

The analogy also exposes an asymmetry that the federation must address. Images cannot negotiate the conditions of their extraction; people can demand that institutions answer them. Political composition therefore requires powers absent from the aesthetic comparison: participants must be able to contest the account of the whole and the consequences of leaving a particular arrangement. Conversely, independence cannot mean freedom to abandon incurred obligations simply by changing one’s description. A disputed water allocation, for example, still needs rules about infrastructure and continuing costs. The non-capture principle should distinguish the preservation of those specified obligations from an institution’s claim to possess the contributor’s future activity.

## 3. Particularity is an obligation to discriminate, not to collect everything

Suggested location: §3, following the existing short paragraph on particularity.

Heppner complicates a simple opposition between Blakean particularity and visual simplification. His reading finds sparse setting and restrained facial expression in *The Woman Taken in Adultery*, while relating significance to posture, narrative and words beyond the image. In the disagreement with Reynolds, he distinguishes discrimination of character from the accumulation of incidental visual detail. The resulting question is what a feature makes intelligible, not how much information a representation contains. [Heppner 1983, pp. 55–56, 59; PDF 13–14, 17.](https://bq.blakearchive.org/17.2.heppner)

For the proposed classification procedure, particularity should therefore mean attention to the difference that bears on a decision. A longer identity record can conceal an injustice if it records language, descent and membership while overlooking who controls a claimant’s wages. Conversely, a deliberately limited record can protect someone whose reasons do not require complete disclosure. The appropriate challenge is whether the institution has represented the relevant relation and consequence, and whether the affected person can identify what its account misses. Exhaustive legibility is neither the measure nor the condition of being treated as a person.

Jerusalem 55:60–64, E205, places doing good through particulars beside the organization of art and science and the relation of infinity to determinate identity. Its organizational vocabulary matters. The alternative to a generalized good is not an administrative world without organization, but work answerable to what its organization does. The same plate’s demand for demonstrations at 55:56–59 prevents a reassuring interpretation in which every appeal to particulars is already gentle. A procedure that asks for concrete evidence can become coercive through the burden of producing it. The reconstruction must identify who has to demonstrate what, what assistance is available, and what remains protected from compulsory exposure.

## 4. Labor must change the conditions of passage

Suggested location: §2, after the reading of Jerusalem 73; connect forward to §5.

Jerusalem 12:24–44, E155–156, makes building a sustained labor of compassion while Jerusalem remains outside Los’s gate. In the central building passage, materials and joints bear ethical names: pity, affection, merciful labor, forgiveness and comforting remembrance. This is an account of relations being made durable through work, rather than a claim that good intentions automatically create a hospitable whole. The continuing distance of Jerusalem from the builders matters equally. An elaborate structure of assistance can exist while its intended participant remains beyond its practical reach. The existence of a forum or service is not evidence that an excluded person can use it.

Jerusalem 55:48–53, E205, makes the temporal dimension more explicit. The speakers converse while working at the furrow; their preference for preventing misery precedes the injunction to attend to the little ones. The contemporary extension is that supported passage must begin before a person has incurred the full cost of challenging a classification. A hearing held after eviction does not restore the lost period of security. A translation service offered only after a missed deadline leaves the original exclusion operative. The procedure should record when assistance becomes available and whether its timing prevents a predictable deprivation.

This changes the proposed outcome of the Xeno-California cases. Counting successful appeals would favor an institution that repeatedly injures people and later reverses itself. A stronger comparison would distinguish avoided injuries, effective interim provision, restoration after injury and unresolved loss. Prevention can itself become paternalistic if officials decide what participants need without hearing them. The proposed safeguard therefore joins anticipatory provision to a power of correction: the claimant can dispute the institution’s account of the threat and the proposed assistance. Material support should enlarge the capacity to act, rather than purchase acquiescence to the provider’s description of a good life.

## 5. Forgiveness must not become another test imposed on the injured person

Suggested location: §3, gender discussion, before its institutional extension.

Heppner’s interpretation leaves a productive difficulty visible: the accusers depart, but the woman remains bound. He treats her posture as combining dependency with strength and reads the unwritten words near Jesus’s finger as demanding imaginative participation. Reprieve and release are thus distinguishable within the design. [Heppner 1983, pp. 49–54; PDF 7–12; figure 1, p. 46/PDF 4.](https://bq.blakearchive.org/17.2.heppner)

A political reconstruction should make that distinction sharper. Withdrawal of an accusation does not return a job, undo a dependency or put the means of action in the accused person’s hands. Nor should release depend on gratitude toward a powerful benefactor. The institution needs to identify which constraints persist after its decision is reversed and who must remove them. Otherwise, the successful appeal becomes a display of the whole’s generosity while the person remains materially subject to it. The possibility of restored agency must include acting against the account of reconciliation that the institution prefers.

Jerusalem 61:17–23, E212, presses on the condition attached to forgiveness. Joseph recounts a dream-voice that challenges debt forgiven only upon payment and pollution forgiven only upon purity. The surrounding exchange begins with Mary asking whether Joseph’s rejection would murder her; it proceeds through embrace into Mary’s song and the vision involving Jerusalem. The passage gives no ready policy for restitution, but it makes conditional restoration a difficult claim. In the proposed federation, basic standing cannot be returned only after the claimant accepts the association’s moral judgment, reconciles with its officials or ceases to criticize them.

The converse is equally concrete. A person harmed by an office must not be required to forgive it to obtain protection. Ending an institution’s power to impose a total identity does not erase evidence, restitution or a justified restriction against further harm. Forgiveness can challenge the identification of a person with an offense while accountability addresses the act and its effects. Their separation is the institutional extension proposed here. Treating continued anger as evidence of a defective poetic state would reproduce precisely the classificatory possession the reconstruction opposes.

## 6. Nations make public images; public images distribute standing

Suggested location: §3, after the national countertexts.

Matthews reads the 1809 exhibition as an intervention in national visual culture. Her argument connects public art, the legacy of Barry and the scandal surrounding Wollstonecraft, then places Jane Shore alongside the exhibition’s national heroes. She also contests an easy inference from evangelical echoes to Blake’s agreement with evangelical moral regulation. The national question becomes whose embodiment and sexuality may appear in public, and under what judgment. [Matthews 2010, paragraphs beginning “Blake’s 1809 exhibition can be seen,” “Andrew Lincoln has argued,” “Blake’s decision to include,” and “Looking back”; notes 18–28, 42–49, 67; cached lines 263–381.](https://www.tate.org.uk/research/tate-papers/14/an-alternative-national-gallery-blakes-1809-exhibition-and-the-attack-on-evangelical-culture)

This makes national production consequential before a body exercises formal jurisdiction. A school’s common history, a civic monument or an association’s public celebration can distribute credibility: some participants appear as makers of the common world, others as its moral problem. An officer may claim no power over conscience while controlling the public setting in which a dissenter must speak. Non-exhaustion must therefore concern the means of representation as well as an express disclaimer that an organization speaks only for a purpose. The question is whether participants can challenge the exemplary figures, omissions and standards through which that purpose becomes public.

Jerusalem 3’s final prose paragraph, E146, links the flourishing of nations to poetry, painting and music. Its immediate setting concerns varied poetic measure and the fitting of different parts. That setting supports asking what a national institution enables people to make, hear and encounter. It also makes a demanding counterclaim possible: if officials believe the nation depends on the right art, they may claim the power to police art for the nation’s survival. The proposed federation should secure access to cultural production while denying any office the final authority to identify imaginative health. A critic of the national image must be able to remain a participant in the institutions that sustain its production.

## 7. A common world need not close the interpretation of its participants

Suggested location: §4, after Architecture A, and §12’s defeat conditions.

Jerusalem 98:28–40, E257–258, presents conversation as the creation of forms, exemplars, space and time. It also places the participants within an encompassing human unity. At 98:51–56, E258, the desolation produced by Albion’s taxation of nations becomes part of a cry voiced from the earth, Golgonooza and thirty-two nations. These are stronger resources for the states/nations argument when read together. National voices participate in an imaginative common world while the cost imposed on nations remains a subject of judgment. Neither unity nor the multiplication of national names settles who may exact that cost.

The counterpressure is unusually clear in Blake’s letter to Flaxman of 19 October 1801, E718: his hope that France and England become one country and their arts one accompanies enthusiasm for peace and artistic work. The manuscript should admit that Blake can imagine emancipation through a form of unification stronger than its own preferred non-fusion. The reconstruction’s distinct commitment is that shared creation does not settle the rightful scale of political decision. A common artistic undertaking can widen a world without authorizing common government; common government can be justified for a specified act without requiring its subjects to accept one imaginative identity.

## Objections to carry into revision

1. **The fixed-repertoire objection changes the thesis itself.** If enduring states mean a complete inventory known to the theorist, execution-generated invention is difficult to accommodate. Defend recurrence without completeness, or acknowledge that the new scholarship weakens the initial inversion.
2. **Mercy may retain a sovereign giver.** Heppner’s bound woman makes the issue visible but does not settle it. A release procedure should be a claimable obligation with an accountable actor; otherwise compassionate authority can preserve dependency.
3. **Prevention may expand surveillance.** Jerusalem 55’s appeal to particulars cannot justify acquiring unlimited personal information. Specify a relevant injury and the minimum evidence needed to prevent it, with a challenge to the collection itself.
4. **Public art is no transparent measure of inclusion.** Matthews offers a historical interpretation, including conjectural links, not evidence that an exhibition redistributed power. The proposed institutional extension needs authority over access and selection to be made contestable.
5. **Composite autonomy may conceal unequal bargaining.** The ability to leave or reuse a practice is distributed unequally. Assess continuing obligations and practical means rather than accepting either the whole’s integrity or a part’s independence as decisive.
