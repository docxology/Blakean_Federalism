# Scholarship expansion for *States Change*

Research-only leaf memo, 23 October 2026. This is advice for the root's manuscript synthesis, not acceptance, legal updating, publication, or a software review. Ownership is this memo and `scholarship-cache-se01-*` evidence files only. No repository or project source was changed.

The starting manuscript is version 0.1.0, headed *States Change: Blakean Federalism and composition without capture*. I inspected it, its annotated bibliography, the earlier primary-source memo, and the supplied Markdown briefs. Earlier reading claims remain historical claims of their respective researchers. The original DOCX files were not re-extracted or visually reviewed.

**Attribution convention:** **Author** identifies an argument in inspected scholarship; **Proposal** identifies Friedman's supplied eternal-STATES/temporal-NATIONS construction; **Extension** identifies an argument or candidate paragraph developed here. Candidate paragraphs are newly written prose, not quotations from scholars. No new Blake quotation is supplied: the root retains primary-text verification and copy collation.

## 1. The strongest substantive expansion

Replace the draft's compressed list of interlocutors with a sustained disagreement over three questions: what persists through change; whether national particularity enables or obstructs universal humanity; and whether collective self-government can preserve difference without acquiring control over persons. The literature supports a more demanding argument than a general endorsement of plurality.

The distinctive claim worth defending is a **discipline for criticizing the conversion of imaginative descriptions into public authority**. The proposal can retain its metaphysical ambition while admitting that its institutional safeguards have separate political-theoretical ancestors. Its strongest contribution may be a way of exposing a bad transition, rather than a new constitutional architecture.

### Permanence: Larrissy, Damrosch, and Lee are not interchangeable

**Author, Larrissy:** the publisher summary identifies substantial Neoplatonic vocabulary and a problem of the character and extent of Blake's indebtedness. It distinguishes judgments about Plato from inheritance through later Platonists. This supports retaining Platonism as a serious question; it does not establish an inventory of national essences. Chapter pp.186–198 remain unread. [Publisher summary and metadata](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/platonism-and-the-english-imagination/blake-and-platonism/D20238AA8F9A0A52635407EFBD7866E4).

**Author, Damrosch:** printed pp.15–18 discuss perception and particulars; pp.19–20 ground their relation to universality in Jesus. The universal is real in its particular manifestations, rather than a detachable archetype; this is not simply nominalism or denial of universality. Page 21 develops a comparison with Cusanus, but p.20 n.25 expressly distinguishes analogy from demonstrated influence. His argument challenges the proposal to explain how enduring forms can remain particular and embodied. It also prevents a casually secular account of Blake's metaphysics. Selected reading only, not the book or complete chapter. [Authorized distributor preview, printed pp.15–21/PDF pp.30–36](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781400853731_A23704199/preview-9781400853731_A23704199.pdf).

**Pagination correction for the root:** in these downloaded bytes, printed p.18 is one-based PDF p.33, not p.32 as the prior bibliography reports. Printed pp.18–20 are PDF pp.33–35. Direct single-page extraction confirms the footer; zero-based web page indices must not be presented as one-based PDF pagination.

**Author, Lee:** the ten-page introduction excerpt argues that preformation can protect identity against an imperial and economic appetite for indefinitely plastic life. PDF pp.2–5 establish the contrast with developmental accounts; pp.7–8, including n.18, distinguish pre-existent form from mechanically identical reproduction; pp.9–10 challenge prevailing epigenesis readings. Lee does not equate liberation with either novelty or biological mutability. The detailed arguments of her later chapters remain unread. [Publisher excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97810096/26453/excerpt/9781009626453_excerpt.pdf).

**Candidate paragraph for §§2–3 — Extension:**

The distinction between enduring states and temporal nations cannot rest on a simple opposition between fixed form and creative change. A collective can change continually while retaining an oppressive capacity to reshape its members. Conversely, an enduring claim about personhood may protect someone against compulsory adaptation. The relevant political distinction is therefore not permanence against becoming, but the conditions under which either is invoked. Does an appeal to permanence protect a person's standing or entrench an institution's mandate? Does an appeal to generation open collective possibilities or demand that people remake themselves for an authorized national future? These questions preserve the distinction between poetic states and historical national formations while refusing to decide its politics by a preference for fluidity.

The paragraph is motivated by the disagreement above. It is not Lee's theory of federalism, Damrosch's constitutional argument, or a conclusion already established by Blake.

### Situated belonging and imperial assimilation: make Fallon central

**Author, Fallon:** the complete twelve-page submitted manuscript makes national mediation indispensable while exposing its dangers. PDF pp.1–4 develop situated vision, possessive Albion, and reciprocity; pp.5–8 examine France, England, national manners, and revolutionary disappointment; pp.9–12 distinguish destructive rooting, institutional agglomeration, and regenerative growth. His conclusion resists both possessive rootedness and abstract cosmopolitanism. His regeneration remains oriented toward Divine Humanity: it does not itself vindicate permanent constitutional plurality. On p.2, Wright's assimilation objection and Clark's Protestant-imperial objection are reported as rival readings. Those cited pages were not independently read in their originals. His p.12 n.40 directly disputes Wright's structural assimilation of loving and polypous relations. Cite this disagreement rather than implying agreement among all three. [Submitted manuscript](https://sure.sunderland.ac.uk/id/eprint/6061/1/Homelands.pdf).

**Author, Clark, bounded update:** the publisher abstract is now directly retrieved. It questions an uncomplicated radical milieu and situates Blake within nationalist bourgeois mercantilism and artistic networks. This is substantive resistance to treating outsider status as established political innocence. It does not independently verify the argument on published p.171 reported by Fallon. [Chapter abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1057/9780230597068_10).

**Candidate paragraph for §3 — Extension:**

National belonging may provide a medium of memory, imagination and mutual obligation without becoming a title of possession. The relevant alternative is not a choice between a rooted nation and a placeless humanity. A homeland may sustain reciprocal relations, while a mobile network can spread extraction and domination. The federation must therefore assess the character of relations rather than celebrate either territorial attachment or circulation in itself. It should also resist making one nation's historical idiom the necessary language of universal emancipation. A collective that welcomes others only after translating them into its own final image has enlarged its reach without accepting their independence.

This remains a contemporary construction. The final sentence supplies an objection to the proposal's own universalizing ambitions as well as a possible criticism of Blake.

### Religious and gender tensions require arguments, not a disclaimer

**Author, Matthews:** the complete publisher excerpt, pp.1–10, challenges readings of late Blake as necessarily rejecting erotic life. Pages 3–6 historicize the vocabulary of sex and sexuality; pp.6–8 address gender categories and the exclusionary possibilities of ostensibly universal humanity; pp.9–10 contest a wholly subcultural account of audience. Matthews proposes a consistent defense of female sexuality, while reporting opposing feminist readings. This is a live disagreement, not proof that every hostile passage is emancipatory. Connolly, Mellor, Essick and the historical authorities she cites were not independently read. [Publisher excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805215/13579/excerpt/9780521513579_excerpt.pdf).

**Candidate paragraph for §§3–4 — Extension:**

An appeal to humanity can protect people against restrictive identities, yet can also make a dominant subject appear universal. The political reconstruction must therefore ask whose activities, bodies and voices remain visible when it speaks of brotherhood, generation or emanation. Neither historical contextualization nor a generous theological interpretation automatically resolves the interests of a woman who contests a community's authority. Religious disagreement raises the parallel problem: a person need not accept the designers' account of divine imagination to possess equal standing. The proposed guarantees must be justifiable in public institutions while the manuscript preserves, rather than erases, the theology that gives Blake's language its force.

For the root's close reading, retain the already identified Jerusalem 52 polemic, 54's eternal emanation, and 88's interchange/dominion context. The supplied passage ledger is a research lead here, not new quotation verification. A further useful lead from Matthews is the late language of humanity and sexual organization; verify complete passages and speakers before insertion.

## 2. A deeper comparison of the four political texts

### Nicolaïdis: joint government needs more than preserved plurality

**Author:** the complete article's main text, author-PDF pp.1–16, differentiates a normative benchmark from observed EU practices. Pages 7–8 explicitly bound its method geographically and warn against universal export. Pages 8–10 make non-domination and mutual recognition jointly demanding; pp.11–12 identify credentialism and integration justified by destiny as pathologies. Principles 1–10, pp.12–15, address autonomy, safeguards, voting, transnational rights, compatibility, mediation, empowerment, accountability, citizenship and diversity. She confronts the conflict between equality among citizens and among peoples, rather than solving it through plural vocabulary. [Author PDF](https://kalypsonicolaidis.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Demoicracy-jcms-March-2013-1.pdf).

**Candidate paragraph for §6 — Extension:**

Demoicracy supplies a demanding comparator because the persistence of several peoples is compatible with their subordination. The proposed federation must identify what its participants owe one another when decisions create consequences beyond their own constituencies. Mutual recognition cannot mean that each authority certifies the others' power over dissenters. Nor does refusing fusion settle how votes, vetoes and public burdens are distributed. A defensible arrangement needs rules for common action and for challenging its exercise. Extending this problem from state-based peoples to overlapping national formations is this project's task; it cannot inherit a solution merely by borrowing the European vocabulary.

### Bauböck: the central objection is the production of political boundaries

**Author:** the December 2001 paper distinguishes cultural liberties, public recognition and political autonomy, PDF pp.3–7. Its comparison covers intergroup relations, intragroup relations and group–state relations. Pages 13–14 examine majority advantage and unequal resources; pp.18–19 contrast internal citizenship protection with external national protection; pp.21–23 explain why politically consequential affiliation entails registers, limited options and exclusion of multiple identification. Pages 24–26 resist a clean separation of cultural and general governmental interests. His conclusion, pp.33–34, prefers territorial autonomy generally while allowing contextual exceptions. This is a stronger objection than administrative inconvenience. [Institutional PDF, §§2–8](https://eif.univie.ac.at/downloads/workingpapers/IWE-Papers/WP22.pdf).

**Candidate paragraph for §§4–6 — Extension:**

A many-to-many account of belonging must survive its translation into taxation, representation and enforceable duties. If every cultural affiliation carries a complete political franchise, multiple membership can duplicate influence; if one affiliation must be selected, the theory's plurality disappears at the point of decision. The answer must distinguish participation in collective life from authorization for particular public acts. One possible design preserves equal general representation while allowing several limited associations, but its scope and safeguards require independent justification. It must also explain how an unrecognized or unaffiliated person receives services and a remedy. Describing a register as revisable does not establish that registration is voluntary.

Do not overread Bauböck's claim against broadly overlapping political jurisdictions as a theorem against every limited overlap. Equally, task-specific overlap does not refute his argument about nationality corporations exercising public powers. These institutional objects must be compared at matching levels.

### Ostrom: a framework is not a validated constitutional model

**Author:** complete main-text reading, printed pp.641–665. Pages 645–648 distinguish a resource's properties, institutional arrangements and the Institutional Analysis and Development framework; p.651 treats property as a bundle of different rights; p.652 differentiates seven rule types. Pages 652–653 explain design principles as regularities, not a universal list of successful specific rules. The experimental and field sections, pp.654–658, and contextual account, pp.659–663, condition outcomes on settings. Pages 664–665 reject single prescriptions and retain a role for governments. The reported empirical studies were read as her evidence, not replicated. [Institution-hosted article](https://web.pdx.edu/~nwallace/EHP/OstromPolyGov.pdf).

**Candidate bridge for §5 — Extension:**

The classification record can be extended from provenance to an account of a decision situation. For each contested action, identify who occupies which position, who can enter or leave it, what information is available, which actions are permitted, how decisions are aggregated, and how benefits and costs are assigned. Then identify who may revise these rules. A national label supplies none of these answers automatically. The resulting description makes a proposal testable against specified objections; it does not show that the rules are followed or that the outcome is legitimate. The present executable checks should consequently be presented as a small stipulated model inside a wider framework of inquiry.

### Hooghe and Marks: nonintersection of membership is not exclusivity of competence

**Author:** complete main text, printed pp.233–241. Table 2, p.236, distinguishes general-purpose Type I and task-specific Type II jurisdictions. Page 237 n.6 explicitly permits overlapping competencies within Type I despite nonintersecting memberships. Page 238 locates many Type II bodies within Type I legal settings and notes variations in franchise. Pages 239–240 explain their different coordination strategies; p.240 contrasts encompassing community with functional participation. Page 241 leaves democratic effects as research questions. Therefore the typology neither makes Type II inherently democratic nor turns nations into functional service bodies. [Author-hosted article](https://garymarks.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13018/2016/09/hooghe.marks_.unravelingcentralstate.apsr_.2003.pdf).

**Extension for §7:** compare membership boundaries, competence overlap, fiscal responsibility and review separately. A stable general jurisdiction could share a water competence without dissolving its citizenship. A task-specific body could exclude affected residents through its franchise. These are hypothetical contrasts to specify, not conclusions about present California institutions.

| Question | Most useful inspected comparator | Remaining burden for the proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What warrants joint rule among peoples? | Nicolaïdis | Defend the constituencies and decision rule |
| Does national autonomy harden identity? | Bauböck | Separate affiliation, public powers and general standing |
| What rules and setting affect cooperation? | Ostrom | Supply evidence about behavior and material outcomes |
| How are jurisdictions composed? | Hooghe–Marks | Account for coordination, franchise and responsibility |

This comparison belongs in the article's argument, not only its bibliography. No source establishes that a Blakean design outperforms the alternatives.

## 3. Treaty relationships, refusal and non-domination

**Author, Henderson 2019:** selected printed pp.17–21 and 35–41/PDF pp.1–5 and 19–25. He argues that treaty federalism is foundational rather than a cultural-minority concession. Pages 35–36 connect territorial jurisdiction to institutional reform; pp.36–39 propose treaty representation; pp.40–41 insist on each Treaty Nation's legitimate consent and specify structural and fiscal changes. His assertion on p.39 that electoral reform need not require constitutional amendment is his constitutional argument, not a new legal conclusion here. The middle historical/doctrinal sections were not fully read, and cited cases were not updated. [Journal PDF](https://www.constitutionalstudies.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/02_Henderson-4.pdf).

The earlier **1994** *Empowering Treaty Federalism* remains metadata/table-of-contents only: direct CanLII PDF and commentary retrievals returned 403. University metadata gives *Saskatchewan Law Review* 58:241–329. Its sections on shared jurisdiction, territorial management and treaty delegations are promising next targets, not read passages. [Institutional record](https://iportal.usask.ca/record/14757).

**Primary instrument, UNDRIP:** the complete English declaration and preamble were read in the UN's March 2008 printing of the 2007 resolution. Articles 3–5, printed pp.4–5, address self-determination and institutions; 18–19, p.8, participation and consultation toward consent; 25–28, p.10, land and redress; 32–35, p.12, projects, membership and institutions; 36–37, p.13, cross-border relations and treaties. Articles 44 and 46, pp.14–15, preserve sex equality, human-rights constraints and territorial-integrity language. These provisions must be read together. No universal veto, secession entitlement, or domestic enforceability determination is inferred. [Official UN text](https://www.un.org/esa/socdev/unpfii/documents/DRIPS_en.pdf).

**Simpson, reading ceiling:** Duke's synopsis presents refusal of settler citizenship and sovereign orders under tension, grounded in Kahnawà:ke. Contents locate substantive next reading at chapter 1, pp.1–36; chapter 4, pp.95–114; and chapter 6, pp.147–176. These are contents-derived targets, not independently read pages. No authorized excerpt was recovered; chapter claims must remain at publisher-synopsis level. Unverified third-party full-book scans were not used as evidence. [Publisher](https://www.dukeupress.edu/mohawk-interruptus).

**Candidate paragraph for §§6–7 — Extension:**

The federation cannot begin by granting recognition to every people as though all political relations originated in its permission. A treaty relationship may concern the source and limits of the federation's own authority. Refusal may likewise challenge the offered terms of incorporation rather than request another category within them. Xeno-California must therefore distinguish an ordinary cultural association from a people asserting a historically specific political relationship. It must ask who authorized the negotiation, which institutions can speak for the parties, which obligations precede the proposed arrangement, and whether nonparticipation is possible. Neither a consent field nor an imaginative map supplies evidence of an actual agreement.

**Pettit, bounded primary comparator:** the publisher abstract of *Republicanism*, chapter 2, pp.51–79, distinguishes arbitrary capacity to interfere from actual interference. Chapter full text was not obtained. The author-site record verifies the 2008 chapter *Republican Liberty: Three Axioms, Four Theorems*, pp.102–130, but its PDF was unreadable through the web extraction and direct retrieval returned 403; no full-chapter reading is claimed. [Oxford abstract](https://academic.oup.com/book/3937/chapter-abstract/145530399?login=false); [author record](https://ppettit.scholar.princeton.edu/publications/republican-liberty-three-axioms-four-theorems).

**Extension:** add a hypothetical quiet-domination case: an association never sanctions dissent because everyone knows it can remove housing or work. Zero observed punishment would not establish freedom. Conversely, a bounded, challengeable public duty need not constitute capture simply because it interferes. This sharpens the draft's distinction between necessary authority and totalizing identification without claiming a complete republican derivation.

## 4. Objections that should survive revision

1. **Universal humanity may impose assimilation.** The proposal must explain how reciprocal translation preserves another people's authority to contest the universal vocabulary itself. A plural surface with one compulsory final metaphysics is insufficient.
2. **Persistent identity may become official identity policing.** Protection against compulsory plasticity must not authorize officers to declare a person's true eternal self. The person/state distinction should constrain the interpreter too.
3. **Fiscal autonomy can purchase segregation.** A wealthy association may acquire facilities while poorer people retain nominal membership rights. Hold resources constant across alternatives and identify obligations of equalization.
4. **An affected-person rule can collide with self-determination.** Do not settle treaty-specific authority simply by counting every affected individual, or settle individual protection by counting only authorized collective delegates. State the conflict and the institutional question.
5. **The translation obligation can itself dominate.** Who sets its language, deadlines, admissible evidence and costs? Accessibility must be reciprocal and resourced; some knowledge or relationship may legitimately resist disclosure.
6. **The account may add no institution.** Compare a version without poetic descriptors. If every safeguard remains intact, claim interpretive or critical value while withdrawing institutional novelty.

These are proposed defeat conditions, not empirical findings. A successful revision should answer them through an explicit conflict and plausible alternatives rather than declaring the vocabulary inherently emancipatory.
