# Concordance, labor and the scale of political form

Interpretation and interface suggestions are this leaf’s arguments; the historical arguments remain attributed. Two new scholarly originals were read: Moyer’s journal article and Mitchell’s seminar discussion paper. Neither appears in the cumulative v0.7.0 reading ledger. ## Proposed manuscript paragraphs

Corpus-wide search can test whether the state/person distinction organizes relations beyond the passages initially selected to establish it. A useful comparison follows what people do within a combination: who supplies food, who works, who receives its products, and who names the resulting whole. These relations may recur without the words *state*, *individual* or *liberty*. Retrieval should therefore bring the conceptual vocabulary beside work, provision, compulsion and ownership. The contribution is a set of inspectable contrasts through which an interpretation can be challenged. A larger concordance becomes politically useful when it enables that comparison, rather than merely enlarging the list of apparently supportive words. [Blake, *Jerusalem* 59:26–55, E209; 16:16–34, E160.](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html)

Moyer argues that Blake’s textile imagery connects metropolitan workers and plantation slaves within an Atlantic division of labor. His reading joins *Visions*, *America*, *The Four Zoas*, *Milton* and *Jerusalem* while preserving the violence specifically represented by Bromion. This suggests a comparison of relationships across source families: who compels work, who possesses bodies or products, and whose suffering becomes audible. A concordance of weaving, buying, employment, chains and daughters could locate candidate passages, including passages without explicit national vocabulary. Their juxtaposition would test the interpretation of a common economic relation; shared vocabulary would not establish it. [Moyer, ¶¶3–10, 14–16, 33.](https://bq.blakearchive.org/48.3.moyer)

*Jerusalem* 59 makes the comparison difficult within one scene. Its daughters work through hunger and interrupted sleep, yet experience delight in that work and continue repairing their wheels. The same sequence names life, love, pity and compassion. A search that collects these affirmative words as evidence of emancipatory association would miss the dependence they accompany. Read against the provision at Los’s porches in plate 73, the question becomes whether assistance changes the recipient’s capacities or sustains an exhausting arrangement. The institutional extension concerns who can interrupt the work and redirect its benefits. Participation, attachment and continuing activity cannot by themselves certify freedom. [Blake, *Jerusalem* 59:26–55, E209; 73:44–54, E229.](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html)

National generation likewise needs comparison with the distribution of labor. Plate 16 sends Scottish sons to furnaces and Welsh daughters to looms, supplies nursing mothers through England, and proceeds to counties, gates and protection. This is a national geography organized through differentiated activities. It does not establish that each named people has an equal voice in deciding those activities. Search results for national names should consequently be read alongside verbs of giving, fixing, governing and laboring. The reconstruction’s promise to preserve national productions becomes more demanding when it asks which participants can revise the functions assigned to them within the common world. [Blake, *Jerusalem* 16:22–34, 59–69, E160–161.](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html)

Moyer’s interpretation also places an uncomfortable commercial object beside the prophetic critique: Blake’s Moore & Co. advertisement. Its display of textile production and national grandeur differs from the exhausting work described in *Jerusalem*. The comparison makes genre and commission material to corpus search. A commercial inscription and a prophetic speech can belong to the same maker without expressing the same position. For the political reconstruction, this suggests examining who commissions a collective image, whom it addresses, and whose work supplies its credibility. Moyer’s conjecture about Blake’s humiliation remains conjectural; the juxtaposed objects do not establish the artist’s private motive. [Moyer, ¶¶18–24 and illustration 2.](https://bq.blakearchive.org/48.3.moyer)

*A Vision of the Last Judgment* supplies a direct pressure on the proposed division between eternal STATES and temporal NATIONS. After distinguishing Moses and Abraham as persons from the states signified by their names, Blake describes those states as appearing differently with distance: one man becomes multitudes of nations as the observer approaches. Nations here participate in visionary representation, rather than simply occupying the temporal side of a fixed opposition. The manuscript should acknowledge this countertext explicitly. It can defend a political reconstruction that distinguishes historical formations from persons without presenting that distinction as Blake’s exhaustive lexical taxonomy. [Blake, notebook p. 76, second passage, E556–557.](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/descriptions_of_last_judgment.html)

Mitchell asks whether the pictures themselves communicate the exemption of individuals from damnation that an interpreter derives from Blake’s writings. His concluding interpretation is offered as a hypothesis, while his diagrams leave geometric meaning open. For the present project, that tension proposes a visual discipline: an overview of the whole should remain answerable to identifiable people, passages and rival readings. A diagram can preserve separate nodes yet imply a settled harmony through its layout. The caption should specify whether a line records a quotation, a lexical association, an affiliation or a proposed political relation. Inspection of a graph cannot supply the warrant that its arrows merely display. [Mitchell, §§II–IV, PDF pp. 8–11.](https://bq.blakearchive.org/pdfs/issues/9.2b.pdf)

The Reynolds annotations provide a complementary test of particularity. Blake’s attack on generalization answers a prefatory passage praising abstraction and classification; the electronic edition distinguishes the quoted host text from his response. Searching the entire page without that distinction can attribute the opposed position to Blake or count the dispute as repeated agreement. Comparison with *Jerusalem* 55 should follow the different work of the claims: polemical annotation, organized assistance and coercive demands for demonstration. The final maxim on unequal creatures in *Marriage* 24 adds another problem: differentiated treatment may answer relevant differences, but its animal contrast supplies no rule for deciding which human classifications deserve different jurisdictions. [Blake, annotations, E641](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/marginalia.html); [*Jerusalem* 55:48–65, E205](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html); [*Marriage* 24, E44.](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/marriage_of_heaven_and_hell.html)

## Three objections to frequency inference

1. **Copies, refrains and arguments have different units.** The existing Urizen audit already establishes that its state matches repeat one refrain across retained copies. A higher normalized rate therefore cannot establish a more developed state/person theory. Compare the refrain’s successive bodily episodes with the explicit instruction in *Milton*, retaining both copy counts and distinct textual locations. A family-level plot should distinguish an absent term from a retained family whose available witnesses repeat one passage. [Existing context audit](urizen-context-0.7.0.md); [manuscript comparison](../manuscript/states-change.md).

2. **Grouped vocabulary can conceal the contested distinction.** The snapshot’s persons group contains individual/identity variants but omits *person*, *persons* and *individuality*; its nations group includes *people*. The Last Judgment passage makes those omissions consequential. Display the declared group and its constituent spellings, then inspect whether each hit names a person, a representation, a condition or a collective. A conceptual absence cannot be inferred from an unsearched variant. Conversely, adding variants changes the question and must remain visible beside the original result. [Existing query account](corpus-statistics-0.6.0.md); [Blake, E556–557.](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/descriptions_of_last_judgment.html)

3. **Presence in the same family does not identify the relation.** Love and labor can describe dependence; national imagery can advertise industry or expose its costs; a named person can represent a state. A co-presence matrix cannot distinguish those relations. Pair the matrix with passage locations, speaker or textual role where established, and a short account of the action. Compare supportive and resistant contexts within the same family before increasing the number of families. This makes concordancing contribute a reproducible route to disputed evidence. [Blake, *Jerusalem* 59:26–55](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html); [Reynolds annotations, E641.](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/marginalia.html)
