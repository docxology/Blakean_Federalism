# Blakean Federalism: primary-source research and objections

Research memo for the private Blakean_Federalism program. Observation date: **23 October 2026**. Research-only contribution; no project sources, corpus, other agents' files, publication records, or repositories were changed. This memo and `research-cache-rp01-*` files are this researcher's outputs.

## Findings that can carry into the synthesis

1. **Entomological Law supports a discipline of separating entities, legal roles, institutions, and consequences.** Its §§2 and 11–13 do not themselves supply a human constitutional theory. A record separating an institution's *assertion* of authority from its actual authorization would be a useful new extension, rather than an existing schema attributable to Friedman.
2. **Issuing jurisdiction is expressly distinct from reach and enforcement.** The decisive sentence belongs to the Figure 13 caption on printed p.28/PDF p.30, within the material associated with §12.2. A federalism diagram should preserve this distinction rather than infer practical sovereignty from the location of an issuing institution.
3. **The cultural-precision counter-model belongs to Shagor Rahman.** His contributor profile in the 2025 Institute report, pp.124–130, proposes broader cultural inference and predicts increased conformity. It supports asking whether wider coordination can intensify capture. It does not establish that outcome empirically, or establish any equivalence between social scale and Blake's kinds of vision.
4. **The political-theory comparators are different projects.** Nicolaïdis supplies plural democratic peoples and transnational non-domination; Bauböck exposes the coercion of politically consequential nationality registration; Ostrom investigates institutional performance; Hooghe and Marks distinguish jurisdictional architectures; Kymlicka differentiates minority-rights claims; Shachar targets vulnerability within accommodated groups. Their disagreements are part of the research problem.
5. **Sabine supplies a candidate temporal framework with explicit limits.** The April letter's literary and mathematical correspondences should remain attributed proposals. The current open phase-gating manuscript qualifies an earlier equation-identity claim and restricts its demonstrated setting. Neither text validates a constitutional cycle, a beneficial rupture, an optimal political precision ratio, or a political rights rule.

The strongest defensible contribution is therefore a **research grammar for identifying capture and testing institutional alternatives**. The examined sources do not yet establish that the proposed institutional arrangement is original, workable, or superior to existing pluralist and federal approaches.

## 3. Entomological Law: the useful distinction and its transfer limit

**Identity verified:** Daniel Ari Friedman, *Entomological Law: A Field Map of Insects as Evidence, Threat, Property, Product, Patient, and Weapon*, Zenodo version **1.0.0**, publication date **2 July 2026**, DOI [10.5281/zenodo.21137277](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21137277). The deposit classifies the item as a journal article; that resource-type label alone does not verify a journal venue or peer-review event. [Author's extracted text](https://danielarifriedman.com/papers/2026_EntomologicalLaw/full_text.md); [record API](https://zenodo.org/api/records/21137277).

### What §§2, 11, and 13 actually support

Section 2 organizes the field through an organism's legal role in a particular dispute rather than through a single master doctrine. Section 11 explains recurring translations among roles; its final discussion on printed p.26/PDF p.28 separates the role, the institution authorized to notice it, and the resulting consequence. The Figure 11 caption says registry counts do not weight legal importance. Section 13 rejects the inference that a coherent field needs one insect code. These are explicit descriptive and methodological claims in Friedman's text. [§§2, 11–13](https://danielarifriedman.com/papers/2026_EntomologicalLaw/full_text.md).

**Brief's extension:** a person, a poetic state, a temporal national affiliation, and a jurisdiction should not be collapsed into a single category. **Memo's extension:** make a classification-and-authority record in which the following questions are independently answerable:

| Field | Question the record should answer |
| --- | --- |
| Entity and context | Which person, collective, organization, action, or relationship is being considered, at what time? |
| Assigned role | Which legal, cultural, administrative, or interpretive role is being asserted? Can several roles coexist? |
| Asserting institution | Who makes the classification or competence claim? Who selected that institution? |
| Authorization and warrant | What source authorizes this institution, for this issue and affected population? What evidence supports its factual premise? |
| Consequence | What specific right, duty, sanction, exclusion, benefit, or resource allocation follows? |
| Contest and revision | Who has standing to challenge the role, authority, evidence, or remedy? What changes if the challenge succeeds? |

The **asserting/authorized** distinction is deliberate: accurately recording that a body claims authority does not validate the claim. Conversely, a legitimate public obligation does not disappear because a person rejects an identity classification. A record may expose a bad inference without deciding a constitutional dispute.

This proposed record is not an already implemented schema in Friedman's paper. Its human-rights commitments are independently introduced normative premises. Humans are not being equated with regulated organisms, human dignity is not made contingent on administrative recognition, and eternal Blakean states are not redescribed as merely temporary legal roles. The transferable insight is a method for inspecting institutional translations.

### Issuance, competence, reach, and enforcement

The **exact explicit issuer/reach/enforcement caveat is in Figure 13**, printed p.28/PDF p.30. Section 12.2 begins on printed p.27/PDF p.29. The caption partitions registered instruments by issuing jurisdiction, while expressly withholding any inference about their reach or enforcement. This is better cited as **§12.2 and Figure 13 caption**, rather than locating every word in the subsection's main prose. [Primary text, PDF-page markers 29–30](https://danielarifriedman.com/papers/2026_EntomologicalLaw/full_text.md).

**Memo's extension:** a federalism map should record separately (a) issuer and institutional level, (b) legal source and competence, (c) affected persons, territory, and subject matter, (d) practical implementation or enforcement capacity, and (e) appeal and review. An agreement issued centrally may have narrowly specified application; a local body may produce consequences outside its borders. These are hypothetical design possibilities, not legal findings about any actual instrument in Friedman's registry.

Section 12.2 also distinguishes offline checks of structure and attribution from live correspondence with a source. Even live quotation correspondence does not resolve legal interpretation or continuing validity. This pass did not run the paper's validation gates or revalidate its individual statutes and cases. Registry density, software closure, valid citations, and practical political legitimacy remain different matters.

**Failure case for the program:** a nation is recognized culturally; an organization then claims jurisdiction over all people classified as its members; a diagram counts that organization as an equal sovereign node; the count is taken to justify coercion. Every arrow in that sequence needs an independent warrant. A well-formed graph alone cannot supply one.

## 4. Rahman's profile: cultural expansion can be a capture mechanism

**Identity verified:** *The Active Inference Institute & Active Inference Ecosystem*, **v3**, dated **18 December 2025**, DOI [10.5281/zenodo.17982447](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17982447). The collective deposit has many creators. **Shagor Rahman is explicitly identified on p.124 as the contributor to “Myth of Objectivity.”** The relevant profile runs through p.130. A report-level citation to Institute et al. should be accompanied by Rahman's name when discussing this proposal. It should not become “Friedman's cultural-precision theory.” [Extracted report](https://danielarifriedman.com/papers/2025_AII_v3/full_text.md); [deposit metadata](https://zenodo.org/api/records/17982447).

**Source claim:** pp.125–126 propose hierarchical social models, with higher cultural levels influencing the precision of subordinate inference. Page 129 predicts developmental increases in cultural precision alongside conformity and symbolic capacity, and describes prospective empirical and AI research. These are a contributor's theoretical proposal and predictions. They are not validated findings established by the profile. Its Blake verse is on p.124. The image-based equations and underlying linked research were not inspected here. [Rahman profile, pp.124–130](https://danielarifriedman.com/papers/2025_AII_v3/full_text.md).

**Memo's inference:** the same increase in the scale of a shared model could support coordination, expose local prejudice, or constrain dissent through a dominant cultural prior. Thus a movement from nation to federation has no warranted monotonic relationship with freedom, epistemic openness, or fourfold vision. This is a *counter-model for research*, rather than a conclusion Rahman draws about federalism or a result this memo measures.

A useful future comparison would hold the coordination problem constant while varying who can revise shared expectations, who controls communication, how costly disagreement is, and whether dissent affects services or legal standing. Measure coordination success and dissent protection separately. High agreement with suppressed challenge should count differently from agreement produced by publicly revisable reasons. No single latent variable called cultural breadth should encode both coordination and emancipation by definition.

The profile's claims about existing AI architectures, human development, and culturally aligned AGI should remain explicitly attributed and speculative unless separately tested. This pass does not supply those tests.

## 5. Political theory: borrow distinct mechanisms, retain distinct objections

### Nicolaïdis: plural peoples are not a larger single people

Kalypso Nicolaïdis, **“European Demoicracy and Its Crisis,”** *JCMS* **51(2)** (2013): **351–369**, DOI [10.1111/jcms.12006](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcms.12006). Crossref gives online publication **13 December 2012** and print issue **March 2013**; the author-hosted PDF uses 2012 copyright and local pp.1–19. Cite the final issue as 2013 and identify local PDF pages when using them. [Author PDF](https://kalypsonicolaidis.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Demoicracy-jcms-March-2013-1.pdf); [metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/jcms.12006).

**Source claim:** the EU can be understood and evaluated through multiple peoples acting jointly without becoming a single demos. The argument combines transnational non-domination and mutual recognition. Its principles include autonomy, safeguards against asymmetry, plural decision arrangements, mediation, and co-citizenship. The author separates a normative benchmark from an adequate description of actual EU practice. [Opening and principles, author-PDF pp.1–5, 12–15](https://kalypsonicolaidis.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Demoicracy-jcms-March-2013-1.pdf).

**Research implication:** this is a close political comparator to composition without capture. It is not a demonstrated framework for arbitrarily overlapping intra-state nations or an endorsement of every federation. Nicolaïdis explicitly differentiates demoicracy from both national and supranational single-demos projects. Extending her argument requires specifying constituent peoples and common authority. Her pluralities principle also creates a real question for the brief's equal-person commitments: preventing domination among peoples can qualify simple population aggregation. The program must defend its weighting rules rather than promise that every kind of equality automatically coincides.

### Bauböck: choosing a nationality can still be compulsory classification

Rainer Bauböck, **“Territorial or Cultural Autonomy for National Minorities?”** **IWE Working Paper 22, December 2001**. The opening note identifies a substantially rewritten version of Working Paper 15, March 2001. Do not substitute the March paper or a similarly titled *Multinational Federalism* publication for this document. [Institutional PDF](https://eif.univie.ac.at/downloads/workingpapers/IWE-Papers/WP22.pdf).

**Source claim:** in a fully developed cultural-autonomy scheme with public powers, a nationality register can impose a limited choice, fail mixed or unlisted affiliations, require identifiable rather than anonymous membership, and create problems of duplicated tax and representation entitlements if a person is counted twice. Bauböck contrasts this with civil associations' more open, multiple, potentially private memberships. He does not claim territorial arrangements lack coercive boundaries. [Especially PDF pp.21–23](https://eif.univie.ac.at/downloads/workingpapers/IWE-Papers/WP22.pdf).

**Research implication:** distinguish freedom to select an authorized box from freedom not to be politically classified by that system. Include mixed, changing, new, and no affiliation. Separate cultural affiliation, ordinary franchise, service entitlement, tax obligation, and any special-representation rule. The warning concerns a particular construction of public membership; it is not a theorem that every overlapping functional authority or shared legal jurisdiction is impossible.

That qualification matters when reading Bauböck alongside Shachar and Hooghe/Marks: they are not all using overlap to mean the same legal relationship. A program that treats them as unanimous support would conceal the very boundary problem it needs to solve.

### Ostrom and Hooghe/Marks: governance scale is not national ontology

Elinor Ostrom, **“Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems,”** *American Economic Review* **100(3)** (2010): **641–672**, DOI [10.1257/aer.100.3.641](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.100.3.641). The article identifies itself as a revised Nobel lecture. [Institution-hosted article](https://web.pdx.edu/~nwallace/EHP/OstromPolyGov.pdf); [publisher metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1257/aer.100.3.641).

**Source claim:** multiple formally independent centers may form an interdependent governance system; whether and how they do so is empirical. Her discussion links durable resource governance with boundaries, participation, monitoring, sanctions, accessible conflict resolution, recognition of local organization, and nesting. The conclusion asks how diverse arrangements help or hinder performance. [pp.643, 652–653, 664–665](https://web.pdx.edu/~nwallace/EHP/OstromPolyGov.pdf).

**Research implication:** non-capture cannot simply mean absent boundaries or absent enforcement. A resource regime may need both while remaining contestable. Nor does decentralization alone guarantee freedom: local control can coexist with concentrated property, gatekeeping, or burdens imposed on nonusers. Those are institutional hypotheses to investigate, not findings about the particular cases this memo has not reviewed.

Liesbet Hooghe and Gary Marks, **“Unraveling the Central State, but How? Types of Multi-level Governance,”** *American Political Science Review* **97(2)** (2003): **233–243**, DOI [10.1017/S0003055403000649](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055403000649). The PDF verifies authorship order and pagination; the retrieved Crossref record has unusual inverted author-name fields and omits pagination. Do not copy those API defects into the citation. [Author-hosted article](https://garymarks.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13018/2016/09/hooghe.marks_.unravelingcentralstate.apsr_.2003.pdf).

**Source claim:** Table 2 distinguishes Type I general-purpose, nonintersecting, limited-level, systemwide structures from Type II task-specific, intersecting, flexible structures. Their discussion treats the types as complementary responses to coordination problems. Greater scale flexibility carries coordination costs; policy decomposition assumes manageable interactions and spillovers. The transit example is historical. [pp.233, 236, 239–240](https://garymarks.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13018/2016/09/hooghe.marks_.unravelingcentralstate.apsr_.2003.pdf).

**Research implication:** a cultural nation is not automatically a Type II authority, and a jurisdiction can bundle functions without expressing an eternal national essence. The proposed federation should identify which functions need stable common guarantees and which permit problem-specific variation. Water, housing, migration, labor, and revenue may resist convenient separation. The cited article provides no audit of 2026 Bay Area institutions.

This memo did not execute the simulations, authenticate the reported numerical outputs, or inspect the cited neuroscience.

**Memo's political extension:** ask how institutions can revise accumulated exclusions while preserving people's rights, relationships, and material security. Compare amendment, negotiated reform, redistribution, abolition of an oppressive institution, and replacement that recreates the same concentration of power. Do not assume coherence is just, rupture liberating, or regeneration restorative. Rights and justice must enter as independently defended evaluation criteria. There is no licensed transition from a model's threshold crossing to the necessity or legitimacy of political violence.

## 7. Substantive objections to composition without capture

The following are this memo's objections and research tests. Sources motivate several problems; they do not themselves endorse the proposed tests or the program.

| Objection | Hard case or defeat condition | What a serious response would need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The interpreter captures the ontology.** Whoever defines eternal states can become an unaccountable constitutional gatekeeper. | An official classification of a community as limited in vision reduces its resources, voice, or eligibility for office. | No civic rank inferred from poetic descriptors; rival interpretations remain possible; authority is warranted independently of metaphysics. |
| **Recognition manufactures the monopoly it promises to avoid.** A community and its authorized spokesperson are not identical. | Funding or seats go to one organization claiming every member, while internal opposition has no standing. | Evidence of mandate, contested representation, transparent finances, and direct routes for dissidents and unrepresented residents. |
| **Voluntary affiliation masks a compulsory register.** Bauböck's problem survives a fluid vocabulary. | A mixed person must choose a single nation to obtain schooling, a vote, tax status, or a remedy. | Explicit separation of affiliation, services, tax, franchise, and representation; provision for no affiliation and new groups. |
| **Rights between peoples conflict with equality among persons.** Equal seats for collective units can entrench unequal individual influence. | A tiny association gains the same general veto as a large population, or a population majority permanently overrides a smaller people. | A defended allocation of decisions and voting weights, with accessible rights review; no claim that one arithmetic solves all conflicts. |
| **Exit is purchased by the wealthy.** Formal mobility can conceal dependency. | A dissident can legally leave but loses housing, livelihood, family access, language services, or the practical ability to contest a decision. | Measured exit costs, portable basic rights, legal assistance and voice within institutions; appropriate protection for dependents. |
| **Polycentricity diffuses responsibility.** Overlap can create blame shifting rather than restraint. | Every authority denies responsibility for a shared water or housing harm, or a claimant cannot afford repeated proceedings. | A designated duty bearer, time limits, funded remedies, conflict rules, and an account of residual risk. |
| **Functional decomposition hides distribution.** The solution to an engineering problem can be a loss of democratic control. | A wealthy service jurisdiction excludes burdens while downstream residents or migrant workers lack standing. | Account of fiscal equalization, affected nonmembers, labor and land history, and spillovers that cannot be cleanly separated. |
| **Cultural breadth becomes conformity.** Rahman's proposal supplies a possible mechanism rather than a freedom guarantee. | A federation achieves agreement by increasing the penalty for challenging the dominant prior. | Independent measures of coordination, revision, disagreement costs, and dissenters' material outcomes. |
| **Plural dialogue presupposes inclusion.** A people may reject incorporation itself. | The federation recognizes a prior people only on terms set by the federation, converting refusal into another administered identity. | Consent and treaty-specific historical inquiry; a defensible account of nonparticipation. The Indigenous scholarship flagged by the scope needs further primary reading before doctrinal claims. |
| **Regeneration rewards endurance of oppression.** A coherence measure may favor a long-lived unjust order. | A model scores preservation of an oppressive arrangement above abolition because persistence is built into its objective. | Independent rights and justice constraints, explicit alternatives, and negative cases in which continuity should lose. |
| **Composition becomes an unfalsifiable virtue word.** Every outcome can be relabeled creative relation. | The proposed system does no better than an existing constitutional-pluralist or multinational baseline, yet claims success from Blakean terminology. | A named institutional difference, a comparator, and advance failure criteria. A result of no added institutional value must remain admissible. |

These objections also distinguish **necessary institutional authority** from **capture**. A public decision can bind dissenters without claiming their whole identity. The question is whether the authority is bounded, publicly warranted, accountable, and contestable, and whether the imposed burden is defensible. If all binding decisions count as capture, the proposal cannot explain how a federation governs. If only total psychological absorption counts, it will overlook ordinary domination through land, employment, administrative barriers, and resource control.

## 8. Suggested next research unit for the root synthesis

Develop one fully specified conflict rather than infer a constitution from an ontology. The scope's water/land/resource case is suitable: include territorial residents, a national-cultural association, an Indigenous people with historically specific claims, migrant workers, service providers, and downstream affected nonmembers. These are hypothetical roles until backed by actual case evidence.

For each competing arrangement, state who issues decisions, the source and scope of competence, who participates, who funds implementation, who enforces, who can challenge, and who bears unresolved losses. Compare at least an ordinary territorial arrangement, a differentiated multinational arrangement, and a hybrid with a task-specific authority. Hold basic rights and material facts explicit rather than defining the hybrid as freer by construction.

The proposed classification-and-authority record could improve transparency if it exposes unjustified transitions between cultural recognition and coercive competence. It would fail if it merely adds provenance fields to decisions whose affected people cannot contest them. This is a tractable proposed contribution, not a report that such a system already exists or works.

Before manuscript-level claims:

1. Read Kymlicka's actual chapters and Shachar's detailed allocation rules through authorized access; retain the present abstract/excerpt boundaries until then.
2. Closely read Nicolaïdis and Bauböck beyond the selected passages, preserving their disagreement with simple transposition to fluid national affiliations.
3. Ask what the proposed institutions change relative to established theories. Avoid claiming to have discovered multinational federalism, overlapping governance, or vulnerability within groups.
4. Keep the underlying Blake textual and copy-specific work with the corpus owner. The scope's passage ledger, mythic distinctions, edition issues, and nationalism/empire countertexts remain research tasks, not newly verified findings of this pass.
5. If CRR is formalized, identify the political variables, channel assumptions, evaluation criteria, and failure cases before choosing numerical thresholds. A model that distinguishes coordination from conformity would be more informative than a diagram assigning a nation to a Zoa.
