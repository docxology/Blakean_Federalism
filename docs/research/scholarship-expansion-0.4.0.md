## 1. Treaty relations challenge the federation's starting point

**Directly read argument.** Henderson treats treaty federalism as constitutive of Canadian authority, not an additional minority concession. Printed pp.21–25 distinguish inherent powers, continuing treaty relationships, and UNDRIP; pp.26–34 develop exclusion, constitutional reconciliation, and overlapping/exclusive jurisdictions. His account retains territorial treaty governance, not merely portable cultural identity (pp.34–35). Institutional proposals include dedicated treaty offices, representation, and fiscal changes (pp.36–41). A consequential tension appears on p.38: direct treaty representation could lessen reliance on consent, consultation, and accommodation; p.40 nevertheless requires each Treaty Nation's legitimate consent to consolidation. These are Henderson's historical-constitutional interpretation and proposals, not this memo's findings about present Canadian law. The complete argument is now read, including previously omitted pp.22–34. [Henderson, 2019, official PDF](https://www.constitutionalstudies.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/02_Henderson-4.pdf), printed pp.17–41, PDF pp.1–25; printed p.42 is blank apart from running matter.

**Proposed manuscript-ready reconstruction, not attributed doctrine:**

A federation cannot establish its authority simply by first defining every collective as one of its constituents. Some relationships put the federation's own jurisdiction in question. In those cases, classification is already a disputed political act. A record that accurately describes a people but assumes the state's exclusive power to recognize it may reproduce the capture it was designed to expose. The relevant question is not only whether a recognized community receives adequate powers, but whether the recognizing institution has established its competence to allocate those powers at all. A people can participate in a negotiation without conceding that the negotiating forum originates its standing.

The distinction requires a change in the proposed grammar. National affiliation, residence, public jurisdiction, property claims, and treaty relationships must remain separately describable even where they concern the same people and land. Describing them separately does not make them practically separable: an institution cannot decompose a land relationship into a language service, a cultural membership, and a water entitlement, then assume that it has preserved everything material to the original claim. Composition without capture therefore needs a jurisdictional remainder: an explicitly recorded question whose resolution is not presumed by completing the rest of the institutional description.

Representation also cannot stand in for agreement to every decision. A delegate's presence, authorization to negotiate, approval of a particular agreement, and continuing power to reopen it are different matters. Conversely, no arrangement can promise that every binding common decision will secure every person's assent. The constitutional task is to specify what kind of agreement a decision requires, whose agreement counts for that purpose, and which continuing obligations survive disagreement. This specification must itself remain contestable.

**What changes:** replace the brief Henderson notice in §6 with a bounded account of foundational jurisdiction; add to §7.1 a question about the watershed body's title to decide. Henderson's p.38 prevents citing him as an uncomplicated endorsement of an invariant consent requirement. His p.40 prevents reducing his program to representation alone. The proposed jurisdictional remainder is ours; it is not Henderson's terminology or a jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion.

## 2. Refusal concerns the production of knowledge as well as membership

**Directly read argument.** Simpson's article begins with anthropology's involvement in governing and possessing difference (pp.67–70), then develops sovereignty as a methodological issue (pp.71–72). Her Kahnawake discussion connects membership disputes, gendered colonial recognition, and contested local responses (pp.72–75). The interview on pp.76–77 distinguishes lived citizenship from formal membership while withholding aspects of a personal predicament. The conclusion makes limits on representation integral to ethnographic practice, including stopping recording (p.78). This is situated analysis, not a blanket endorsement of every boundary maintained by a community. The article itself presents exclusion and disagreement within the community. No claim about present membership rules or the current Indian Act follows here. [Simpson, 2007, publisher PDF](https://junctures.org/index.php/junctures/article/download/66/60/108), printed pp.67–80 = PDF pp.1–14; main argument ends p.78, acknowledgements/notes occupy pp.79–80.

**Proposed manuscript-ready reconstruction:**

A passage guarantee should not require everyone to become fully legible to the federation. One may challenge a classification without supplying an alternative exhaustive identity, and one may have reasons to decline the questions through which recognition is offered. The present proposal therefore distinguishes access to public remedies from a duty to narrate one's entire cultural, familial, or spiritual life. Where a decision has coercive consequences, the deciding body owes an explanation of its authority and reasons. That duty does not symmetrically entitle the body to every form of knowledge held by the people affected.

This distinction qualifies the translation obligation. Translation should make an institution's exercise of power answerable; it should not promise the complete conversion of another people's practices into the federation's preferred ontology. A disagreement may concern whether the federation has asked a permissible question, rather than which answer should fill an available field. A refusal must therefore remain distinguishable from missing data, administrative failure, or tacit assent. An explicitly limited representation can sometimes be more faithful than a purportedly complete one.

There is a difficult converse. Protecting information cannot become a general license for officers to conceal decisions from people whose rights they affect. A resident contesting exclusion may need records that a governing body would prefer not to release. The proposal must distinguish protection against extractive disclosure from insulation against accountability. It cannot resolve this conflict by treating either public openness or collective secrecy as absolute. A defensible arrangement would specify the decision, relevant evidence, persons entitled to challenge it, protections for sensitive information, and a forum whose authority is itself justified. This is a new institutional question prompted by the reading, not a procedure supplied by the ethnography.

**What changes:** revise §4's translation obligation and §5's record-completeness language. Add a genuine refusal state to the proposed research design, without making software certify the validity of a refusal. Preserve the interview's tension between local sovereignty and exclusion; do not use Simpson solely as an inspirational defense of optional affiliation. Her 2014 book remains unread.

## 3. Gender tests the universality of the imaginative grammar

**Directly read argument.** Mellor argues that metaphoric and visual reciprocity can coexist with female subordination. Her opening challenges claims that Blakean brotherhood and androgyny already secure sexual equality (p.148). The division of creative labor and the priority assigned to masculine form occupy pp.149–151; her treatment of *Jerusalem* plate 99 asks whether the represented transformation is asymmetrical (pp.151–152). She explicitly distinguishes harmonious mutual dependence from equal standing (p.153). The closing biographical inference concerning Catherine is an argument based on reported materials, not independently verified biography in this pass (pp.154–155). Reading Mellor does not establish her interpretation as settled, nor license reproducing all her psychological or iconographical assumptions. [Mellor, Winter 1982/1983, official journal facsimile](https://bq.blakearchive.org/pdfs/16.3.mellor.pdf), printed pp.148–155 = PDF pp.2–9; PDF p.1 is a modern cover.

**Proposed manuscript-ready reconstruction:**

The distinction between a person and a state is politically promising only if the supposedly universal person is not already modeled on a privileged participant. A grammar can announce that everyone exceeds classification while quietly reserving authorship, interpretation, and decision for some, and assigning mediation, care, or embodiment to others. Mutual dependence does not answer this objection. An authority may depend on those whose work sustains it while denying them an equal part in determining its purposes. The question is therefore who can initiate a claim, refuse an assigned function, interpret the shared vocabulary, and alter the distribution of authority.

This objection reaches the proposed use of emanation. Treating a nation's creative emergence as emanation risks borrowing a relation whose gendered structure is among the very matters in dispute. Replacing its vocabulary with neutral labels would not establish equality if the new model preserved the same asymmetry between a creative center and those who realize its purposes. The current formal separation of affiliation, interpretation, and authorization should be retained, but its normative justification must be independent of any presumption that poetic complementarity is emancipatory.

The same discipline applies to images. A diagram of equal circles cannot settle whether a textual vision grants equal agency. Neither does a picture of interpenetration demonstrate freedom to withhold participation. The manuscript should place interpretive alternatives beside the visual evidence, identifying what each explains and what it leaves unresolved. Its modern political commitment to equal standing remains a commitment for which the authors must give reasons. It is not vindicated by announcing that every participant is contained within a universal human form.

**What changes:** promote gender from a contextual caution in §3 to a test of the proposed universal subject. Retain Q11's declared omission and its *Jerusalem* 88:3–5,12–13 locator. Mellor's older E244 is not the current manuscript's E246; that difference is edition evidence, not permission to “correct” either source without collation. Plate 99 discussion remains attributed criticism; no new copy-specific Blake image verdict is claimed.

## 4. A rival reading should change the method, not merely the conclusion

**Directly read argument.** Ostriker organizes the essay around four partly inconsistent orientations: sexual liberation, gendered complementarity, erotic confinement, and female subordination (p.156). Her Oothoon reading emphasizes agency without successful deliverance (pp.157–158); the middle sections pursue differentiated relationships and their transformations (pp.158–161). The late-poem critique is especially severe on female agency and mediation (pp.162–163). Yet the conclusion refuses a simple chronological replacement of one Blake by another: inconsistent tendencies coexist in varying proportions (p.164). Note 16 identifies Erin as a partial exception while qualifying her development as a character (p.165). Thus this article complicates both an entirely emancipatory Blake and an entirely uniform patriarchal system. Its psychoanalytic and comparative references were read as Ostriker's arguments, not independently established correspondences. [Ostriker, Winter 1982/1983, official journal facsimile](https://bq.blakearchive.org/pdfs/16.3.ostriker.pdf), printed pp.156–165 = PDF pp.2–11.

**Proposed manuscript-ready reconstruction:**

The manuscript need not decide that Blake's entire corpus speaks with a single political voice before it can make a productive inheritance. It must, however, allow contradictions to limit what it inherits. When a passage supports deliverance from classification while another makes freedom appear as absorption into a privileged whole, the second passage cannot be dismissed as a mere failure to understand the first. It may expose a limit within the imaginative vocabulary itself. The contemporary construction can retain a resource for criticism while declining a relation that the poem presents as redemptive.

This changes how the states/nations distinction should be defended. The claim is not that the chosen distinctions resolve all contrary evidence once correctly decoded. It is that they enable certain questions about classification and collective life, questions that can also be turned against their own poetic setting. A nation that describes its particular history as a contribution to universal humanity still needs to explain what becomes of those who resist the proposed union. If their resistance can only appear as error, deficiency, or a stage to be overcome, the reconstruction has restored capture at the level of interpretation.

The rival explanation therefore deserves an actual opportunity to win. Perhaps independent political principles do all the institutional work, while Blake supplies a historically situated vocabulary that is as liable to obscure as illuminate. The article should acknowledge this possibility when reporting the existing descriptor-removal control. Unchanged programmed safeguards establish a limited implementation property. They leave open whether the poetic interpretation adds insight, motivates attention to neglected experience, or merely ornaments a normative position available elsewhere.

**What changes:** strengthen §12's defeat conditions. Do not present historical contextualization as a way to discharge gender objections. Do not turn Ostriker's four interpretive orientations into four political organs, four scientific states, or a hierarchy of civic competence. Her qualification concerning Erin also prevents a careless assertion that the article reports literally no active female exception.

## 5. Plural authority requires a remedy that can operate

**Directly read argument.** Shachar distinguishes internal feminist criticism of accommodation from approaches that reject public recognition of difference altogether (pp.51–59,78–86). Her joint-governance account specifies allocation by submatter, a prohibition on monopoly, and reversal points (pp.71–73). The proposed arbitration redesign adds independent legal advice and recorded choices (pp.75–76), but treats these as insufficient against loyalty pressures. Mandatory review before an award becomes enforceable shifts the initiative away from the vulnerable party (pp.76–77); note 89 includes secular as well as religious family arbitration. Footnote 90 proposes prior agreement on a review standard and potentially a joint oversight body. The argument remains a contextual institutional proposal, with explicit limits in its conclusion (pp.86–88), not evidence that adding jurisdictions invariably reduces domination. [Shachar, 2005, publisher PDF](https://lawjournal.mcgill.ca/wp-content/uploads/pdf/410530-1225240850_Shachar.pdf), printed pp.49–88 = PDF pp.1–40.

**Proposed manuscript-ready reconstruction:**

A right to complain does not establish that a remedy can be obtained without intolerable cost. Someone dependent on an association for housing, work, care, or family relationships may be unable to initiate the very proceeding that is supposed to protect them. The institutional comparison should therefore specify who must activate review. For some consequential decisions, an institution may have to demonstrate compliance before enforcement rather than wait for the least powerful participant to allege a violation. This is a possible design response, not a universal rule for every association or administrative act.

Review itself requires limits. The manuscript cannot solve a monopoly problem by giving an appellate body unrestricted power to define acceptable belief or culture. A reviewing institution needs a defined competence, a justified standard, access to relevant evidence, a workable timetable, and means to make its remedy effective. Its own members, funding, interpretive assumptions, and accountability are part of the inquiry. Where authority is disputed, the reviewer cannot simply assume the answer under the description of neutral protection.

A meaningful comparison would hold the dispute and material dependency constant while varying the arrangements: exclusive association control; an individual appeal requiring the dissenter to carry every cost; and a defined review condition with support and protection against retaliation. The last arrangement could improve access but could also generate delay, professional gatekeeping, or collusion between authorities. A fourth possibility is that an ordinary public provision, available regardless of affiliation, removes the dependency more effectively than another jurisdictional forum. The proposal should remain open to that result. More institutions and more affiliations supply no monotonic freedom measure.

**What changes:** §§5–7 should distinguish formal consent, informed choice, feasible refusal, initiation of review, and effective remedy. This article supplies direct institutional contact beyond the previously read abstract of the 2001 book; that book remains unread. Shachar's treatment of French civic republicanism must not be equated with Pettit's distinct non-domination argument.

## 6. Integration proposals and unresolved conflicts

The following are research-generated proposals for the root's consideration. They are not findings attributed collectively to the five scholars.

First, state the constitutional remainder alongside the existing three mechanisms. Non-exhaustion limits claims about identity; supported passage concerns affiliation; translation makes exercises of power answerable; a remainder identifies unresolved authority to classify, adjudicate, or incorporate. The fourth item is not numerically privileged. If it is better expressed as a limit on the other three, use that arrangement. It must not become a compulsory slot that converts every refusal into the federation's property.

Second, distinguish refusal of disclosure, refusal of membership, refusal of a particular decision, and refusal of the forum's jurisdiction. These are different actions with different consequences. A resident's duty to contribute to shared sanitation cannot automatically be escaped by rejecting a cultural label. Conversely, an institution cannot infer its jurisdiction over a people merely because their members use a public service. The paper needs reasons for each transition; a relational graph can expose the transition but cannot provide those reasons.

Third, acknowledge a conflict that no slogan resolves. Protective review may be demanded by an excluded person while rejected as incorporation by a collective authority. Assuming that either claim always defeats the other would pre-decide the inquiry. The article should specify competing warrants, practical exposure, and the possibility of negotiated or jointly constituted forums without declaring those forums inherently legitimate. Shachar's religious-arbitration setting and Indigenous treaty relationships cannot be collapsed into a single class of voluntary associations.

Fourth, make dependence visible in the hypothetical drought case. Who owns storage? Who can wait through an appeal? Who keeps water flowing while competence is disputed? Which proposed interim action protects life without being treated as a permanent settlement of title? These are stipulated questions, not current California observations. They strengthen the case program precisely because there may be no arrangement that simultaneously maximizes speed, equality of influence, jurisdictional autonomy, and material security.

Fifth, preserve a public-reasoning threshold without compulsory metaphysical inclusion. Officials should explain coercive acts in terms that opponents can contest without accepting a Blakean account of Imagination. That does not entail demanding that every community translate its theology or knowledge into an exhaustive common language. The institutional duty is to justify the act and the asserted competence. Agreement about the ultimate nature of persons is neither an input field nor a condition of standing.

## 7. Visualization and formalism recommendations for the root

One useful figure would follow a single invented decision through affiliation, asserted competence, proposed consequence, objection, review, and remedy. Its purpose would be to expose a missing warrant, not show an inevitable progression toward legitimate authority. Place a branch before review for a challenge to the forum itself. A caption should state explicitly that completion of the diagram establishes neither jurisdiction nor consent.

A second figure could compare three arrangements under the same material dependency: no appeal; costly party-initiated appeal; and review required before enforcement. Use descriptive rows for trigger, decision-maker, standard, cost, interim protection, and enforceability. Do not color one column universally green. A mandatory procedure can protect a person or merely delay relief. Include a row identifying who can challenge the reviewer, and a final unresolved question concerning the governing body's authority.

A small text-image critical panel could pair the existing Q11 context with two clearly attributed interpretive questions: does interdependence redistribute agency, and what status does refusal receive? Adding *Jerusalem* plate 99 would require a new official copy-specific acquisition and rights record by the root. This memo's view of Mellor's reproduction does not clear reuse of the reproduced image. Do not generate a Blake-like image as evidence or imply that equal graphic placement proves textual equality.

No new formula is necessary. If the root introduces a formal authorization condition, its predicates should explicitly represent stipulated warrants and unresolved claims. A successful evaluation means consistency with chosen assumptions, not Blake's algebra, a legal judgment, or empirical validation. Template numbering and cross-references should be handled by the implementation owner; manual equation numbers in this memo would add no scholarship. The counterexample to preserve is an impeccably completed record whose forum lacks justified jurisdiction or whose remedy cannot be reached.
