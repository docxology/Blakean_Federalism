# Participation, contestation and national mediation

The 25 October revision connects five questions: how a recurrent state differs from an institution's continuing mandate; whether national mediation transforms possession; how someone can refuse a role while remaining a participant; who initiates effective review; and which institutions allocate representation, resources and remedies.

## Direct textual corrections

Pettit's 2005 working paper distinguishes reactive, representative and **regulative** contestation (printed pp. 9–14). Anticipation operates within his account but is not the name of the third category. The working paper remains separate from the later 2006 journal article.

For Jerusalem 27:85–88 the ASU transcription uses E173; Fallon's submitted manuscript p. 4 prints E174. The article identifies which locator it follows and preserves the older discrepancy record. The thirteen selected quotation records are unchanged.

## Two additions that change the comparison

[Makdisi's authorized introduction excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805215/86047/excerpt/9780521586047_excerpt.pdf), pp. 1–10, contrasts universalizing historical sequence with heterogeneous prophetic time, especially pp. 1–3. The article uses that argument against ranking historical nations as stages of one authorized future. The later Blake chapter and full book were not read.

The [Swiss Parliament's March 2024 manual](https://www.parlament.ch/centers/documents/en/20240301_BV-en.pdf) contains constitutional text consolidated to January 2024. Selected provisions supply a specific comparison of two chambers, political rights, financial equalization, procedural assistance and limited judicial remedies. Article 72(3)'s exclusion is discussed alongside its plural institutions. The English text is informational; it is not a current-law survey or an assessment of outcomes.
