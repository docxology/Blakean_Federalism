## Wright: a sharper objection within an unchanged ceiling

**Directly warranted premise.** The publisher presents Wright's subject as the relationship between individual imagination and national community, organized through national histories and expected development. Its limited description supports asking what kind of community is formed; it does not support reading Blake as simply preferring isolated individuals. This premise is narrower than the detailed allegation that the late *Jerusalem* assimilates difference. The latter remains reported through Fallon in the project's existing bibliography. No new claim that Wright pp.137 or 155 were read should enter the article. [Publisher description](https://www.ohioswallow.com/9780821415191/blake-nationalism-and-the-politics-of-alienation/).

**Extension: assimilation can operate through the conditions of recognition.** The strongest objection to the proposed federation is not merely that national forms might become rigid. A federation could permit many affiliations while monopolizing the account of what a legitimate affiliation is. It could recognize a community only when its practices are translated into enduring poetic possibilities, or treat a refusal of that description as evidence of incomplete emancipation. Multiplicity at the level of memberships would coexist with compulsory convergence at the level of justification. This is a criticism of the contemporary construction, not a conclusion newly established from Wright's unread chapter.

That distinction changes the burden of response. A non-exhaustion rule tells an organization that it cannot express the entirety of a person. It does not yet tell the federation that its own metaphysical vocabulary cannot exhaust the public reasons of a people. Likewise, material exit can protect an individual without answering whether the collective must accept its assigned place in the federation's story. The relevant question is whether the recognized people can challenge the terms of recognition, including the interpreter's claimed competence, while retaining protection and a hearing. Administrative translation may be necessary; mandatory conversion into the federation's account of humanity needs a separate defense.

The objection also applies to the article's narrative of improvement. A theory can avoid biological descent and still divide communities into those that have achieved imaginative reciprocity and those awaiting correction. If the latter judgment allocates credibility or authority, a temporal sequence of national generation becomes a hierarchy. The manuscript already rejects vision-based civic ranking in §7. It should extend that restraint to subtler narrative rankings: an account of historical creativity cannot itself warrant deciding whose refusal is immature. This recommendation follows from the project's own stated premises, with Wright's publisher description supplying a research question rather than a certified answer.

## Pettit: effective control rather than a list of opportunities

**Texts actually read.** Philip Pettit, *Two-dimensional Democracy, National and International*, IILJ Working Paper 2005/8, History and Theory of International Law Series, New York University School of Law, 2005. The retained institutional PDF has 23 pages: two covers followed by printed pp.1–21. I read the complete main argument and accompanying notes continuously. Its first text page says *The Monist*, forthcoming. I cite the working-paper pagination, not the pagination or title of a later journal version. Its references to the 1997 and 2000 works do not transfer reading credit to them. [Institutional full text](https://www.iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Petit-Two-Dimensional-Democracy-National-and-International-2005.pdf).

Philip Pettit, *On the People's Terms: A Republican Theory and Model of Democracy* (Cambridge University Press, 2012), ISBN 9781107005112. The publisher excerpt contains printed pp.1–10, exactly PDF pages 1–10: the start, not the entirety, of the introduction. All ten pages were read continuously. Separately retrieved frontmatter identifies first publication in 2012 and a third printing in 2014; it does not establish which physical printing supplied every excerpt page. Chapters 1–5 remain unread. [Publisher excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/05112/excerpt/9781107005112_excerpt.pdf); [edition information](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/05112/frontmatter/9781107005112_frontmatter.pdf).

**Source: quiet dependency.** In the 2012 excerpt, pp.1–2 use asymmetric relationships in work, family, education, credit and minority standing to explain vulnerability to another's will. Page 7 makes the decisive modal point: goodwill does not remove the power to intervene. Pages 3–6 distinguish protection against private domination from protection against domination by the protective state itself. Page 10 explains why public interference need not be dominating when it is subjected to the appropriate controls. This supplies direct textual support for the article's quiet-domination example while blocking the converse equation of every binding duty with capture. It does not establish any actual association's conduct. [Pettit 2012, pp.1–10](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/05112/excerpt/9781107005112_excerpt.pdf).

**Source: different forms of control.** The 2005 paper distinguishes the people collectively from persons in their plurality, and active decision-making from control retained through a capacity to correct decisions, pp.1–4. It argues that collective electoral authorization leaves room for domination by majorities and insiders. Pages 5–8 explain why decisions need an intelligible, revisable structure of reasons; individually consistent votes can produce an inconsistent package. This is his argument for representative arrangements, including a toy example, not an impossibility proof independently verified here. Nor should the article infer that its own preferred overlapping bodies thereby escape aggregation difficulties. [Pettit 2005, pp.1–8](https://www.iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Petit-Two-Dimensional-Democracy-National-and-International-2005.pdf).

Pages 8–14 distinguish reactive challenges, contestation through representatives, and institutional constraints or arrangements that anticipate challenges. Publicly available reasons and transparency are preconditions, not optional decorations. Appeals presuppose independence and impartiality; representatives can become sectional lobbies; some restraints work before a harmful decision occurs. Pettit discusses consultation and delegated functions but leaves questions about their extent open. Thus his paper supplies a differentiated account of power, not a warrant for selecting one exact board, court or delegation scheme. [Pettit 2005, pp.8–14](https://www.iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Petit-Two-Dimensional-Democracy-National-and-International-2005.pdf).

**Extension: voice, exit and revision are distinct burdens.** Voice concerns making a challenge audible and consequential. Exit concerns leaving a particular relationship without losing indispensable means of living or basic standing. Revision concerns changing the rules and distribution of powers that produce decisions. None substitutes automatically for another. The 2005 paper's p.15 discusses formally retained departure from international arrangements becoming infeasible because of accumulated costs. That is a useful analogy, not evidence about personal exit from cultural nations. Pages 16–21 investigate international contestability through states, movements and dispersed institutions, while conceding shortcomings. Their historically situated examples were read as Pettit's arguments, not independently updated legal findings. [Pettit 2005, pp.15–21](https://www.iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Petit-Two-Dimensional-Democracy-National-and-International-2005.pdf).

For the hypothetical watershed conflict, these distinctions direct inquiry to who can challenge an allocation, survive delay, obtain the relevant information and secure a change if the challenge succeeds. A completed forum field cannot answer those questions. Conversely, perpetual reopening without a way to decide shared obligations can undermine essential provision and leave the most dependent people exposed. Effective contestability needs an account of decisions and remedies, including what happens during review; it is not a promise that every person obtains the result they prefer.

## Five serious objections the next draft must answer

1. **Recognition may require assimilation.** The federation can preserve many national names while demanding one account of their meaning. Who can reject the poetic-state description without forfeiting public standing? If only the federation decides whether refusal is authentic, its anti-capture vocabulary protects its own monopoly. The response must explain contesting the recognition framework itself. This is an extension motivated by the limited Wright material, not a paraphrase of an unread chapter.

2. **The common-reasons requirement can exclude the dissenter.** Pettit's 2005 pp.8–9 assume considerations treated as relevant across public discussion. What happens when a dominant public rejects a minority's evidence or a people's account of jurisdiction as inadmissible? Better translation cannot cure a forum that precludes the issue. The article needs a way to examine exclusions from the accepted stock of reasons, without pretending that any asserted reason must determine the outcome. [Pettit, pp.8–9](https://www.iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Petit-Two-Dimensional-Democracy-National-and-International-2005.pdf).

3. **Quiet is observationally ambiguous.** Low complaint and sanction counts could reflect trustworthy restraint, effective anticipated challenge, dependency or retaliation fears. Pettit's accounts of latent power run in both directions: officials may anticipate citizens' corrections, or residents may anticipate officials' punishment. A future inquiry needs evidence about these capacities and dependencies; agreement rates cannot distinguish them. The prototype should remain a description of stipulated safeguards until practice is independently examined. [Pettit 2012, p.7](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/05112/excerpt/9781107005112_excerpt.pdf); [Pettit 2005, pp.2–3, 9–10](https://www.iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Petit-Two-Dimensional-Democracy-National-and-International-2005.pdf).

4. **Protectors and representatives can capture contestation.** A national representative, appeals officer or translation service can become another dependency. Public power may correct private subordination while introducing its own; additional forums may reward those able to finance repeated proceedings. The response must identify who can challenge the protector, how nonmembers gain access and how remedies reach the original decision. Dispersal alone supplies no guarantee. [Pettit 2012, pp.3–6](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/05112/excerpt/9781107005112_excerpt.pdf); [Pettit 2005, pp.11–13](https://www.iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Petit-Two-Dimensional-Democracy-National-and-International-2005.pdf).

5. **Revision and exit can externalize obligations.** An affluent association could invoke passage or change of affiliation to avoid shared burdens, while dependent residents cannot afford departure. A federation needs reasons for continuing duties and for revising their scope, alongside protections against identity-based coercion. Pettit's expensive-exit argument sharpens the practical question but does not resolve fiscal allocation, treaty authority or individual membership. Those require separate arguments and evidence. [Pettit 2005, p.15](https://www.iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Petit-Two-Dimensional-Democracy-National-and-International-2005.pdf).

## Candidate insertions

**For §3, after the assimilation discussion — extension:**

Plural national membership does not by itself prevent assimilation. A federation might retain many communities while requiring all of them to understand their place through its own final account of humanity. The proposed distinction between persons and states must therefore limit the federation's interpretive authority as well as that of its constituent organizations. People must be able to dispute the terms through which they are recognized, including the claim that their refusal expresses an inferior imaginative condition. This is a contemporary burden of justification. Wright's detailed argument remains incompletely accessed, and Clark's opening counterinterpretation establishes a dispute to pursue rather than a historical verdict to inherit.

**For §§5–6, replacing the abstract-only Pettit comparison — extension:**

Pettit's directly available writings make non-capture more demanding than the presence of an appeal mechanism. The relevant protection concerns power: whether people can make a challenge affect an authority's conduct, and whether that capacity persists when they have no immediate complaint. An association can dominate quietly through control of work, housing or access; a public body can impose an unwanted obligation without dominating if its authority is adequately controlled. Voice, practical exit and revision consequently require separate investigation. Their inclusion in a descriptive record identifies questions to answer; it does not demonstrate that an institution answers them.
