# Conceptual lexicon and warrant types

Adapted from the supplied scope. T means a textual anchor, I an interpretation, N a new construct. A label is not an inference rule authorizing a transition to political power. The executable schema is a project proposal rather than a model already present in the cited papers.

Labels in this section: **T** = anchored in Blakean text; **I** = interpretive reconstruction; **N** = new construct proposed for this research. These labels can coexist, but they should not be silently exchanged.

## Person individual identity T I

Milton's distinction between individuals and states is central. It supports the claim that a person is not identical with a passing condition, even a condition given a powerful moral or mythic name. However, translating Blake's eternal individuals into contemporary legal persons requires argument. Modern rights apply to embodied people under institutions, including those who reject the metaphysical story. The political presumption should be that no classification removes a person's standing as a rights-bearing participant.

## Poetic state T

Not a sovereign government. Blake's states can be named conditions, formations of imagination and relation, or combinations of individuals. Their permanence, alterability, and relations to imagination vary across passages. A state may be something from which persons need release, not a community entitled to constitutional representation. This is the most important barrier against a naive translation of the vocabulary.

## Eternal state T I

A state can be said to endure while people enter and leave it. That does not automatically mean an abstract universal existing independently of particulars. Nor does “eternal” necessarily mean a timeless bureaucratic category. The inquiry should distinguish recurrent possibility, visionary permanence, eschatological organization, and metaphysical essence. These alternatives make different demands on the political model.

## Nation T in Blake N in this model

Blake uses national and imperial figures in historically charged prophetic works. The new model uses nation for a temporally generated, self-interpreting collective formation with practices, narratives, institutions, and contested membership. That definition is intentionally not “a homogeneous descent group.” Nations may exist across jurisdictions; jurisdictions may contain several nations; persons may participate in more than one or refuse the label.

The natal etymology is a productive poetic hinge, not an entailment. “Birth” might refer to biological birth, historical emergence, initiation into a language, collective political founding, artistic generation, or the birth of a new public. Choosing among these is normative work. No etymology proves that ancestry should determine citizenship, that migration is contamination, or that nations are genetically bounded. A generation is also internally heterogeneous and overlaps with other generations.

## Emanation T N when applied to nation formation

Blake's emanations are not merely detachable output objects. Their gendered, relational, theological, and narrative dimensions must be studied before the term becomes a general production function. The phrase “nations emanate from states” can remain an exploratory metaphor, but the formal model should initially use the less loaded “generated through combinations of relations and practices.” Otherwise the analogy imports an unexamined hierarchy between originating state and derivative nation.

## Jurisdiction constituent unit N

A legally constituted institution with specified competencies, territory where applicable, revenues, coercive powers, and accountability. This term should do the institutional work that ordinary political discourse assigns to “state.” Keeping “jurisdiction” separate from “poetic state” prevents a metaphor from disguising who can police, tax, exclude, or punish whom.

## Federation N in dialogue with political theory

An arrangement of shared rule and self-rule in which authority is distributed and contested across levels and constituent units. In this project it should be a federation of accountable jurisdictions and associated publics, capable of accommodating multiple national formations. It cannot literally allocate parliamentary seats to metaphysical conditions unless there is an independently defended representation rule. A federation “containing states” can thus be an orienting image, while actual constitutional subjects remain persons and authorized institutions.

## Post civic nationalism N

A working label for belonging beyond the civic/ethnic binary, not a claim that equal citizenship is obsolete. A possible clearer subtitle is “plural national belonging under common guarantees of personhood.” Research must ask whether retaining nationalism adds explanatory or emancipatory value beyond democratic pluralism, multinational federalism, or nonterritorial autonomy. The label earns its place only if it identifies a problem those alternatives obscure.

## Contraries negation T I

Contraries can sustain productive differentiation; negation can erase the conditions of relationship. Institutionally this suggests disagreement without civil exclusion. It does not establish that every conflict is beneficial, that abuse should be tolerated as a productive contrary, or that destructive political movements have a right to dominate. The boundary must be defended in terms of harm, rights, and democratic contestability, not decided by poetic labeling.

## Eternity and time T I

The attraction of “Eternity is in love with the productions of time” is that temporal creation need not be a fallen copy to be dismissed. In the new theory, this supports respect for contingent human worlds without declaring any one of them final. The political formulation is a defeasible analogy: institutions should protect ongoing collective creativity while remaining answerable for concrete harms.
