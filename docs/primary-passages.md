# Primary passage program

The following locator and reading program is inherited from the supplied scope. This session independently checked selected central/counter passages in Milton, Jerusalem and America; it has not collated every copy or newly completed every listed reading. See the structured ledger for per-entry status. Preserve E-page, plate/line, dramatic speaker and edition uncertainty separately.

Textual source: university-hosted electronic *The Complete Poetry and Prose of William Blake*, edited by David V. Erdman: <https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/home.html>. Work, plate, and line should take precedence over an isolated E-page. E-pages below follow the displayed electronic edition; older print editions can differ. This is a selected passage verification, not a complete copy-specific collation of the illuminated books.

## Milton

Source: <https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/milton.html>.

• **32:10–18, E131–132**, also cited 32\[35\], added plate e: states as combinations of individuals; free brotherhood contrasted with tyrannical aggregation. The most direct associational anchor, spoken within the poem rather than a constitutional treatise.

• **32:22–24, E132:** distinguish states from individuals; states change while individual identities do not cease. The project must not make persons merely temporal while states alone endure.

• **32:25–29, E132:** Satan, Adam, and Milton can be called states. The category is not one simple inventory of national essences.

• **32:30–38, E132:** imagination is not a state; memory and reason are states; forms and created things are differentiated. Central evidence against treating every state as the same kind of immutable abstraction.

• **30:1–14, E129:** Beulah permits contrarieties to be equally true but is not the whole of eternity. Juxtaposition is not automatically the final political ideal.

• **34:8–18, E134:** a different fourfold arrangement, Beulah/Alla/Al-Ulro/Or-Ulro, warns against one universal fourfold lookup table.

• **40:32–37, E142:** redemption of contraries and rejection of negation. Contemporary translation must target harmful relations, not identify populations as negations.

## Jerusalem

Source: <https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html>.

• **4:23–35, E146–147:** possessive Albion and exclusionary territorial assertion appear critically, not as an uncomplicated recommendation.

• **7:63–67, E150:** generation can become protective and regenerative; it is not simply a synonym for fallen matter or ethnic reproduction.

• **10:8–16, E152–153:** abstraction can consume the substance from which it was derived. A warning against political categories that swallow persons.

• **15:17–20, E159:** externally cogged tyrannical wheels contrasted with freely revolving coordinated wheels. A design image, not an institutional proof.

• **17:33–39, E162:** contraries and negations differ. Pluralism is not indiscriminate validation of every claim.

• **27:77–88, E173 in the electronic display:** family pride and destruction contrasted with reciprocal movement among lands and mutual construction of Jerusalem. Fallon cites the final stanza at E174; retain plate/line and resolve the edition before final publication.

• **31:13–16, E177–178:** a harmful state can remain while Albion rises from it. Eternal does not mean desirable or politically legitimate.

• **38:19–23, E185; 55:60–64, E205; 91:29–30, E251:** particularity, definite identity, and general forms living in particulars are anti-abstraction tests.

• **43:6–8, E191:** election of Albion and gift of nations are an imperial-sounding countertext that an honest pluralist reading must confront.

• **49:65–75, E199:** iniquity belongs to a state rather than human existence; deliverance and forgiveness depend on the distinction. Accountability and protection are not thereby abolished.

• **52, E200:** spiritual states are eternal; persons must be distinguished from their present state. Rahab's eternity does not make it a legitimate order.

• **54:1–5, E203:** particular forms emanate in Great Eternity. This directly blocks an automatic equation of emanation with temporal national birth.

• **71:6–19, E225:** eternal states and humanized landscapes occur within a complex imaginative spatiality, not a simple container diagram.

• **73:44–45, E229:** persons pass through states as pilgrims through countries; the analogy does not turn states into legal territories.

• **88:3–11, E246:** emanations mediate encounter and mutual interchange. The surrounding gender and dominion tensions must remain visible.

• **92:1–6, E252:** Briton, Saxon, Roman, and Norman amalgamate into an English nation. Composition is supported, but assimilation is a genuine concern.

• **98:35–40, E258; 98:54–56 and 99:1–5, E258–259:** variable perception/time/space and nations in the closing vision complicate both simple nationalism and the disappearance of all difference.

## Other central anchors

• **The Marriage of Heaven and Hell, plate 7, E36:** “Eternity is in love with the productions of time.” It is a proverb in a provocative dialogic work, not a theorem that every historical product is good. **Plate 3, E34:** contraries and progression. Source: <https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/marriage_of_heaven_and_hell.html>.

• **A Vision of the Last Judgment, notebook p69, E555:** imagination/eternity and temporal generation/vegetation; **notebook pp79–80, E556:** both identities and states endure. Source: <https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/descriptions_of_last_judgment.html>.

• **Letter to Thomas Butts, 22 November 1802, verse lines 83–88, E722:** fourfold, threefold, twofold, and single vision in their poetic sequence. Source: <https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/Letters/23.htm>.

• **America a Prophecy, 8:13–17, E54:** Orc's speech, life, and revolutionary transformation. Read alongside the complete poem, whose Angels, Governors, Orc, and Albion's Angel must not be conflated. Source: <https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/america_a_prophecy.html>.
